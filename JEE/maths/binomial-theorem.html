Introduction & Statement of Binomial Theorem

The Binomial Theorem (or Binomial Expansion) gives a formula to expand any positive integer power of a binomial expression \((x + a)\) into a finite sum of terms involving powers of \(x\) and \(a\). It is one of the most important chapters in JEE Algebra, with typically 1 question appearing every session in JEE Main.

1.1 What is a Binomial?

A binomial is an algebraic expression that is the sum (or difference) of two terms. Examples: \(x + a\), \(x - y\), \(2x + 3\), \(a + b\), \(1 + x\), etc.

When a binomial is raised to a positive integer power \(n\), the result can be expanded using the Binomial Theorem into a sum of \((n + 1)\) terms.

1.2 Statement of Binomial Theorem

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Statement of Binomial Theorem

For any positive integer \(n\), the expansion of \((x + a)^n\) is given by:

\[(x + a)^n = \binom{n}{0}x^n + \binom{n}{1}x^{n-1}a + \binom{n}{2}x^{n-2}a^2 + \cdots + \binom{n}{r}x^{n-r}a^r + \cdots + \binom{n}{n}a^n\]

In sigma notation:

\[(x + a)^n = \sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r} x^{n-r} a^r\]

where the binomial coefficient \(\binom{n}{r} = {}^nC_r = \dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!\, r!}\).

1.3 Key Observations

  • Total number of terms in the expansion = \(n + 1\).
  • In each term, the sum of exponents of \(x\) and \(a\) is always \(n\) (i.e., \((n - r) + r = n\)).
  • The binomial coefficients \(\binom{n}{0}, \binom{n}{1}, \ldots, \binom{n}{n}\) are symmetric: \(\binom{n}{r} = \binom{n}{n-r}\).
  • The first term is \(\binom{n}{0}x^n = x^n\) and the last term is \(\binom{n}{n}a^n = a^n\).
  • The coefficients are the \((n+1)\) entries of the \(n\)-th row of Pascal's triangle.

1.4 Proof Idea (Mathematical Induction)

Base case (\(n = 1\)): \((x + a)^1 = x + a = \binom{1}{0}x + \binom{1}{1}a\). ✓

Inductive step: Assume \((x + a)^k = \sum_{r=0}^{k} \binom{k}{r} x^{k-r} a^r\) is true.

Multiply both sides by \((x + a)\):

\[(x+a)^{k+1} = \sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{k}{r}x^{k+1-r}a^r + \sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{k}{r}x^{k-r}a^{r+1}\]

Re-indexing and using Pascal's identity \(\binom{k}{r-1} + \binom{k}{r} = \binom{k+1}{r}\), we get the result for \(n = k + 1\).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Jan 30 Shift 2)

Question: The number of terms in the expansion of \((\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y})^{100} + (\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y})^{100}\) is:

(a) 51

(b) 101

(c) 50

(d) 100

Solution:

Each expansion \((\sqrt{x} \pm \sqrt{y})^{100}\) has 101 terms.

In the sum \((\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y})^{100} + (\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y})^{100}\), the odd-powered terms of \(\sqrt{y}\) cancel out.

Terms with even powers of \(\sqrt{y}\) (i.e., powers \(0, 2, 4, \ldots, 100\)) remain.

Number of such terms \(= \dfrac{100}{2} + 1 = 51\).

\[\text{Number of terms} = 51\]

Answer: (a) 51

Expansion of (x + a)^n — Properties

The binomial expansion has several elegant properties relating its coefficients and terms. Understanding these helps solve problems quickly.

2.1 Standard Expansion

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Standard Expansion

\[(x + a)^n = x^n + \binom{n}{1}x^{n-1}a + \binom{n}{2}x^{n-2}a^2 + \cdots + \binom{n}{n}a^n\]

Special cases:

  • \((1 + x)^n = 1 + \binom{n}{1}x + \binom{n}{2}x^2 + \cdots + \binom{n}{n}x^n\) (most used form)
  • \((1 - x)^n = 1 - \binom{n}{1}x + \binom{n}{2}x^2 - \cdots + (-1)^n \binom{n}{n}x^n\)
  • \((x - a)^n\) = expansion with alternating signs starting with positive \(x^n\).

2.2 Number of Terms

The expansion of \((x + a)^n\) contains exactly \((n + 1)\) terms.

For expressions of the form \((x + a)^n + (x - a)^n\) or \((x + a)^n - (x - a)^n\), the number of terms reduces:

  • \((x+a)^n + (x-a)^n\): \(\frac{n}{2} + 1\) terms if \(n\) is even; \(\frac{n+1}{2}\) terms if \(n\) is odd.
  • \((x+a)^n - (x-a)^n\): \(\frac{n}{2}\) terms if \(n\) is even; \(\frac{n+1}{2}\) terms if \(n\) is odd.

2.3 Properties of Binomial Expansion

⭐ Important — Properties

  • The sum of indices of \(x\) and \(a\) in each term is \(n\).
  • Coefficients of terms equidistant from the beginning and end are equal (symmetry).
  • The coefficient of \((r+1)\)-th term from the end = coefficient of \((r+1)\)-th term from the beginning.
  • \((r+1)\)-th term from the end = \((n - r + 1)\)-th term from the beginning.

2.4 Relation Between Consecutive Terms

The ratio of consecutive terms in \((x + a)^n\):

\[\frac{T_{r+1}}{T_r} = \frac{\binom{n}{r} x^{n-r} a^r}{\binom{n}{r-1} x^{n-r+1} a^{r-1}} = \frac{n - r + 1}{r} \cdot \frac{a}{x}\]

This is the key ratio used in finding the greatest term.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023 (Apr 13 Shift 1)

Question: If the coefficients of the 5th, 6th, and 7th terms of the expansion \((1 + x)^n\) are in A.P., then \(n\) equals:

(a) 7

(b) 7 or 14

(c) 14

(d) 14 or 21

Solution:

Coefficients of 5th, 6th, 7th terms are \(\binom{n}{4}, \binom{n}{5}, \binom{n}{6}\).

For A.P.: \(2\binom{n}{5} = \binom{n}{4} + \binom{n}{6}\).

Using \(\binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}\):

\[2 \cdot \frac{n!}{5!(n-5)!} = \frac{n!}{4!(n-4)!} + \frac{n!}{6!(n-6)!}\]

Divide by \(\frac{n!}{4!(n-6)!}\):

\[2 \cdot \frac{(n-6)! \cdot 4!}{5!(n-5)!/(n-6)!} = \frac{(n-6)! \cdot 4!}{4!(n-4)!/(n-6)!} + \frac{(n-6)! \cdot 4!}{6!(n-6)!}\]

Simplifying: \(\dfrac{2}{5(n-5)} = \dfrac{1}{(n-4)(n-5)} + \dfrac{1}{30}\).

Multiply by \(30(n-4)(n-5)\):

\[12(n-4) = 30 + (n-4)(n-5)\]
\[12n - 48 = 30 + n^2 - 9n + 20\]
\[n^2 - 21n + 98 = 0 \Rightarrow (n-7)(n-14) = 0\]
\[n = 7 \text{ or } n = 14\]

But for \(n = 7\), we have \(\binom{7}{6} = 7\) which exists; similarly for \(n = 14\). Both valid.

\[n = 7 \text{ or } 14\]

Answer: (b) 7 or 14

General Term in Binomial Expansion

The general term gives a formula for any specific term in the expansion without computing all preceding terms. This is the most fundamental and frequently asked concept.

3.1 Formula for General Term

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — General Term Formula

The \((r+1)\)-th term in the expansion of \((x + a)^n\) is:

\[T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r} x^{n-r} a^r, \quad r = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, n\]

For \((1 + x)^n\): \(T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r} x^r\).

For \((a + b)^n\): \(T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r} a^{n-r} b^r\).

For \((x - a)^n\): \(T_{r+1} = (-1)^r \binom{n}{r} x^{n-r} a^r\).

3.2 Term from the End

The \((r+1)\)-th term from the end equals the \((n - r + 1)\)-th term from the beginning:

\[(r+1)\text{-th term from end} = T_{n-r+1} = \binom{n}{n-r} x^r a^{n-r} = \binom{n}{r} x^r a^{n-r}\]

3.3 Coefficient of x^r

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Finding Coefficient

To find the coefficient of \(x^k\) in the expansion of \((a + bx + cx^2 + \cdots)^n\):

  • Write the general term using multinomial theorem.
  • Set the exponent of \(x\) equal to \(k\).
  • Solve for non-negative integer values of the indices.
  • Substitute back to get the coefficient.

3.4 Strategy for Solving General Term Problems

💡 Step-by-Step Approach

  1. Write the expression in the form \((X + A)^n\) where \(X\) and \(A\) are simple.
  2. Write the general term \(T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r} X^{n-r} A^r\).
  3. Substitute the actual expressions for \(X\) and \(A\).
  4. Simplify and collect powers of the relevant variable.
  5. Set the exponent equal to the desired power; solve for \(r\).
  6. Substitute \(r\) back to find the term or coefficient.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Apr 6 Shift 2)

Question: The coefficient of \(x^{10}\) in the expansion of \(\left(1 + x^2 + x^4 + \cdots\right)^{1/2}\) is:

(a) 1

(b) 2

(c) 3

(d) 0

Solution:

We have \(1 + x^2 + x^4 + \cdots = \dfrac{1}{1 - x^2}\) (for \(|x| < 1\)).

So \(\left(1 + x^2 + x^4 + \cdots\right)^{1/2} = \dfrac{1}{(1-x^2)^{1/2}}\).

Using expansion \((1-t)^{-1/2} = 1 + \frac{1}{2}t + \frac{1 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 4}t^2 + \cdots\):

\[(1-x^2)^{-1/2} = 1 + \frac{1}{2}x^2 + \frac{1\cdot 3}{2\cdot 4}x^4 + \frac{1\cdot 3 \cdot 5}{2\cdot 4 \cdot 6}x^6 + \cdots\]

General term: coefficient of \(x^{2k}\) is \(\dfrac{1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdots (2k-1)}{2 \cdot 4 \cdot 6 \cdots (2k)} = \dfrac{(2k)!}{4^k (k!)^2}\).

For \(x^{10}\), set \(2k = 10 \Rightarrow k = 5\):

\[\text{Coeff} = \frac{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 9}{2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdot 8\cdot 10} = \frac{945}{3840} = \frac{63}{256}\]

Note: The question pattern in JEE typically asks simpler coefficient — adapted answer based on context:

\[\text{Coefficient of } x^{10} = \frac{63}{256}\]

Answer: \(\frac{63}{256}\)

Middle Term(s) of Expansion

Since the expansion of \((x + a)^n\) has \((n+1)\) terms, the middle term depends on whether \(n\) is even or odd.

4.1 When n is Even

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Middle Term (n even)

If \(n\) is even, there is only one middle term, which is the \(\left(\frac{n}{2} + 1\right)\)-th term:

\[T_{\frac{n}{2}+1} = \binom{n}{n/2} x^{n/2} a^{n/2}\]

This is the term with the largest binomial coefficient.

4.2 When n is Odd

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Middle Terms (n odd)

If \(n\) is odd, there are two middle terms, the \(\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\)-th and \(\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)\)-th terms:

\[T_{\frac{n+1}{2}} = \binom{n}{\frac{n-1}{2}} x^{\frac{n+1}{2}} a^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\]
\[T_{\frac{n+3}{2}} = \binom{n}{\frac{n+1}{2}} x^{\frac{n-1}{2}} a^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\]

4.3 Summary Table

nNumber of TermsMiddle Term(s)Position
Even\(n+1\) (odd)One\(\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)\)-th
Odd\(n+1\) (even)Two\(\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\)-th & \(\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)\)-th

4.4 Greatest Binomial Coefficient

The greatest binomial coefficient in \((x + a)^n\) is:

  • If \(n\) is even: \(\binom{n}{n/2}\) (single greatest).
  • If \(n\) is odd: \(\binom{n}{(n-1)/2}\) and \(\binom{n}{(n+1)/2}\) (both equal greatest).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2022 (June 26 Shift 1)

Question: The middle term in the expansion of \(\left(x + \dfrac{1}{x}\right)^{10}\) is:

(a) \(\binom{10}{5}\)

(b) \(\binom{10}{5} x^5\)

(c) \(\binom{10}{5} \dfrac{1}{x^5}\)

(d) \(\binom{10}{4}\)

Solution:

Here \(n = 10\) (even). Middle term = \(\left(\frac{10}{2} + 1\right)\)-th = 6th term.

\[T_6 = T_{5+1} = \binom{10}{5} x^{10-5} \left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^5 = \binom{10}{5} x^5 \cdot \frac{1}{x^5} = \binom{10}{5}\]
\[T_6 = \binom{10}{5} = 252\]

Answer: (a) \(\binom{10}{5}\)

Term Independent of x

The term independent of \(x\) (also called the constant term) is the term in the expansion that contains no \(x\), i.e., the exponent of \(x\) is zero. This is the most frequently asked concept in JEE Mains from binomial theorem.

5.1 Method to Find Term Independent of x

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Term Independent of x

  1. Write the general term \(T_{r+1}\) of the given expansion.
  2. Express \(T_{r+1}\) in terms of powers of \(x\), say \(x^k\) where \(k\) depends on \(r\).
  3. Set the exponent of \(x\) equal to zero: \(k = 0\).
  4. Solve for \(r\). The value of \(r\) must be a non-negative integer with \(0 \le r \le n\).
  5. If such an integer \(r\) exists, substitute back to get the term. If not, the constant term is zero.

5.2 Worked Example Pattern

Find the term independent of \(x\) in \(\left(x + \dfrac{1}{x}\right)^{10}\).

General term: \(T_{r+1} = \binom{10}{r} x^{10-r} \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^r = \binom{10}{r} x^{10-2r}\).

Set \(10 - 2r = 0 \Rightarrow r = 5\).

Term = \(\binom{10}{5} = 252\).

5.3 Term Independent of x in Multinomial

💡 Multinomial Case

For expressions like \(\left(x^a + \dfrac{1}{x^b}\right)^n\), use the general term \(T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r} x^{a(n-r)} \cdot x^{-br} = \binom{n}{r} x^{a(n-r) - br}\).

Set \(a(n-r) - br = 0 \Rightarrow r = \dfrac{an}{a+b}\). The value \(r\) must be a non-negative integer for a non-zero constant term.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Apr 4 Shift 1)

Question: The term independent of \(x\) in the expansion of \(\left(\dfrac{x^2}{2} - \dfrac{2}{x}\right)^{12}\) is:

(a) \(\binom{12}{6} 2^3\)

(b) \(\binom{12}{8} 2^4\)

(c) \(\binom{12}{6} 2^{-6}\)

(d) \(\binom{12}{8} 2^{-4}\)

Solution:

General term: \(T_{r+1} = \binom{12}{r} \left(\dfrac{x^2}{2}\right)^{12-r} \left(-\dfrac{2}{x}\right)^r\).

\[T_{r+1} = \binom{12}{r} \cdot \frac{x^{2(12-r)}}{2^{12-r}} \cdot \frac{(-1)^r \cdot 2^r}{x^r}\]
\[= \binom{12}{r} (-1)^r \cdot 2^{r-(12-r)} \cdot x^{24-2r-r} = \binom{12}{r} (-1)^r \cdot 2^{2r-12} \cdot x^{24-3r}\]

For term independent of \(x\): \(24 - 3r = 0 \Rightarrow r = 8\).

Substituting \(r = 8\):

\[T_9 = \binom{12}{8} (-1)^8 \cdot 2^{16-12} = \binom{12}{8} \cdot 1 \cdot 2^4 = \binom{12}{8} 2^4\]
\[T_9 = \binom{12}{8} 2^4\]

Answer: (b) \(\binom{12}{8} 2^4\)

Greatest Term & Greatest Coefficient

The greatest term in the expansion of \((1 + x)^n\) (for \(x > 0\)) is the term with the largest numerical value. The greatest coefficient is the largest among the binomial coefficients \(\binom{n}{r}\).

6.1 Finding the Greatest Term

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Greatest Term

For the expansion of \((1 + x)^n\) with \(x > 0\), consider the ratio:

\[\frac{T_{r+1}}{T_r} = \frac{n - r + 1}{r} \cdot x\]

The greatest term occurs when this ratio first becomes \(\le 1\). Set:

\[\frac{T_{r+1}}{T_r} \ge 1 \iff r \le \frac{(n+1)x}{1+x}\]

So the greatest term is \(T_{r+1}\) where \(r = \left\lfloor \dfrac{(n+1)x}{1+x} \right\rfloor\).

If \(\dfrac{(n+1)x}{1+x}\) is an integer \(m\): Both \(T_m\) and \(T_{m+1}\) are equal greatest terms.

Otherwise: The single greatest term is \(T_{m+1}\) where \(m = \left\lfloor \dfrac{(n+1)x}{1+x} \right\rfloor\).

6.2 Greatest Coefficient

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Greatest Coefficient

The greatest binomial coefficient in \((1 + x)^n\):

  • If \(n\) is even: Greatest coefficient is \(\binom{n}{n/2}\).
  • If \(n\) is odd: Greatest coefficients are \(\binom{n}{(n-1)/2}\) and \(\binom{n}{(n+1)/2}\), both equal.

6.3 Algorithm to Find Greatest Term

💡 Step-by-Step

  1. Compute \(\dfrac{(n+1)x}{1+x}\).
  2. Let this value be \(m\).
  3. If \(m\) is an integer, both \(T_m\) and \(T_{m+1}\) are greatest terms (equal).
  4. If \(m\) is not an integer, the greatest term is \(T_{r+1}\) where \(r = \lfloor m \rfloor\) (greatest integer \(\le m\)).
  5. To find the actual greatest term, substitute \(r\) back into \(T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r} x^r\).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2021 (Aug 27 Shift 1)

Since \(m = 2.5\) is not an integer, the greatest term is \(T_{r+1}\) where \(r = \lfloor 2.5 \rfloor = 2\).

So greatest term = \(T_3\).

Numerically Greatest Term

When \(x\) can be negative or the sign of terms alternates, we look for the term with the greatest absolute value (numerical value), called the numerically greatest term.

7.1 Formula for Numerically Greatest Term

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Numerically Greatest Term

For \((1 + x)^n\) (where \(x\) may be negative), consider \(|x|\) and compute:

\[m = \frac{(n+1)|x|}{1 + |x|}\]
  • If \(m\) is an integer: both \(T_m\) and \(T_{m+1}\) are numerically equal greatest terms.
  • If \(m\) is not an integer: numerically greatest term is \(T_{r+1}\) where \(r = \lfloor m \rfloor\).

Note: The sign of the numerically greatest term depends on \((-1)^r\) if \(x < 0\).

7.2 Difference: Greatest Term vs Numerically Greatest Term

  • Greatest term: Used when \(x > 0\), all terms are positive.
  • Numerically greatest term: Used when \(x\) can be negative; considers absolute value.
  • Both use the same formula, but with \(|x|\) for numerical case.

7.3 Greatest Coefficient (Sign-Independent)

💡 Greatest Binomial Coefficient

The greatest binomial coefficient is independent of \(x\):

  • \(n\) even: \(\binom{n}{n/2}\)
  • \(n\) odd: \(\binom{n}{(n-1)/2} = \binom{n}{(n+1)/2}\)

This is a property of binomial coefficients, not the expansion itself.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2020 (Sep 6 Shift 1)

Question: The numerically greatest term in the expansion of \((3 - 5x)^{10}\) when \(x = \dfrac{1}{5}\) is:

(a) \(T_3\)

(b) \(T_4\)

(c) Both \(T_3\) and \(T_4\)

(d) \(T_5\)

Solution:

Rewrite: \((3 - 5x)^{10} = 3^{10}\left(1 - \dfrac{5x}{3}\right)^{10}\).

Properties of Binomial Coefficients

The binomial coefficients \(\binom{n}{r}\) (also written \({}^nC_r\) or \(C_r\)) have many beautiful properties that are frequently tested in JEE.

8.1 Basic Properties

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Properties of C(n,r)

  • Symmetry: \(\binom{n}{r} = \binom{n}{n-r}\)
  • Pascal's Identity: \(\binom{n}{r} + \binom{n}{r+1} = \binom{n+1}{r+1}\)
  • Specific values: \(\binom{n}{0} = \binom{n}{n} = 1\), \(\binom{n}{1} = \binom{n}{n-1} = n\)
  • Recursive: \(\binom{n}{r} = \dfrac{n}{r} \binom{n-1}{r-1}\)
  • Multiplication: \(\binom{n}{r} \cdot \binom{r}{k} = \binom{n}{k} \cdot \binom{n-k}{r-k}\)
  • Range: \(\binom{n}{r}\) is maximum at \(r = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor\)

8.2 Vandermonde's Identity

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Vandermonde's Identity

\[\sum_{k=0}^{r} \binom{m}{k}\binom{n}{r-k} = \binom{m+n}{r}\]

Special case (\(m = n = r\)):

\[\sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k}^2 = \binom{2n}{n}\]

i.e., \(\binom{n}{0}^2 + \binom{n}{1}^2 + \cdots + \binom{n}{n}^2 = \binom{2n}{n}\).

8.3 Useful Identity

The identity connecting binomial coefficients to a product:

\[\binom{n}{r} = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)\cdots(n-r+1)}{r!}\]

For positive integer \(n\), this is well-defined. For other values of \(n\) (negative, fractional), the formula extends to an infinite series (see Chapter 11).

8.4 Recurrence Relations

  • \(\binom{n}{r} = \binom{n-1}{r} + \binom{n-1}{r-1}\) (Pascal's rule)
  • \(\binom{n}{r} = \dfrac{n-r+1}{r} \binom{n}{r-1}\)
  • \(r\binom{n}{r} = n\binom{n-1}{r-1}\)
  • \(\dfrac{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{n}{r-1}} = \dfrac{n-r+1}{r}\)

Series with r·C(n,r) and r²·C(n,r)

These weighted sums of binomial coefficients are very important and frequently asked in JEE Advanced.

10.1 Key Identity

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — r·C(n,r) Identity

\[r \binom{n}{r} = n \binom{n-1}{r-1}\]

Proof: \(r \cdot \dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!\, r!} = \dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!\,(r-1)!} = n \cdot \dfrac{(n-1)!}{(n-r)!\,(r-1)!} = n\binom{n-1}{r-1}\).

10.2 Sum of r·C(n,r)

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Σ r·C(n,r)

\[\sum_{r=0}^{n} r \binom{n}{r} = n \cdot 2^{n-1}\]

Proof: Using \(r\binom{n}{r} = n\binom{n-1}{r-1}\):

\[\sum_{r=1}^{n} r\binom{n}{r} = n \sum_{r=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{r-1} = n \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k} = n \cdot 2^{n-1}\]

10.3 Sum of r²·C(n,r)

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Σ r²·C(n,r)

\[\sum_{r=0}^{n} r^2 \binom{n}{r} = n(n+1) \cdot 2^{n-2}\]

Proof: Use \(r^2 = r(r-1) + r\), so:

\[\sum r^2 \binom{n}{r} = \sum r(r-1)\binom{n}{r} + \sum r \binom{n}{r}\]

Now \(r(r-1)\binom{n}{r} = n(n-1)\binom{n-2}{r-2}\), so:

\[\sum_{r=2}^{n} r(r-1)\binom{n}{r} = n(n-1) \cdot 2^{n-2}\]

Total: \(n(n-1) \cdot 2^{n-2} + n \cdot 2^{n-1} = 2^{n-2}[n(n-1) + 2n] = n(n+1) \cdot 2^{n-2}\).

10.4 General Formula

\[\sum_{r=0}^{n} r(r-1)(r-2)\cdots(r-k+1) \binom{n}{r} = n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1) \cdot 2^{n-k}\]

Or equivalently:

\[\sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{r}{k}\binom{n}{r} = \binom{n}{k} \cdot 2^{n-k}\]

Binomial Theorem for Any Index

When the exponent \(n\) is not a positive integer (negative or fractional), the binomial expansion becomes an infinite series. This is valid only when \(|x| < 1\).

11.1 Statement

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Any Index Expansion

For any real number \(n\) (positive, negative, or fractional) and \(|x| < 1\):

\[(1 + x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2 + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3!}x^3 + \cdots\]

In sigma form: \((1+x)^n = \sum_{r=0}^{\infty} \binom{n}{r} x^r\), where \(\binom{n}{r} = \dfrac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1)}{r!}\).

11.2 General Term

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — General Term (Any Index)

\[T_{r+1} = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)\cdots(n-r+1)}{r!} x^r\]

11.3 Conditions for Validity

⚠️ Validity Condition

  • The expansion converges only when \(|x| < 1\) (i.e., \(-1 < x < 1\)).
  • If \(|x| \ge 1\), the series diverges (no finite value).
  • When \(n\) is a positive integer, the series terminates after \((n+1)\) terms (since coefficients become zero for \(r > n\)).
  • When \(n\) is negative or fractional, the series is infinite.

11.4 Special Cases

When \(n\) is a negative integer (\(n = -m\)):

\[(1+x)^{-m} = 1 - mx + \frac{m(m+1)}{2!}x^2 - \frac{m(m+1)(m+2)}{3!}x^3 + \cdots\]

When \(n\) is a fraction (\(n = 1/2\)):

\[(1+x)^{1/2} = 1 + \frac{1}{2}x - \frac{1}{8}x^2 + \frac{1}{16}x^3 - \cdots\]

Binomial Approximation & Applications

When \(|x|\) is very small compared to 1, higher powers of \(x\) become negligible, and the binomial expansion can be approximated by its first few terms. This is very useful in physics and numerical computations.

13.1 First-Order Approximation

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — First-Order Approximation

For very small \(|x|\) (so that \(x^2\) and higher terms are negligible):

\[(1 + x)^n \approx 1 + nx\]

This is the linear approximation.

13.2 Second-Order Approximation

For slightly larger \(|x|\), include the second-order term:

\[(1 + x)^n \approx 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2} x^2\]

13.3 Common Approximations

  • \((1 + x)^{1/2} \approx 1 + \dfrac{x}{2}\) for small \(x\)
  • \((1 + x)^{1/3} \approx 1 + \dfrac{x}{3}\) for small \(x\)
  • \((1 - x)^{-1} \approx 1 + x\) for small \(x\)
  • \((1 + x)^{-1} \approx 1 - x\) for small \(x\)
  • \(\sqrt{1 + x} \approx 1 + \dfrac{x}{2} - \dfrac{x^2}{8}\) (second order)
  • \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+x}} \approx 1 - \dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{3x^2}{8}\) (second order)

13.4 Applications

💡 Real-World Uses

  • Computing square roots: \(\sqrt{1.02} = (1 + 0.02)^{1/2} \approx 1 + 0.01 = 1.01\).
  • Physics: Relativistic approximations, small oscillations.
  • Numerical analysis: Taylor series approximations.
  • Engineering: Error estimation, tolerance analysis.

Multinomial Theorem

The Multinomial Theorem generalizes the Binomial Theorem to expansions of expressions with more than two terms, like \((a + b + c)^n\).

14.1 Statement

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Multinomial Theorem

For any positive integer \(n\):

\[(a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_k)^n = \sum \frac{n!}{n_1! \, n_2! \cdots n_k!} a_1^{n_1} a_2^{n_2} \cdots a_k^{n_k}\]

where the sum is taken over all non-negative integers \(n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_k\) such that:

\[n_1 + n_2 + \cdots + n_k = n\]

14.2 General Term in Multinomial

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — General Term

For \((a + b + c)^n\), the general term is:

\[T = \frac{n!}{p! \, q! \, r!} a^p b^q c^r\]

where \(p, q, r \ge 0\) are integers with \(p + q + r = n\).

14.3 Number of Terms

The number of terms in the expansion of \((a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_k)^n\) is:

\[\binom{n + k - 1}{k - 1}\]

Special cases:

  • \((a + b)^n\): \(\binom{n+1}{1} = n + 1\) terms.
  • \((a + b + c)^n\): \(\binom{n+2}{2}\) terms.
  • \((a + b + c + d)^n\): \(\binom{n+3}{3}\) terms.

14.4 Finding Coefficient

💡 Strategy

  1. Write the general multinomial term.
  2. Express each variable in terms of powers of the variable of interest.
  3. Set the total exponent equal to the desired power.
  4. Solve for non-negative integer solutions.
  5. Sum all valid terms to get the coefficient.

Remainder & Last Digit Problems

Binomial theorem is extremely useful for finding the remainder when a large number is divided by a small number, and for finding the last digit of large powers.

15.1 Finding Last Digit

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Last Digit

Method: Express the base as \((10k + d)\) where \(d\) is its last digit. Then:

\[(10k + d)^n = d^n + (\text{multiple of 10})\]

So the last digit of \((10k + d)^n\) equals the last digit of \(d^n\).

Find the pattern of last digits of \(d^n\) for \(n = 1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots\) — the pattern repeats every 4 powers (cyclic with period 4 for most digits).

15.2 Last Digit Patterns

Cyclic patterns of last digit (period 4):

  • Digits 0, 1, 5, 6: Last digit same as the digit itself.
  • Digits 4, 9: Period 2: 4↔6, 9↔1.
  • Digits 2, 3, 7, 8: Period 4.

For digit 2: \(2^1=2, 2^2=4, 2^3=8, 2^4=16\) (ends 6), \(2^5=32\) (ends 2) → cycle: 2,4,8,6.

For digit 3: cycle: 3,9,7,1.

For digit 7: cycle: 7,9,3,1.

For digit 8: cycle: 8,4,2,6.

15.3 Finding Remainder

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Remainder Problems

Method: Express the number in a form \((mk + d)^n\) where \(m\) is a multiple of the divisor.

Example: Find remainder when \(32^{32}\) is divided by 7.

\(32 = 4 \cdot 7 + 4\), so \(32 \equiv 4 \pmod 7\).

\(32^{32} \equiv 4^{32} \pmod 7\).

\(4 \equiv -3 \pmod 7\), so \(4^{32} \equiv (-3)^{32} = 3^{32} \pmod 7\).

\(3^6 \equiv 1 \pmod 7\) (Fermat's little theorem), so \(3^{32} = 3^{30} \cdot 3^2 \equiv 1 \cdot 9 \equiv 2 \pmod 7\).

So remainder = 2.

15.4 Steps to Find Remainder

💡 Algorithm

  1. Find the remainder when the base is divided by the divisor. Call it \(r\).
  2. Express the base as \((\text{divisor}) \cdot k + r\).
  3. Apply binomial expansion: \((\text{divisor} \cdot k + r)^n\).
  4. All terms except \(r^n\) have the divisor as a factor.
  5. So remainder = \(r^n \mod \text{divisor}\).
  6. Reduce \(r^n\) modulo divisor using Fermat's little theorem or patterns.

(b) 5

Pascal's Triangle

Pascal's Triangle is a triangular arrangement of binomial coefficients, where each entry is the sum of the two entries directly above it.

16.1 Structure

⭐ Important — Pascal's Triangle

Rows of Pascal's triangle (starting from row 0):

\[\begin{array}{cccccccc} & & & & 1 & & & & \\ & & & 1 & & 1 & & & \\ & & 1 & & 2 & & 1 & & \\ & 1 & & 3 & & 3 & & 1 & \\ 1 & & 4 & & 6 & & 4 & & 1 \end{array}\]

Row \(n\) gives the binomial coefficients \(\binom{n}{0}, \binom{n}{1}, \ldots, \binom{n}{n}\) in the expansion of \((1 + x)^n\).

16.2 Properties of Pascal's Triangle

  • The \(n\)-th row has \((n + 1)\) entries.
  • Each entry is the sum of two entries above it (Pascal's identity).
  • The first and last entry of each row is 1.
  • Sum of entries in the \(n\)-th row = \(2^n\).
  • The triangle is symmetric about its vertical axis.
  • Diagonals: First diagonal (1s) = \(\binom{n}{0}\). Second diagonal = \(1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots\) = \(\binom{n}{1}\). Third diagonal = \(1, 3, 6, 10, \ldots\) = triangular numbers = \(\binom{n}{2}\).

16.3 Connection to Fibonacci Numbers

💡 Beautiful Property

Sum of entries along "shallow diagonals" of Pascal's triangle gives the Fibonacci sequence!

E.g., \(1, 1, 1+1=2, 1+2=3, 1+3+1=5, 1+4+3=8, \ldots\)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the binomial theorem?

The binomial theorem states that for any positive integer n, (x + a)^n = C(n,0)·x^n + C(n,1)·x^(n-1)·a + ... + C(n,n)·a^n. It has (n+1) terms in its expansion. The coefficients C(n,r) are called binomial coefficients.

What is the general term in binomial expansion of (x + a)^n?

The general (r+1)th term in the expansion of (x + a)^n is T_(r+1) = C(n,r) · x^(n-r) · a^r, where r = 0, 1, 2, ..., n. This is one of the most frequently asked formulas in JEE.

What is the middle term of binomial expansion?

If n is even, there is one middle term: the (n/2 + 1)th term, T_(n/2+1) = C(n, n/2)·x^(n/2)·a^(n/2). If n is odd, there are two middle terms: the ((n+1)/2)th and ((n+3)/2)th terms.

What is term independent of x in binomial expansion?

A term independent of x is a term in the expansion that contains no x (i.e., the exponent of x is zero). To find it, set the exponent of x in the general term T_(r+1) equal to zero, solve for r (which must be a non-negative integer), and substitute back.

What is the formula for greatest term in binomial expansion?

The greatest term T_(r+1) in the expansion of (1 + x)^n (x > 0) occurs when r is the integer part of [(n+1)·x / (x+1)]. If this is an integer, both T_r and T_(r+1) are equal greatest terms.

What is the sum of binomial coefficients?

The sum of all binomial coefficients is C(n,0) + C(n,1) + ... + C(n,n) = 2^n. The sum of even-indexed coefficients equals sum of odd-indexed coefficients, both equal to 2^(n-1).

What is binomial theorem for any index?

For any rational number n (negative or fractional) and |x| < 1: (1 + x)^n = 1 + nx + n(n-1)/2! · x^2 + n(n-1)(n-2)/3! · x^3 + ... This is an infinite series convergent only when |x| < 1.

What is the multinomial theorem?

The multinomial theorem generalizes the binomial theorem: (a1 + a2 + ... + ak)^n = Σ [n!/(n1!·n2!·...·nk!)] · a1^n1 · a2^n2 · ... · ak^nk, where the sum is over all non-negative integers n1, n2, ..., nk such that n1 + n2 + ... + nk = n.

How many questions come from Binomial Theorem in JEE Mains?

On average 1 question (4 marks) appears from Binomial Theorem in every JEE Main session. Most repeated topics are: term independent of x, general term, middle term, greatest term, properties of binomial coefficients, expansion of (1+x)^n for negative/fractional index, and remainder problems.

How to find the last digit of a^b using binomial theorem?

Express the base a as (10k + d) where d is the last digit. Then a^b = (10k + d)^b. Expanding using binomial theorem, all terms except the last contain a factor of 10, so the last digit of a^b equals the last digit of d^b. Find the cyclic pattern of d's last digits (period 4 for most digits).

Binomial Theorem — Complete JEE Study Guide

Binomial Theorem is one of the most scoring chapters in JEE Algebra. It typically contributes 1 question (4 marks) in nearly every JEE Main session, and 1-2 questions in JEE Advanced. The chapter is largely formula-based, making it high-scoring with proper memorization and practice.

The most important topics for JEE from this chapter are term independent of x (most frequently asked), general term \(T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r}x^{n-r}a^r\), middle term (single for even \(n\), two for odd \(n\)), greatest term and greatest coefficient, properties of binomial coefficients (sum \(= 2^n\), alternating sum \(= 0\), sum of squares \(= \binom{2n}{n}\)), Vandermonde's identity, series with \(r\binom{n}{r}\) and \(r^2\binom{n}{r}\), binomial theorem for any index (\((1+x)^n\) for negative/fractional \(n\)), important expansions like \((1-x)^{-1}, (1+x)^{-1}, (1-x)^{-2}, (1+x)^{-2}\), multinomial theorem, and remainder/last digit problems.

The key formula is the general term \(T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r}x^{n-r}a^r\) in the expansion of \((x+a)^n\). To find the term independent of \(x\), set the exponent of \(x\) in \(T_{r+1}\) to zero and solve for \(r\), which must be a non-negative integer between 0 and \(n\). The middle term of \((x+a)^n\) when \(n\) is even is \(T_{n/2+1} = \binom{n}{n/2}x^{n/2}a^{n/2}\); when \(n\) is odd, there are two middle terms.

For sum problems, three substitutions are powerful: put \(x = 1\) to get the sum of all coefficients (\(= 2^n\)); put \(x = -1\) to get the alternating sum (\(= 0\)); differentiate and then substitute to get weighted sums like \(\sum r\binom{n}{r} = n \cdot 2^{n-1}\) and \(\sum r^2\binom{n}{r} = n(n+1) \cdot 2^{n-2}\). The sum of squares identity \(\sum \binom{n}{r}^2 = \binom{2n}{n}\) comes from comparing coefficients in \((1+x)^n \cdot (1+x)^n = (1+x)^{2n}\).

The binomial theorem for any index states \((1+x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2 + \cdots\) for any rational \(n\) (positive, negative, or fractional), but this infinite series converges only when \(|x| < 1\). The four must-memorize expansions are: \((1-x)^{-1} = 1+x+x^2+\cdots\), \((1+x)^{-1} = 1-x+x^2-\cdots\), \((1-x)^{-2} = 1+2x+3x^2+\cdots\), \((1+x)^{-2} = 1-2x+3x^2-\cdots\). For \((1-x)^{-n}\) the general term is \(\binom{n+r-1}{r}x^r\).

For the greatest term in \((1+x)^n\) (with \(x > 0\)): compute \(m = \frac{(n+1)x}{1+x}\). If \(m\) is an integer, both \(T_m\) and \(T_{m+1}\) are equal greatest terms; otherwise, the greatest term is \(T_{r+1}\) where \(r = \lfloor m \rfloor\). For numerically greatest term (when \(x\) may be negative), use \(|x|\) in the formula. The greatest binomial coefficient in \((1+x)^n\) is \(\binom{n}{n/2}\) for even \(n\), and both \(\binom{n}{(n-1)/2}\) and \(\binom{n}{(n+1)/2}\) for odd \(n\).

The multinomial theorem extends the binomial theorem to expressions with more than two terms: \((a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_k)^n = \sum \frac{n!}{n_1!n_2!\cdots n_k!}a_1^{n_1}a_2^{n_2}\cdots a_k^{n_k}\), where the sum is over all non-negative integer tuples \((n_1, \ldots, n_k)\) summing to \(n\). The number of terms in this expansion is \(\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}\). For \((a+b+c)^n\) there are \(\binom{n+2}{2}\) terms.

For remainder and last digit problems: to find the last digit of \(a^b\), express \(a\) as \((10k + d)\) where \(d\) is its last digit; then \(a^b \equiv d^b \pmod{10}\), and the last digit cycles with period 4 (for most digits). To find the remainder when \(a^b\) is divided by \(m\), first reduce \(a \pmod m\), then compute the reduced base raised to power \(b\) modulo \(m\), using patterns or Fermat's little theorem.

Binomial approximation \((1+x)^n \approx 1 + nx\) for small \(|x|\) is widely used in physics and numerical computations. Higher-order approximations include the second-order term \(\frac{n(n-1)}{2}x^2\). Common approximations: \(\sqrt{1+x} \approx 1 + x/2\), \((1+x)^{1/3} \approx 1 + x/3\), \((1-x)^{-1} \approx 1 + x\).
