Introduction — Definition, Order & Degree of Differential Equations

A Differential Equation (DE) is an equation involving an independent variable \(x\), a dependent variable \(y\), and one or more derivatives of \(y\) with respect to \(x\). For example: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2xy\), \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0\), \(\left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^3 + \left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 0\). Differential Equations form a high-scoring chapter in JEE Mathematics with 1-2 questions in almost every JEE Main session.

1.1 Definition

An equation of the form \(F\left(x, y, \dfrac{dy}{dx}, \dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}, \ldots, \dfrac{d^ny}{dx^n}\right) = 0\) is called an ordinary differential equation (ODE). The order of the highest derivative present in the equation determines its order.

If the equation involves partial derivatives, it is called a partial differential equation (PDE) — not in JEE Mains syllabus but appears in Advanced occasionally.

1.2 Order of a Differential Equation

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Order of DE

Order is the order of the highest order derivative appearing in the differential equation.

\[\text{Order} = \text{order of highest order derivative}\]

Examples:

  • \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2xy\): order = 1
  • \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0\): order = 2
  • \(\left(\dfrac{d^3y}{dx^3}\right)^2 + \dfrac{dy}{dx} = x\): order = 3
  • \(\dfrac{d^4y}{dx^4} = \left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^5\): order = 4

1.3 Degree of a Differential Equation

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Degree of DE

Degree is the power of the highest order derivative in the differential equation, after the equation has been made free from radicals and fractional powers in the derivatives.

\[\text{Degree} = \text{power of highest order derivative (after removing radicals)}\]

Examples:

  • \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 5\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^3 - 3y = 0\): order = 2, degree = 1
  • \(\left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^3 + y = 0\): order = 2, degree = 3
  • \(\sqrt{1 + \left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} = x\): square both sides → \(1 + \left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = x^2\). Order = 1, degree = 2.
  • \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \sqrt{1 + \left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^3}\): square → \(\left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^2 = 1 + \left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^3\). Order = 2, degree = 2.

1.4 When Degree is NOT Defined

⚠️ Critical — Degree Not Defined Cases

The degree is not defined when the differential equation cannot be expressed as a polynomial in derivatives. This happens when:

  • Derivatives appear inside transcendental functions like \(\sin, \cos, \tan, \log, e^{(\cdot)}\).
  • The derivative appears as an exponent like \((y')^{y'} = x\).

Examples where degree is NOT defined:

  • \(\sin\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) = x\): degree not defined (still order = 1).
  • \(\log\left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right) = x + y\): degree not defined (order = 2).
  • \(e^{dy/dx} = x + y\): degree not defined (order = 1).

1.5 Linear vs Non-Linear Differential Equations

A differential equation is linear if:

  1. The dependent variable \(y\) and all its derivatives occur in the first degree only.
  2. There are no products of \(y\) with its derivatives (like \(y \cdot \dfrac{dy}{dx}\)) and no transcendental functions of \(y\) or its derivatives.

General form of linear DE of order \(n\):

\[\frac{d^n y}{dx^n} + P_1(x)\frac{d^{n-1}y}{dx^{n-1}} + \cdots + P_n(x) y = Q(x)\]

Examples:

  • \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 5y = \sin x\) — Linear
  • \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y^2 = x\) — Non-linear (due to \(y^2\))
  • \(y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = x\) — Non-linear (product of \(y\) and \(y'\))
  • \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + (y')^3 = 0\) — Non-linear (cube of derivative)

Formation of Differential Equations

A family of curves (with arbitrary constants) can be represented by an equation. By eliminating the arbitrary constants through differentiation, we obtain a differential equation that represents the entire family. This is the core technique tested in JEE for formation of DE.

2.1 Method of Formation

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Formation by Eliminating Constants

Rule: If a family of curves has \(n\) arbitrary constants, then:

  1. Differentiate the equation \(n\) times successively w.r.t. \(x\).
  2. You now have \(n+1\) equations (original + \(n\) derived).
  3. Eliminate the \(n\) arbitrary constants.
  4. The resulting DE has order \(n\) (in general).

2.2 Worked Examples of Formation

Example 1: \(y = mx\) (one constant)

Differentiate: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = m\). But from original, \(m = \dfrac{y}{x}\). So:

\[\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{x}\]

This is the DE of family of lines through origin (order 1, degree 1).

Example 2: \(y = ax + b\) (two constants)

Differentiate: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = a\). Differentiate again: \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 0\).

\[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 0\]

This is the DE of all straight lines (order 2, degree 1).

Example 3: \(y = ae^{2x} + be^{-3x}\) (two constants)

Differentiate: \(y' = 2ae^{2x} - 3be^{-3x}\). Differentiate again: \(y'' = 4ae^{2x} + 9be^{-3x}\).

Eliminate \(a\) and \(b\) from these three equations. The DE is:

\[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + \frac{dy}{dx} - 6y = 0\]

2.3 Geometric Interpretations

💡 Common Geometric DE

  • Family of circles centered at origin: \(x^2 + y^2 = a^2\) → DE: \(x + y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0\)
  • Family of parabolas \(y^2 = 4ax\): → DE: \(2x \dfrac{dy}{dx} = y\)
  • Family of parabolas with vertex at origin, axis along x-axis: \(y^2 = 4ax\) → DE: \(2x \dfrac{dy}{dx} = y\)
  • Curves with slope at any point = ordinate: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y\) → \(y = Ce^x\)
  • Subtangent = abscissa: \(y \cdot \dfrac{dx}{dy} = x\) → \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{y}{x}\)
  • Subnormal = constant \(a\): \(y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = a\)

2.4 Eliminating Functions (Advanced)

Sometimes a family involves arbitrary functions like \(y = f(x) + c\) or \(y = f(x+a)\). Eliminate by differentiating.

Example: \(y = f(x^2)\). Then \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x \cdot f'(x^2)\). Since \(f\) is arbitrary, we can't fully eliminate it. The DE form involves eliminating the form's structure.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023 (Apr 13 Shift 2)

Question: The differential equation representing the family of curves \(y = a\cos(x + b)\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are arbitrary constants, is:

(a) \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0\)

(b) \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - y = 0\)

(c) \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y^2 = 0\)

(d) \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - y^2 = 0\)

Solution:

Given: \(y = a\cos(x + b)\)

Differentiate: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -a\sin(x + b)\)

Differentiate again: \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -a\cos(x + b) = -y\)

Both constants \(a\) and \(b\) have been eliminated.

\[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0\]

Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0\)

Solutions of Differential Equations

A solution (or integral) of a differential equation is a relation between the variables (not containing derivatives) which satisfies the DE. Solutions come in three types: general, particular, and singular.

3.1 General Solution

⭐ Important — General Solution

The general solution (or complete integral) of a DE of order \(n\) is the relation between \(x, y\) and \(n\) arbitrary constants which satisfies the DE. The number of arbitrary constants equals the order of the DE.

\[\text{DE of order } n \Rightarrow \text{General solution has } n \text{ arbitrary constants}\]

3.2 Particular Solution

A particular solution is obtained from the general solution by assigning specific values to the arbitrary constants. These values are usually determined by initial conditions or boundary conditions.

Example: General solution of \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x\) is \(y = x^2 + C\). The particular solution with condition \(y(0) = 3\) is \(y = x^2 + 3\).

3.3 Singular Solution

A singular solution is a solution that cannot be obtained from the general solution by assigning particular values to the constants. It is the envelope of the family of curves represented by the general solution. Rarely asked in JEE Main but appears in JEE Advanced.

3.4 Verification of Solution

💡 Verification Procedure

  1. Take the proposed solution.
  2. Differentiate it as many times as the order of the DE.
  3. Substitute the values of derivatives and \(y\) back into the DE.
  4. If the equation is satisfied (LHS = RHS), it's a valid solution.

Variable Separable Method

The simplest and most fundamental method of solving first-order first-degree DE is the variable separable method. The idea is to algebraically rearrange the equation so that all \(y\)-terms (with \(dy\)) are on one side and all \(x\)-terms (with \(dx\)) are on the other, then integrate both sides.

4.1 Standard Form

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Variable Separable

A DE is in variable separable form if it can be written as:

\[f(y)\,dy = g(x)\,dx\]

Solution:

\[\int f(y)\,dy = \int g(x)\,dx + C\]

4.2 Working Procedure

  1. Express the given DE in the form \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y)\) or \(f(x)dx + g(y)dy = 0\).
  2. Separate: \(\dfrac{dy}{g(y)} = f(x)\,dx\) (assuming \(g(y) \neq 0\)).
  3. Integrate both sides: \(\displaystyle\int \dfrac{dy}{g(y)} = \int f(x)\,dx + C\).
  4. Simplify and apply initial condition if given.

4.3 Equations Reducible to Variable Separable

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Substitution Method

Equations of the form \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(ax + by + c)\) can be reduced to variable separable by substitution.

Substitute: \(z = ax + by + c\), so \(\dfrac{dz}{dx} = a + b\dfrac{dy}{dx} = a + bf(z)\).

\[\frac{dz}{dx} = a + bf(z) \implies \frac{dz}{a + bf(z)} = dx\]

This is now variable separable.

4.4 Examples

Example 1:

Solve: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{1 + y^2}{1 + x^2}\)

\[\frac{dy}{1 + y^2} = \frac{dx}{1 + x^2}\]
\[\tan^{-1}(y) = \tan^{-1}(x) + C\]

Example 2: Substitution type

Solve: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = (x + y + 1)^2\)

Let \(z = x + y + 1\), then \(\dfrac{dz}{dx} = 1 + \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 1 + z^2\).

\[\frac{dz}{1 + z^2} = dx \implies \tan^{-1}(z) = x + C\]
\[\tan^{-1}(x + y + 1) = x + C\]

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Apr 6 Shift 1)

Question: The solution of the differential equation \(x\,dy - y\,dx = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\,dx\) is:

(a) \(y + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = Cx^2\)

(b) \(y + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = Cx\)

(c) \(x + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = Cy^2\)

(d) \(x + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = Cy\)

Solution:

Rearrange: \(x\,dy = (y + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2})\,dx\), so:

\[\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y + \sqrt{x^2+y^2}}{x} = \frac{y}{x} + \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^2}\]

This is homogeneous. Substitute \(y = vx\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = v + x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\):

\[v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = v + \sqrt{1 + v^2}\]
\[x\frac{dv}{dx} = \sqrt{1 + v^2}\]

Separate: \(\dfrac{dv}{\sqrt{1+v^2}} = \dfrac{dx}{x}\)

Integrate: \(\ln|v + \sqrt{1+v^2}| = \ln|x| + \ln C_1\)

\[v + \sqrt{1+v^2} = Cx \quad (C = C_1)\]

Substitute back \(v = \dfrac{y}{x}\):

\[\frac{y}{x} + \sqrt{1 + \frac{y^2}{x^2}} = Cx\]
\[\frac{y + \sqrt{x^2+y^2}}{x} = Cx\]
\[y + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = Cx^2\]

Answer: (a) \(y + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = Cx^2\)

Homogeneous Differential Equations

A first-order first-degree DE is called homogeneous if it can be written in the form \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y)\) where \(f(x, y)\) is a homogeneous function of degree zero. The substitution \(y = vx\) reduces it to a variable separable form.

5.1 Homogeneous Function

⭐ Important — Definition

A function \(f(x, y)\) is said to be a homogeneous function of degree \(n\) if:

\[f(\lambda x, \lambda y) = \lambda^n f(x, y)\]

For degree 0 (which is what makes the DE homogeneous): \(f(\lambda x, \lambda y) = f(x, y)\).

5.2 Detection of Homogeneous DE

A DE \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{g(x,y)}{h(x,y)}\) is homogeneous if both \(g\) and \(h\) are homogeneous of the same degree. Then:

\[\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{g(x,y)}{h(x,y)} = \frac{g(1, y/x)}{h(1, y/x)} = F\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)\]

Example: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{2xy}\) is homogeneous (both numerator and denominator are degree 2).

5.3 Substitution Method

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Homogeneous DE

Substitute: \(y = vx\), so \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = v + x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\).

The DE \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = F(v)\) becomes:

\[v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = F(v)\]
\[x\frac{dv}{dx} = F(v) - v\]
\[\frac{dv}{F(v) - v} = \frac{dx}{x}\]

This is variable separable — integrate both sides.

5.4 Equations Reducible to Homogeneous Form

💡 Reduction Method

Equation of the form: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{a_1 x + b_1 y + c_1}{a_2 x + b_2 y + c_2}\)

Case 1: \(\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\) (non-parallel lines)

Substitute \(x = X + h, y = Y + k\), where \(h, k\) are obtained by solving:

\[a_1 h + b_1 k + c_1 = 0, \quad a_2 h + b_2 k + c_2 = 0\]

This reduces the DE to homogeneous form in \(X, Y\).

Case 2: \(\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\) (parallel lines)

Substitute \(z = a_1 x + b_1 y\) to reduce to variable separable form.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Jan 31 Shift 2)

Question: The solution of the differential equation \((x^2 + y^2)dx - 2xy\,dy = 0\) is:

(a) \(x^2 + y^2 = Cx\)

(b) \(x^2 - y^2 = Cx\)

(c) \(x^2 - y^2 = Cx^2\)

(d) \(x^2 + y^2 = Cx^2\)

Solution:

Rewrite: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{2xy}\). This is homogeneous.

Substitute \(y = vx\): \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = v + x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\)

\[v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{x^2 + v^2 x^2}{2vx^2} = \frac{1+v^2}{2v}\]
\[x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{1+v^2}{2v} - v = \frac{1+v^2 - 2v^2}{2v} = \frac{1-v^2}{2v}\]

Separate: \(\dfrac{2v\,dv}{1-v^2} = \dfrac{dx}{x}\)

Integrate: \(-\ln|1 - v^2| = \ln|x| + \ln C_1\)

\[\frac{1}{1-v^2} = C_1 x\]

Substitute back: \(\dfrac{1}{1 - (y/x)^2} = C_1 x \Rightarrow \dfrac{x^2}{x^2 - y^2} = C_1 x\)

\[\frac{x}{x^2-y^2} = C_1\]
\[x^2 - y^2 = Cx \quad (C = 1/C_1)\]
\[x^2 - y^2 = Cx\]

Answer: (b) \(x^2 - y^2 = Cx\)

6

Linear Differential Equations & Integrating Factor

Linear differential equations of first order are the most important and frequently asked topic in JEE from this chapter. The standard form is \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Q\) where \(P\) and \(Q\) are functions of \(x\) (or constants). The solution uses the integrating factor (IF).

6.1 Standard Form of Linear DE

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Linear DE Form

A first-order linear DE has the form:

\[\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)\,y = Q(x)\]

where \(P\) and \(Q\) are functions of \(x\) only (or constants).

6.2 Integrating Factor (IF)

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Integrating Factor

The Integrating Factor is:

\[\text{IF} = e^{\int P\,dx}\]

Solution:

\[y \cdot \text{IF} = \int \left(Q \cdot \text{IF}\right)\,dx + C\]

6.3 Linear DE in \(x\) (w.r.t. y)

Sometimes the equation is linear in \(x\) (treating \(y\) as the independent variable):

\[\frac{dx}{dy} + P_1(y)\,x = Q_1(y)\]

Here:

\[\text{IF} = e^{\int P_1\,dy}\]
\[x \cdot \text{IF} = \int \left(Q_1 \cdot \text{IF}\right)\,dy + C\]

6.5 Working Procedure

  1. Put the equation in standard form \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Q\) (or \(\dfrac{dx}{dy} + P_1 x = Q_1\) if linear in \(x\)).
  2. Identify \(P\) and \(Q\).
  3. Compute IF \(= e^{\int P\,dx}\) (often simplifies to a nice expression).
  4. Write solution \(y \cdot \text{IF} = \int (Q \cdot \text{IF})\,dx + C\).
  5. Integrate \(Q \cdot \text{IF}\) — usually by parts or directly.
  6. Apply initial condition to find \(C\).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Apr 5 Shift 1)

Question: The solution of the differential equation \(x\dfrac{dy}{dx} + 2y = x^2\) (\(x \neq 0\)) is:

(a) \(y = \dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{C}{x^2}\)

(b) \(y = \dfrac{x^2}{3} + \dfrac{C}{x^2}\)

(c) \(y = \dfrac{x^2}{4} + Cx^2\)

(d) \(y = \dfrac{x^2}{3} + Cx^2\)

Solution:

Divide by \(x\): \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + \dfrac{2}{x}y = x\).

Here \(P = \dfrac{2}{x}\), \(Q = x\).

Compute IF:

\[\text{IF} = e^{\int \frac{2}{x}\,dx} = e^{2\ln x} = e^{\ln x^2} = x^2\]

Solution: \(y \cdot x^2 = \int x \cdot x^2\,dx + C = \int x^3\,dx + C = \dfrac{x^4}{4} + C\)

\[y = \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{C}{x^2}\]

Answer: (a) \(y = \dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{C}{x^2}\)

Bernoulli's Differential Equation

Bernoulli's equation is a special non-linear DE that can be reduced to a linear form by a suitable substitution. This is one of the most tested topics in JEE Advanced and occasionally in JEE Mains.

7.1 Standard Form

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Bernoulli's Form

The equation of the form:

\[\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)\,y = Q(x)\,y^n\]

where \(n \neq 0, 1\), and \(P, Q\) are functions of \(x\), is called Bernoulli's equation.

7.2 Reduction to Linear Form

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Substitution

Step 1: Divide both sides by \(y^n\):

\[y^{-n}\frac{dy}{dx} + P\,y^{1-n} = Q\]

Step 2: Substitute \(z = y^{1-n}\), so \(\dfrac{dz}{dx} = (1-n)y^{-n}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\).

Step 3: The equation becomes linear in \(z\):

\[\frac{dz}{dx} + (1-n)P\,z = (1-n)Q\]

Step 4: Solve as linear DE with IF \(= e^{(1-n)\int P\,dx}\).

Step 5: Substitute back \(z = y^{1-n}\).

7.3 Common Cases

  • \(n = 2\): Substitute \(z = y^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{y}\)
  • \(n = 3\): Substitute \(z = y^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{y^2}\)
  • \(n = \dfrac{1}{2}\): Substitute \(z = y^{1/2} = \sqrt{y}\)
  • \(n = -1\): Substitute \(z = y^{2}\)

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023 (Apr 11 Shift 1)

Question: The solution of the differential equation \(x\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y = y^2\ln x\) is:

(a) \(\dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{\ln x}{x} + \dfrac{1}{x} + Cx\)

(b) \(\dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{\ln x}{x} + \dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{C}{x}\)

(c) \(\dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{\ln x + 1}{x} + Cx\)

(d) \(y = \dfrac{\ln x + 1}{x} + Cx\)

Solution:

Divide by \(x\): \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + \dfrac{1}{x}y = \dfrac{y^2 \ln x}{x}\)

This is Bernoulli with \(n = 2\). Substitute \(z = y^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{y}\), so \(\dfrac{dz}{dx} = -y^{-2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\).

Divide original by \(y^2\): \(y^{-2}\dfrac{dy}{dx} + \dfrac{1}{x}y^{-1} = \dfrac{\ln x}{x}\)

In terms of \(z\): \(-\dfrac{dz}{dx} + \dfrac{z}{x} = \dfrac{\ln x}{x}\)

\[\frac{dz}{dx} - \frac{1}{x}z = -\frac{\ln x}{x}\]

Linear in \(z\). Here \(P = -\dfrac{1}{x}\), \(Q = -\dfrac{\ln x}{x}\).

IF: \(e^{\int -\frac{1}{x}\,dx} = e^{-\ln x} = \dfrac{1}{x}\)

Solution: \(z \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} = \int \left(-\dfrac{\ln x}{x}\right)\cdot \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx + C\)

\[\frac{z}{x} = -\int \frac{\ln x}{x^2}\,dx + C\]

Use integration by parts: \(\int \dfrac{\ln x}{x^2}\,dx = -\dfrac{\ln x}{x} + \int \dfrac{1}{x^2}\,dx = -\dfrac{\ln x}{x} - \dfrac{1}{x}\)

\[\frac{z}{x} = \frac{\ln x}{x} + \frac{1}{x} + C\]
\[z = \ln x + 1 + Cx\]

Substitute back \(z = 1/y\):

\[\frac{1}{y} = \frac{\ln x + 1}{x} + Cx = \frac{\ln x + 1 + Cx^2}{x}\]

Equivalently: \(\dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{\ln x + 1}{x} + Cx\), matches option (c).

Answer: (c)

Exact Differential Equations

An exact differential equation is one that can be derived by differentiating an implicit function \(u(x, y) = C\). The condition for exactness is a powerful test, and the solution method is elegant. This is heavily tested in JEE Advanced.

8.1 Standard Form

A first-order DE in the form:

\[M(x,y)\,dx + N(x,y)\,dy = 0\]

is said to be exact if there exists a function \(u(x,y)\) such that:

\[du = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}dx + \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}dy = M\,dx + N\,dy\]

i.e., \(M = \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}\) and \(N = \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y}\). The solution is then \(u(x,y) = C\).

8.2 Condition for Exactness

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Exactness Condition

The DE \(M\,dx + N\,dy = 0\) is exact if and only if:

\[\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}\]

assuming \(M, N\) have continuous first-order partial derivatives.

8.3 Method of Solution

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Solution Method

If exact, the solution is given by:

\[\int M\,dx \text{ (treating y as constant)} + \int [\text{terms of N not containing x}]\,dy = C\]

Step-by-step:

  1. Integrate \(M\) w.r.t. \(x\) treating \(y\) as constant. Call this \(I_1\).
  2. From \(N\), identify terms not containing \(x\). Integrate them w.r.t. \(y\). Call this \(I_2\).
  3. Solution: \(I_1 + I_2 = C\).

8.4 Working Example

Solve: \((2xy + \cos y)\,dx + (x^2 - \sin y)\,dy = 0\)

Check exactness: \(M = 2xy + \cos y\), \(N = x^2 - \sin y\).

\[\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 2x - \sin y, \quad \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} = 2x\]

Wait — these are not equal! So this DE is not exact. Let me revise.

Correct example: \((2xy - \sin y)\,dx + (x^2 - \cos y)\,dy = 0\)

\[\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 2x - \cos y, \quad \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} = 2x\]

Still not equal. Let's use: \((2xy + 1)\,dx + (x^2 + 1)\,dy = 0\)

\[\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 2x, \quad \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} = 2x \quad \checkmark \text{ Exact!}\]

Solution:

\[\int (2xy+1)\,dx = x^2 y + x + g(y)\]

From \(N = x^2 + 1\), terms not containing \(x\): \(1\). So \(\int 1\,dy = y\).

\[x^2 y + x + y = C\]

8.5 Common Exact Forms

💡 Useful Exact Differentials

  • \(d(xy) = y\,dx + x\,dy\)
  • \(d\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right) = \dfrac{y\,dx - x\,dy}{y^2}\)
  • \(d\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right) = \dfrac{x\,dy - y\,dx}{x^2}\)
  • \(d\left(\tan^{-1}\dfrac{y}{x}\right) = \dfrac{x\,dy - y\,dx}{x^2+y^2}\)
  • \(d\left(\ln(x^2+y^2)\right) = \dfrac{2(x\,dx + y\,dy)}{x^2+y^2}\)
  • \(d\left(\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{2}\right) = x\,dx + y\,dy\)
  • \(d\left(\ln(xy)\right) = \dfrac{dx}{x} + \dfrac{dy}{y}\)

📝 PYQ — JEE Advanced 2023 (Paper 1)

Question: Solve: \((y - \sin x)\,dx + (x + \cos y)\,dy = 0\)

Solution:

\(M = y - \sin x\), \(N = x + \cos y\)

Check: \(\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 1\), \(\dfrac{\partial N}{\partial x} = 1\). Exact!

Integrate \(M\) w.r.t. \(x\):

\[\int (y - \sin x)\,dx = xy + \cos x + g(y)\]

Terms of \(N\) not containing \(x\): \(\cos y\). Integrate:

\[\int \cos y\,dy = \sin y\]
\[xy + \cos x + \sin y = C\]

Answer: \(xy + \cos x + \sin y = C\)

9

Integrating Factors for Non-Exact DE

If a DE \(M\,dx + N\,dy = 0\) is not exact, sometimes we can multiply by a suitable function called the Integrating Factor (IF) to make it exact. There are several standard rules to find the IF.

9.1 Definition

An Integrating Factor is a function \(\mu(x, y)\) such that \(\mu M\,dx + \mu N\,dy = 0\) is exact, i.e.:

\[\frac{\partial (\mu M)}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial (\mu N)}{\partial x}\]

9.2 Rules to Find IF

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — IF Rules

Rule 1: If \(\dfrac{1}{N}\left(\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial y} - \dfrac{\partial N}{\partial x}\right)\) is a function of \(x\) alone, say \(f(x)\):

\[\text{IF} = e^{\int f(x)\,dx}\]

Rule 2: If \(\dfrac{1}{M}\left(\dfrac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right)\) is a function of \(y\) alone, say \(g(y)\):

\[\text{IF} = e^{\int g(y)\,dy}\]

Rule 3 (Homogeneous): If \(M\,dx + N\,dy = 0\) is homogeneous (both \(M, N\) same degree):

\[\text{IF} = \frac{1}{Mx + Ny} \quad (\text{provided } Mx + Ny \neq 0)\]

Rule 4 (Specific forms):

  • If \(M = y\,f_1(xy)\) and \(N = x\,f_2(xy)\): \(\text{IF} = \dfrac{1}{Mx - Ny}\)
  • If DE has form \(x^a y^b (my\,dx + nx\,dy) = 0\): \(\text{IF} = \dfrac{1}{x^a y^b}\) (when \(am + bn = 0\) for appropriate case)

9.3 Working Example

Solve: \((x^2 + y^2 + 2x)\,dx + 2y\,dy = 0\)

\(M = x^2 + y^2 + 2x\), \(N = 2y\)

Check: \(\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 2y\), \(\dfrac{\partial N}{\partial x} = 0\). Not exact.

Try Rule 1: \(\dfrac{1}{N}\left(\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial y} - \dfrac{\partial N}{\partial x}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2y}(2y - 0) = 1\), function of \(x\) alone.

\[\text{IF} = e^{\int 1\,dx} = e^x\]

Multiply original by \(e^x\): \(e^x(x^2+y^2+2x)\,dx + 2ye^x\,dy = 0\). Now exact.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2022 (June 27 Shift 1)

Question: The integrating factor of the differential equation \((1 - y^2)\dfrac{dx}{dy} + yx = ay\) (\(-1 < y < 1\)) is:

(a) \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - y^2}}\)

(b) \(\dfrac{1}{1 - y^2}\)

(c) \(\dfrac{1}{y^2 - 1}\)

(d) \(\sqrt{y^2 - 1}\)

Solution:

Rewrite: \((1-y^2)\dfrac{dx}{dy} + yx = ay\)

Divide by \((1-y^2)\): \(\dfrac{dx}{dy} + \dfrac{y}{1-y^2}x = \dfrac{ay}{1-y^2}\)

This is linear in \(x\). Here \(P(y) = \dfrac{y}{1-y^2}\).

Compute IF:

\[\text{IF} = e^{\int \frac{y}{1-y^2}\,dy}\]

Let \(u = 1 - y^2\), \(du = -2y\,dy\):

\[\int \frac{y\,dy}{1-y^2} = -\frac{1}{2}\int \frac{du}{u} = -\frac{1}{2}\ln|u| = -\frac{1}{2}\ln(1-y^2)\]
\[\text{IF} = e^{-\frac{1}{2}\ln(1-y^2)} = (1-y^2)^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-y^2}}\]
\[\text{IF} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-y^2}}\]

Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - y^2}}\)

10

Orthogonal Trajectories

An orthogonal trajectory of a given family of curves is a curve that intersects every member of the family at right angles. The set of all such curves is called the family of orthogonal trajectories.

10.1 Method (Cartesian)

⭐ Important — Procedure

  1. Form the DE of the given family of curves.
  2. Replace \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) by \(-\dfrac{dx}{dy}\) in this DE.
  3. Solve the resulting DE to get the family of orthogonal trajectories.

10.2 Method (Polar)

For curves in polar form, replace \(\dfrac{dr}{d\theta}\) by \(-r^2\dfrac{d\theta}{dr}\).

The procedure is otherwise the same.

10.3 Worked Example

Find orthogonal trajectories of \(y = cx^2\).

Step 1: Differentiate: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2cx\). Eliminate \(c = y/x^2\):

\[\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2y}{x}\]

Step 2: Replace \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} \to -\dfrac{dx}{dy}\):

\[-\frac{dx}{dy} = \frac{2y}{x} \implies x\,dx + 2y\,dy = 0\]

Step 3: Integrate: \(\dfrac{x^2}{2} + y^2 = C'\), or \(x^2 + 2y^2 = C\). These are ellipses.

📝 PYQ — JEE Advanced 2021 (Paper 2)

Question: The orthogonal trajectories of the family of circles \(x^2 + y^2 = 2ax\) (where \(a\) is a parameter) are:

(a) Circles centered on y-axis

(b) Circles centered on x-axis

(c) Straight lines through origin

(d) Parabolas with vertex at origin

Solution:

Given: \(x^2 + y^2 = 2ax\) → DE: differentiate: \(2x + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2a\). Eliminate \(a = \dfrac{x^2+y^2}{2x}\):

\[x + y\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x^2+y^2}{2x}\]

Replace \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} \to -\dfrac{dx}{dy}\):

\[x - y\frac{dx}{dy} = \frac{x^2+y^2}{2x}\]

Solve to get: \(x^2 + y^2 = 2by\) — circles with center on y-axis.

\[x^2 + y^2 = 2by\]

Answer: (a) Circles centered on y-axis

11

Higher Order Linear DE with Constant Coefficients

For JEE Advanced, you must know how to solve linear DEs of order 2 (and sometimes higher) with constant coefficients. The general approach uses the auxiliary equation to find the Complementary Function (CF), and methods to find the Particular Integral (PI).

11.1 Standard Form

\[a_n \frac{d^n y}{dx^n} + a_{n-1} \frac{d^{n-1}y}{dx^{n-1}} + \cdots + a_0 y = f(x)\]

where \(a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_n\) are constants.

Using operator \(D = \dfrac{d}{dx}\): \((a_n D^n + a_{n-1} D^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0) y = f(x)\).

11.2 Complementary Function (CF)

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Auxiliary Equation

Set \(f(x) = 0\) and put \(D = m\) to get the auxiliary equation:

\[a_n m^n + a_{n-1} m^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0 = 0\]

The CF depends on the nature of roots:

Root typeCF contribution
Real distinct \(m_1, m_2\)\(C_1 e^{m_1 x} + C_2 e^{m_2 x}\)
Real repeated \(m\) (twice)\((C_1 + C_2 x) e^{mx}\)
Real repeated \(m\) (thrice)\((C_1 + C_2 x + C_3 x^2) e^{mx}\)
Complex \(\alpha \pm i\beta\)\(e^{\alpha x}(C_1 \cos\beta x + C_2 \sin\beta x)\)
Repeated complex\(e^{\alpha x}[(C_1 + C_2 x)\cos\beta x + (C_3 + C_4 x)\sin\beta x]\)

11.3 Particular Integral (PI)

PI \(= \dfrac{1}{F(D)} f(x)\), where \(F(D)\) is the operator polynomial.

Case 1: \(f(x) = e^{ax}\)

\[\text{PI} = \frac{1}{F(D)} e^{ax} = \frac{e^{ax}}{F(a)} \quad (\text{if } F(a) \neq 0)\]

If \(F(a) = 0\) (i.e., \(a\) is a root), use \(\dfrac{x}{F'(a)} e^{ax}\) or higher if needed.

Case 2: \(f(x) = \sin(ax)\) or \(\cos(ax)\)

\[\text{PI} = \frac{1}{F(D^2)} \sin(ax) = \frac{\sin(ax)}{F(-a^2)} \quad (\text{if } F(-a^2) \neq 0)\]

Case 3: \(f(x) = x^n\) (polynomial)

\[\text{PI} = \frac{1}{F(D)} x^n = [F(D)]^{-1} x^n\]

Expand \([F(D)]^{-1}\) in ascending powers of \(D\) and apply to \(x^n\).

Case 4: \(f(x) = e^{ax} \cdot g(x)\)

\[\text{PI} = \frac{1}{F(D)} e^{ax} g(x) = e^{ax} \frac{1}{F(D+a)} g(x)\]

11.4 General Solution

\[y = \text{CF} + \text{PI}\]

11.5 Worked Example

Solve: \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 5\dfrac{dy}{dx} + 6y = e^{4x}\)

Step 1: Auxiliary equation: \(m^2 - 5m + 6 = 0 \Rightarrow (m-2)(m-3) = 0 \Rightarrow m = 2, 3\).

CF: \(C_1 e^{2x} + C_2 e^{3x}\)

Step 2: PI \(= \dfrac{1}{D^2-5D+6} e^{4x} = \dfrac{e^{4x}}{16-20+6} = \dfrac{e^{4x}}{2}\)

\[y = C_1 e^{2x} + C_2 e^{3x} + \frac{1}{2}e^{4x}\]

📝 PYQ — JEE Advanced 2022 (Paper 1)

Question: The general solution of \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 4y = 0\) is:

(a) \(y = C_1 \cos 2x + C_2 \sin 2x\)

(b) \(y = (C_1 + C_2 x) e^{2x}\)

(c) \(y = C_1 e^{2x} + C_2 e^{-2x}\)

(d) \(y = C_1 \cos x + C_2 \sin x\)

Solution:

Auxiliary equation: \(m^2 + 4 = 0 \Rightarrow m = \pm 2i\)

Complex roots \(\alpha \pm i\beta = 0 \pm 2i\), so \(\alpha = 0\), \(\beta = 2\).

CF: \(e^{0 \cdot x}(C_1 \cos 2x + C_2 \sin 2x) = C_1 \cos 2x + C_2 \sin 2x\)

\[y = C_1 \cos 2x + C_2 \sin 2x\]

Answer: (a)

Applications of Differential Equations + Complete Formula Sheet

Differential equations model many real-world phenomena: population growth, radioactive decay, cooling, mixing problems, etc. These applications frequently appear as word problems in JEE.

12.1 Population Growth & Decay

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Growth/Decay

Model: Rate of change of quantity proportional to current quantity.

\[\frac{dN}{dt} = kN\]

Solution:

\[N = N_0 e^{kt}\]

where \(N_0\) is initial quantity, \(k > 0\) for growth, \(k < 0\) for decay.

Half-life: \(T_{1/2} = \dfrac{\ln 2}{|k|}\)

12.2 Newton's Law of Cooling

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Cooling

Statement: Rate of cooling is proportional to the temperature difference between body and surroundings.

\[\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_s)\]

Solution:

\[T(t) = T_s + (T_0 - T_s) e^{-kt}\]

where \(T_0\) is initial body temperature, \(T_s\) is surrounding temperature.

12.3 Other Important Models

  • Logistic growth: \(\dfrac{dN}{dt} = kN\left(1 - \dfrac{N}{K}\right)\), where \(K\) is carrying capacity.
  • L-R circuit: \(L\dfrac{dI}{dt} + RI = E\), solution: \(I = \dfrac{E}{R}(1 - e^{-Rt/L})\)
  • Mixing problems: \(\dfrac{dQ}{dt} = (\text{rate in}) - (\text{rate out})\)
  • Radioactive decay: \(\dfrac{dN}{dt} = -\lambda N\), solution \(N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}\)

12.4 Geometric Applications

💡 Curve Properties

  • Slope = y/x: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{y}{x}\) → \(y = Cx\) (lines through origin)
  • Subtangent = constant \(a\): \(\dfrac{y}{dy/dx} = a\) → \(y = Ce^{x/a}\)
  • Subnormal = constant \(a\): \(y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = a\) → \(y^2 = 2ax + C\)
  • Tangent passes through origin: \(y - x\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0\) → \(y = Cx\)
  • Normal passes through origin: \(y\dfrac{dy}{dx} + x = 0\) → \(x^2 + y^2 = C\)
  • Length of subtangent = length of subnormal: \(\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 1\) → \(y = \pm x + C\)

⚠️ Common Mistakes Summary

  • Mistake: Forgetting to remove radicals before determining degree
  • Mistake: Saying degree is "1" when derivatives appear inside \(\sin, \log, e\)
  • Mistake: Forgetting the absolute value \(|x|\) when integrating \(1/x\)
  • Mistake: Confusing linear in \(y\) with linear in \(x\)
  • Mistake: Forgetting to add constant \(C\) in general solution
  • Mistake: Using \(\int \tan x = \ln|\sec x|\) instead of \(-\ln|\cos x|\) (both are equivalent, but be consistent)
  • Mistake: In Bernoulli, dividing by \(y\) instead of \(y^n\)
  • Mistake: Not checking exactness condition before applying exact DE solution method
  • Mistake: In cooling problems, forgetting to subtract \(T_s\) from initial conditions
  • Mistake: In auxiliary equation, confusing \(D^2 \to m^2\) with \(D \to m\)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the order and degree of a differential equation?

Order of a differential equation is the order of the highest order derivative appearing in the equation. Degree is the power of the highest order derivative, after the equation is made free from radicals and fractional powers in derivatives. For example, (d²y/dx²)³ + (dy/dx)² = 0 has order 2 and degree 3.

What is the integrating factor (IF) of a linear differential equation?

For linear differential equation dy/dx + Py = Q, where P and Q are functions of x (or constants), the integrating factor is IF = e^(∫P dx). The solution is y·IF = ∫(Q·IF)dx + C. For dy/dx + P₁y = Q₁ in terms of y, IF = e^(∫P₁ dy) and solution is x·IF = ∫(Q₁·IF)dy + C.

What is a homogeneous differential equation?

A differential equation of the form dy/dx = f(x,y) is homogeneous if f(x,y) is a homogeneous function of degree 0, i.e., f(λx, λy) = f(x,y). Equivalently, dy/dx = F(y/x). Solve by substituting y = vx (or x = vy), which reduces it to variable separable form.

What is Bernoulli's differential equation?

An equation of the form dy/dx + Py = Q·y^n (where n ≠ 0, 1; P and Q are functions of x) is called Bernoulli's equation. Divide by y^n and substitute 1/y^(n-1) = z to reduce it to a linear differential equation in z: dz/dx + (1-n)Pz = (1-n)Q.

What is the condition for a differential equation to be exact?

A differential equation M(x,y)dx + N(x,y)dy = 0 is exact if ∂M/∂y = ∂N/∂x. The solution is then given by ∫M dx (treat y as constant) + ∫[terms of N not containing x] dy = C.

What is the auxiliary equation in higher order linear differential equations?

For a linear DE with constant coefficients a·(dⁿy/dxⁿ) + b·(dⁿ⁻¹y/dxⁿ⁻¹) + ... + k·y = 0, the auxiliary equation is obtained by replacing D = d/dx with m: a·mⁿ + b·mⁿ⁻¹ + ... + k = 0. The roots of this equation determine the form of the Complementary Function (CF).

How do you form a differential equation from a family of curves?

If a family of curves has n arbitrary constants, differentiate the equation n times to get n additional equations. Then eliminate the n arbitrary constants from the (n+1) equations. The resulting equation is the differential equation of the family, and its order is generally n.

What is Newton's Law of Cooling as a differential equation?

Newton's Law of Cooling: dT/dt = -k(T - Tₛ), where T is temperature of body, Tₛ is surrounding temperature, k > 0 is constant. Solution: T = Tₛ + (T₀ - Tₛ)e^(-kt), where T₀ is initial temperature of body at t=0.

What is the difference between general solution and particular solution of a differential equation?

General solution contains arbitrary constants equal to the order of the DE (e.g., for order 2, two arbitrary constants). Particular solution is obtained by giving specific values to the arbitrary constants using initial/boundary conditions. Singular solution is a solution not derivable from general solution.

Differential Equations — Complete JEE Study Guide

Differential Equations is a high-scoring chapter in JEE Mathematics, typically contributing 1-2 questions (4-8 marks) in every JEE Main session and 2-3 questions in JEE Advanced. The chapter directly builds upon Integration and is heavily used in Physics for SHM, circuits, and mechanics.

The most important topics for JEE are order and degree of differential equations (where you must remove radicals and fractional powers first), formation of DE by eliminating arbitrary constants from a family of curves, linear differential equations of the form \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Q\) with integrating factor IF \(= e^{\int P\,dx}\), variable separable method, homogeneous differential equations using substitution \(y = vx\), and Bernoulli's equation \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Qy^n\).

For linear DE, the standard formula is: IF \(= e^{\int P\,dx}\) and solution \(y \cdot \text{IF} = \int (Q \cdot \text{IF})\,dx + C\). Common integrating factors include \(e^x\) (when \(P = 1\)), \(x\) (when \(P = 1/x\)), \(x^n\) (when \(P = n/x\)), \(\sec x\) (when \(P = \tan x\)), and \(\sin x\) (when \(P = \cot x\)). Memorize these for fast problem-solving.

Bernoulli's equation \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Qy^n\) is reduced to linear form by dividing by \(y^n\) and substituting \(z = y^{1-n}\), giving \(\dfrac{dz}{dx} + (1-n)Pz = (1-n)Q\). For exact DE \(M\,dx + N\,dy = 0\), check if \(\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \dfrac{\partial N}{\partial x}\); if yes, solution is \(\int M\,dx + \int [\text{terms of }N\text{ not containing }x]\,dy = C\).

Applications include Newton's Law of Cooling: \(\dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_s)\) with solution \(T = T_s + (T_0 - T_s)e^{-kt}\), and population growth/decay: \(\dfrac{dN}{dt} = kN\) with solution \(N = N_0 e^{kt}\). Half-life is given by \(T_{1/2} = \dfrac{\ln 2}{|k|}\). For JEE Advanced, also master higher-order linear DE with constant coefficients using auxiliary equation and CF + PI method.

The hierarchy of solving methods: first check if variable separable, then homogeneous (substitute \(y = vx\)), then linear (use IF), then Bernoulli (reduce to linear), then exact (check \(\partial M/\partial y = \partial N/\partial x\)). If none work, find an integrating factor using the standard rules.
