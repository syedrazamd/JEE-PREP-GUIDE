> HOW TO USE

Complete JEE Mathematics Preparation Guide 2026

Why Choose Our Mathematics Notes?

Every chapter here is built for JEE — proofs and concepts for a first read, short notes that compress a full chapter into 15 minutes, a formula sheet with one-line "use when" notes, and a PYQ page with past questions tagged by year and paper.

High-weightage chapters like Calculus and Coordinate Geometry carry extra solved examples because that is exactly where JEE Main and Advanced award the most marks.

Chapter-wise JEE Weightage

Calculus is the single largest block at roughly 35% of JEE Main Maths, with Algebra at about 30% and Coordinate Geometry around 20%. Limits, Integration, and Applications of Derivatives appear in nearly every paper.

Vectors & 3D, Trigonometry, and Probability together contribute the remaining ~20%. These are shorter chapters that give quick, predictable marks — ideal for the last phase of preparation.

How to Use These Maths Notes Effectively?

  1. 1. Start with Sets, Relations & Functions, then Complex Numbers and Quadratic Equations.
  2. 2. Master Trigonometry early — it feeds into Coordinate Geometry and calculus.
  3. 3. Complete Calculus systematically: Limits → Differentiation → Applications → Integration → Differential Equations.
  4. 4. Practice Coordinate Geometry daily — it carries roughly 20% weightage.
  5. 5. Solve 30–50 problems per chapter before moving on; Maths is won by volume of practice.
  6. 6. Time yourself in mocks — target about 3 minutes per question.
  7. 7. Learn the shortcut tricks from the notes to save minutes in the exam.
  8. 8. Solve chapter-wise PYQs after finishing the notes — untimed first, then timed.

> FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What are all the chapters in JEE Mathematics?

JEE Mathematics has 32 chapters grouped into Algebra, Trigonometry, Coordinate Geometry, Calculus, and Vectors & 3D — plus Probability and Statistics. 27 chapters are live right now across every section: Algebra, Calculus, Coordinate Geometry, Trigonometry, Vectors & 3D, and Probability & Statistics.

What are the high-weightage chapters in JEE Maths?

High-weightage JEE Maths chapters: Calculus (Limits, Integration, Applications of Derivatives) ~35%, Coordinate Geometry (Circles, Conics) ~20%, Algebra (Matrices, P&C, Complex Numbers) ~30%, Vectors & 3D Geometry ~12%, Probability ~8%. Calculus is the single highest-weightage section and should be prioritised.

What are the Calculus chapters for JEE?

Calculus chapters for JEE are: Limits, Continuity & Differentiability, Differentiation (chain rule, implicit, parametric), Applications of Derivatives (maxima/minima, tangents, monotonicity), Indefinite Integration, Definite Integration & Area under Curves, and Differential Equations. Together they carry ~35% of JEE Maths marks.

What are the Coordinate Geometry chapters for JEE?

Coordinate Geometry chapters for JEE are: Straight Lines, Circles, Parabola, Ellipse, and Hyperbola. Circles and conics (Parabola, Ellipse, Hyperbola) are especially high-weightage in JEE Mains, often contributing 2–3 questions per paper.

Which Maths chapters are available right now?

27 chapters are live across Algebra, Calculus, Coordinate Geometry, Trigonometry, Vectors & 3D, and Probability & Statistics. The remaining chapters are being added through 2026.

Is JEE Maths hard?

JEE Maths is considered the most time-consuming section but is also the most scoring with practice. Calculus and Coordinate Geometry require deep problem-solving skills. Algebra (Matrices, P&C) and Trigonometry are relatively more formula-based. With consistent daily practice and PYQ solving, JEE Maths becomes manageable and predictable.
