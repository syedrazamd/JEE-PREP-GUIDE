Rate of Change of Quantities

The derivative $\frac{dy}{dx}$ represents the rate of change of $y$ with respect to $x$. In physical applications, if $y = f(t)$ where $t$ is time, then $\frac{dy}{dt}$ is the rate of change of $y$ with respect to time. This concept is widely used to solve "related rates" problems.

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ Topic — Volume/Surface Area Expansion

If two variables $x$ and $y$ are related, say $y = f(x)$, and both vary with time $t$, we use the Chain Rule:

\[\frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{dy}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}\]

For example, if the radius of a sphere is changing at rate $\frac{dr}{dt}$, the rate of change of volume $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ is $\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \frac{dr}{dt}$.

1.1 Common Geometrical Rates

  • Sphere: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \implies \frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \frac{dr}{dt}$. Surface Area $S = 4\pi r^2 \implies \frac{dS}{dt} = 8\pi r \frac{dr}{dt}$.
  • Cube: $V = x^3 \implies \frac{dV}{dt} = 3x^2 \frac{dx}{dt}$. $S = 6x^2 \implies \frac{dS}{dt} = 12x \frac{dx}{dt}$.
  • Cylinder: $V = \pi r^2 h$. If $h$ constant, $\frac{dV}{dt} = 2\pi r h \frac{dr}{dt}$. If $r$ constant, $\frac{dV}{dt} = \pi r^2 \frac{dh}{dt}$.
  • Cone: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$. Often involves similar triangles to relate $r$ and $h$.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Jan 31 Shift 1)

Question: Water is being filled in a right circular cone at a constant rate of $5 \text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$. The height of the cone is $10 \text{ cm}$ and the semi-vertical angle is $30^\circ$. Find the rate at which the height of the water is increasing when the water level is $5 \text{ cm}$.

Solution:

Volume of cone $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$.

Given semi-vertical angle $\alpha = 30^\circ$. We know $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{r}{h} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{r}{h} \implies r = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$.

Substitute $r$ in $V$:

\[V = \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi \frac{h^3}{3} = \frac{\pi h^3}{9}\]

Differentiate w.r.t time $t$:

\[\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{\pi}{9} \cdot 3h^2 \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{\pi h^2}{3} \frac{dh}{dt}\]

We are given $\frac{dV}{dt} = 5$ and $h = 5$. Substitute to find $\frac{dh}{dt}$:

\[5 = \frac{\pi (5)^2}{3} \frac{dh}{dt} \implies 5 = \frac{25\pi}{3} \frac{dh}{dt}\]
\[\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{15}{25\pi} = \frac{3}{5\pi} \text{ cm/s}\]
\[\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{3}{5\pi} \text{ cm/s}\]

Answer: $\frac{3}{5\pi} \text{ cm/s}$

Tangents and Normals

The derivative $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at a point gives the slope of the tangent to the curve $y = f(x)$ at that point. The normal is the line perpendicular to the tangent at that point.

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ Topic — Slope & Equations of Tangent/Normal

For a curve $y = f(x)$ at point $(x_1, y_1)$:

  • Slope of Tangent: $m = \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)_{(x_1, y_1)}$
  • Slope of Normal: $m_N = -\frac{1}{m} = -\frac{dx}{dy}$ (if $m \neq 0$)
  • Equation of Tangent: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$
  • Equation of Normal: $y - y_1 = -\frac{1}{m}(x - x_1)$

2.1 Subtangent and Subnormal

For point $P(x_1, y_1)$ on curve, lengths of subtangent and subnormal are projections on the x-axis:

  • Subtangent: $T_m = \left| \frac{y_1}{dy/dx} \right|$
  • Subnormal: $N_m = \left| y_1 \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} \right|$
  • Length of Tangent: $\left| \frac{y_1 \sqrt{1+(dy/dx)^2}}{dy/dx} \right|$
  • Length of Normal: $\left| y_1 \sqrt{1+(dy/dx)^2} \right|$

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023 (Apr 10 Shift 2)

Question: If the tangent to the curve $y = x^3 - x$ at a point $P$ is parallel to the line $2x - y + 1 = 0$, then the coordinates of $P$ are:

(a) $(1, 0)$

(b) $(-1, 0)$

(c) $(2, 6)$

(d) Both (a) and (b)

Solution:

Slope of given line $2x - y + 1 = 0 \implies y = 2x + 1$ is $m = 2$.

Curve: $y = x^3 - x \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 - 1$.

Since tangent at $P(x_1, y_1)$ is parallel to the line, their slopes are equal:

\[3x_1^2 - 1 = 2 \implies 3x_1^2 = 3 \implies x_1^2 = 1\]
\[x_1 = 1 \text{ or } x_1 = -1\]

If $x_1 = 1$, $y_1 = (1)^3 - 1 = 0$. Point is $(1, 0)$.

If $x_1 = -1$, $y_1 = (-1)^3 - (-1) = -1 + 1 = 0$. Point is $(-1, 0)$.

\[\text{Points are } (1, 0) \text{ and } (-1, 0)\]

Answer: (d) Both (a) and (b)

Approximations and Errors

Differentials are used to estimate small changes in the dependent variable $y$ corresponding to small changes in the independent variable $x$. This is highly useful for approximating values of complex functions and calculating relative/percentage errors.

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ Topic — Approximating Values

If $y = f(x)$, and $\Delta x$ is a small increment in $x$, then the corresponding increment in $y$ is approximated by:

\[\Delta y \approx dy = f'(x) \Delta x\]

Thus, $f(x + \Delta x) \approx f(x) + f'(x)\Delta x$.

3.1 Error Calculation Formulas

  • Absolute Error: $\Delta x$ (or $dx$)
  • Relative Error: $\frac{\Delta x}{x}$
  • Percentage Error: $\frac{\Delta x}{x} \times 100\%$

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Apr 5 Shift 2)

Question: If the radius of a sphere is measured as $9 \text{ cm}$ with an error of $0.03 \text{ cm}$, then calculate the approximate error in calculating its surface area. (Use $\pi \approx 3.14$)

Solution:

Surface Area of sphere $S = 4\pi r^2$.

Given: $r = 9 \text{ cm}$, Error $\Delta r = 0.03 \text{ cm}$.

Differentiate $S$ w.r.t $r$:

\[\frac{dS}{dr} = 8\pi r\]

Approximate error $\Delta S \approx dS = \frac{dS}{dr} \Delta r$:

\[\Delta S \approx 8\pi r \Delta r = 8 \times 3.14 \times 9 \times 0.03\]
\[\Delta S \approx 8 \times 3.14 \times 0.27 = 6.7824 \text{ cm}^2\]
\[\Delta S \approx 6.78 \text{ cm}^2\]

Answer: $6.78 \text{ cm}^2$

4

Increasing and Decreasing Functions

A function is said to be monotonic in an interval if it is either entirely increasing or entirely decreasing in that interval. The sign of the first derivative $\frac{dy}{dx}$ determines the monotonicity.

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ Topic — Intervals of Monotonicity

Let $f(x)$ be differentiable on $(a, b)$.

  • Strictly Increasing: $f'(x) > 0$ for all $x \in (a, b)$
  • Strictly Decreasing: $f'(x) < 0$ for all $x \in (a, b)$
  • Monotonically Increasing (Non-decreasing): $f'(x) \geq 0$
  • Monotonically Decreasing (Non-increasing): $f'(x) \leq 0$

4.1 Critical Points

Points where $f'(x) = 0$ or $f'(x)$ is undefined are called critical points. These points divide the domain into intervals of increasing and decreasing behavior.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Jan 30 Shift 2)

Question: The function $f(x) = \frac{x}{\ln x}$ is strictly decreasing in the interval:

(a) $(0, 1) \cup (1, e)$

(b) $(e, \infty)$

(c) $(1, e)$

(d) $(0, e)$

Solution:

$f(x) = \frac{x}{\ln x}$. Domain: $x > 0, x \neq 1$.

Find $f'(x)$ using quotient rule:

\[f'(x) = \frac{1 \cdot \ln x - x \cdot (1/x)}{(\ln x)^2} = \frac{\ln x - 1}{(\ln x)^2}\]

For $f(x)$ to be strictly decreasing, $f'(x) < 0$:

\[\frac{\ln x - 1}{(\ln x)^2} < 0\]

Since denominator $(\ln x)^2 > 0$ (for $x \neq 1$), we need $\ln x - 1 < 0$:

\[\ln x < 1 \implies x < e\]

Also $x>0$ and $x \neq 1$. So the intervals are $(0, 1) \cup (1, e)$.

\[(0, 1) \cup (1, e)\]

Answer: (a) $(0, 1) \cup (1, e)$

5

Maxima and Minima

Maxima and minima are the largest and smallest values of a function within a given range. We study local (relative) and absolute (global) extrema.

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ Topic — Second Derivative Test

Let $f(x)$ be differentiable. Find $x$ where $f'(x) = 0$ (critical point $c$). Then check $f''(c)$:

  • Local Maximum: $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) < 0$
  • Local Minimum: $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) > 0$
  • Point of Inflection / Test Fails: $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) = 0$ (use first derivative test)

5.1 First Derivative Test

At critical point $c$:

  • If $f'(x)$ changes sign from positive to negative as $x$ passes through $c$, then $c$ is a point of Local Maximum.
  • If $f'(x)$ changes sign from negative to positive as $x$ passes through $c$, then $c$ is a point of Local Minimum.
  • If $f'(x)$ does not change sign, $c$ is neither max nor min.

5.2 Absolute Extrema in Closed Interval

💡 How to find Absolute Max/Min on $[a, b]$

  • Step 1: Find all critical points $f'(x) = 0$ inside $(a, b)$.
  • Step 2: Evaluate $f(x)$ at these critical points.
  • Step 3: Evaluate $f(a)$ and $f(b)$.
  • Step 4: Largest value is Absolute Max, smallest is Absolute Min.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023 (Apr 13 Shift 1)

Question: The sum of the absolute maximum and absolute minimum values of the function $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 2$ in the interval $[0, 2]$ is:

Solution:

Given $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 2$ on $[0, 2]$.

Find $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3$.

Set $f'(x) = 0 \implies 3x^2 - 3 = 0 \implies x^2 = 1 \implies x = 1, -1$.

Critical point in $(0, 2)$ is $x = 1$. (We discard $x = -1$).

Evaluate $f(x)$ at critical point and endpoints:

\[f(1) = (1)^3 - 3(1) + 2 = 1 - 3 + 2 = 0\]
\[f(0) = (0)^3 - 3(0) + 2 = 2\]
\[f(2) = (2)^3 - 3(2) + 2 = 8 - 6 + 2 = 4\]

Absolute Maximum = $4$ (at $x=2$)

Absolute Minimum = $0$ (at $x=1$)

Sum = $4 + 0 = 4$.

\[4\]

Answer: 4

Rolle's & Mean Value Theorem

These theorems establish a relationship between the values of a function at its endpoints and the value of its derivative inside the interval.

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ Topic — Verifying Conditions of Rolle's Theorem

Rolle's Theorem: If a function $f(x)$ is:

  • Continuous on closed interval $[a, b]$
  • Differentiable on open interval $(a, b)$
  • $f(a) = f(b)$

Then there exists at least one point $c \in (a, b)$ such that $f'(c) = 0$.

6.1 Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem (MVT)

If $f(x)$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$, then there exists at least one $c \in (a, b)$ such that:

\[f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}\]

Geometrically, there is a tangent at $c$ that is parallel to the chord joining $(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2021 (Aug 27 Shift 1)

Question: If Rolle's theorem holds for the function $f(x) = x^3 - ax^2 + bx - 4$ in $[1, 2]$ with $f'\left(\frac{4}{3}\right) = 0$, then find the values of $a$ and $b$.

Solution:

Since Rolle's Theorem holds, $f(1) = f(2)$.

\[f(1) = (1)^3 - a(1)^2 + b(1) - 4 = 1 - a + b - 4 = -a + b - 3\]
\[f(2) = (2)^3 - a(2)^2 + b(2) - 4 = 8 - 4a + 2b - 4 = -4a + 2b + 4\]

Equating $f(1)$ and $f(2)$:

\[-a + b - 3 = -4a + 2b + 4 \implies 3a - b = 7 \implies b = 3a - 7\]

Given $f'\left(\frac{4}{3}\right) = 0$. Find $f'(x)$:

\[f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2ax + b\]
\[3\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^2 - 2a\left(\frac{4}{3}\right) + b = 0 \implies 3\left(\frac{16}{9}\right) - \frac{8a}{3} + b = 0\]
\[\frac{16}{3} - \frac{8a}{3} + b = 0 \implies 16 - 8a + 3b = 0\]

Substitute $b = 3a - 7$:

\[16 - 8a + 3(3a - 7) = 0 \implies 16 - 8a + 9a - 21 = 0 \implies a - 5 = 0 \implies a = 5\]
\[b = 3(5) - 7 = 15 - 7 = 8\]
\[a = 5, b = 8\]

Answer: $a = 5, b = 8$

Optimization & Word Problems

These involve finding the absolute maximum or minimum values of a function representing a physical quantity (like area, volume, cost, distance) to optimize a real-world scenario.

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ Topic — Geometry Based Optimization

Steps to solve:

  • Step 1: Draw a figure and label variables.
  • Step 2: Write the quantity to be minimized/maximized as a function of one variable.
  • Step 3: Differentiate, set to $0$, and verify max/min using second derivative test.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2022 (June 28 Shift 1)

Question: A wire of length $36 \text{ m}$ is cut into two pieces. One of the pieces is bent in the form of a square and the other in the form of an equilateral triangle. Find the length of the side of the square that minimizes the total area.

Solution:

Let the side of square be $x$ and side of triangle be $y$.

Perimeter of square = $4x$, Perimeter of triangle = $3y$.

Total wire: $4x + 3y = 36 \implies 3y = 36 - 4x \implies y = 12 - \frac{4x}{3}$.

Total Area $A = x^2 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}y^2$.

\[A(x) = x^2 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\left(12 - \frac{4x}{3}\right)^2\]

Differentiate $A$ w.r.t $x$:

\[A'(x) = 2x + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 2\left(12 - \frac{4x}{3}\right)\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = 2x - \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}\left(12 - \frac{4x}{3}\right)\]

Set $A'(x) = 0$ for minima:

\[2x = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}\left(12 - \frac{4x}{3}\right) \implies x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\left(12 - \frac{4x}{3}\right)\]
\[3x = \sqrt{3}\left(12 - \frac{4x}{3}\right) \implies 3x = 12\sqrt{3} - \frac{4\sqrt{3}x}{3}\]
\[9x = 36\sqrt{3} - 4\sqrt{3}x \implies x(9 + 4\sqrt{3}) = 36\sqrt{3} \implies x = \frac{36\sqrt{3}}{9 + 4\sqrt{3}}\]

Rationalizing the denominator:

\[x = \frac{36\sqrt{3}(9 - 4\sqrt{3})}{81 - 48} = \frac{36\sqrt{3}(9 - 4\sqrt{3})}{33} = \frac{12\sqrt{3}(9 - 4\sqrt{3})}{11}\]

Wait, checking calculations. Let's simplify $A'(x) = 2x - 8\sqrt{3} + \frac{8\sqrt{3}x}{9} = 0$.

\[x\left(2 + \frac{8\sqrt{3}}{9}\right) = 8\sqrt{3} \implies x\left(\frac{18 + 8\sqrt{3}}{9}\right) = 8\sqrt{3}\]
\[x = \frac{72\sqrt{3}}{18 + 8\sqrt{3}} = \frac{36\sqrt{3}}{9 + 4\sqrt{3}} = \frac{36\sqrt{3}(9 - 4\sqrt{3})}{81 - 48} = \frac{36\sqrt{3}(9 - 4\sqrt{3})}{33} = \frac{12\sqrt{3}(9 - 4\sqrt{3})}{11}\]
\[x = \frac{36\sqrt{3}}{9 + 4\sqrt{3}} \text{ m}\]

Answer: $\frac{36\sqrt{3}}{9 + 4\sqrt{3}} \text{ m}$

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the slope of the tangent to a curve at a point?

The slope of the tangent to the curve y = f(x) at point (x1, y1) is given by the value of the derivative at that point, i.e., m = f'(x1) = dy/dx evaluated at x = x1.

What is the condition for a function to be strictly increasing?

A function f(x) is strictly increasing in an interval if its derivative f'(x) > 0 for all x in that interval. If f'(x) ≥ 0, it is monotonically increasing (non-decreasing).

What are the conditions for Rolle's Theorem?

For Rolle's Theorem to hold, the function must be continuous on [a, b], differentiable on (a, b), and f(a) must equal f(b). Then there is at least one c in (a, b) where f'(c) = 0.
