1

Classification of Hydrocarbons & Alkanes

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds composed exclusively of carbon and hydrogen. They are the simplest organic compounds and serve as the parent chain for naming all organic molecules. Based on structure, they are classified as Open-chain (Aliphatic) and Closed-chain (Cyclic/Aromatic). Aliphatic hydrocarbons are further divided into alkanes (saturated), alkenes, and alkynes (unsaturated).

1.1 Alkanes: Preparation & Properties

⭐ Most Repeated PYQ: Wurtz Reaction & Decarboxylation

1. Wurtz Reaction: Two molecules of alkyl halide react with sodium in dry ether to form a higher alkane.

\[R-X + 2Na + X-R \xrightarrow{dry\ ether} R-R + 2NaX\]

Limitation: Always gives a mixture of alkanes if two different alkyl halides are used. Best for preparing symmetrical alkanes with an even number of carbons.

2. Decarboxylation (Duma Reaction): Sodium salt of carboxylic acid heated with soda lime (NaOH + CaO).

\[RCOONa + NaOH(CaO) \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-H + Na_2CO_3\]

1.2 Chemical Properties of Alkanes (Halogenation)

Alkanes are generally unreactive due to strong C-C and C-H bonds and non-polarity. However, they undergo free radical substitution with halogens ($Cl_2, Br_2$) in the presence of UV light or heat.

Free Radical Halogenation Mechanism

Step 1: Initiation (Homolytic fission)

\[Cl-Cl \xrightarrow{hv} Cl^\bullet + Cl^\bullet\]

Step 2: Propagation

\[Cl^\bullet + CH_4 \rightarrow HCl + \bullet CH_3\]
\[\bullet CH_3 + Cl_2 \rightarrow CH_3Cl + Cl^\bullet\]

Step 3: Termination

\[Cl^\bullet + Cl^\bullet \rightarrow Cl_2\]

⚠️ JEE Alert: Reactivity vs Selectivity

  • Order of Reactivity of Halogens: $F_2 > Cl_2 > Br_2 > I_2$
  • Order of Reactivity of H-atoms: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$ (due to stability of free radicals)
  • Selectivity Order: $Br_2 > Cl_2 > F_2$ (Bromine is highly selective for $3^\circ$ H, Chlorine gives a mixture based on statistical probability).

1.3 Conformational Isomerism

Conformations are different spatial arrangements of the same molecule obtained by rotation around a C-C single bond. Represented using Sawhorse and Newman projections.

🔥 High Weightage: Ethane Conformations

Staggered Conformation

  • Dihedral angle = 60°
  • Minimum repulsion (torsional strain)
  • Most stable conformation
  • Minimum potential energy

Eclipsed Conformation

  • Dihedral angle = 0°
  • Maximum repulsion (torsional strain)
  • Least stable conformation
  • Maximum potential energy

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024

Question: The major product formed in the photochemical chlorination of 2-methylbutane is:

(a) 1-chloro-2-methylbutane

(b) 2-chloro-2-methylbutane

(c) 2-chloro-3-methylbutane

(d) 1-chloro-3-methylbutane

Solution:

2-methylbutane structure: $CH_3-CH(CH_3)-CH_2-CH_3$

Types of hydrogens available for abstraction:

  • Four $1^\circ$ H on terminal $CH_3$ groups
  • Two $1^\circ$ H on $CH_2$
  • One $3^\circ$ H on the $CH$ group

Reactivity order: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$

Chlorination favors the $3^\circ$ hydrogen because the resulting $3^\circ$ free radical is highly stabilized by hyperconjugation and inductive effects.

Abstraction of the $3^\circ$ H forms a $3^\circ$ radical, which then reacts with $Cl_2$ to give 2-chloro-2-methylbutane.

\[\text{Major Product: 2-chloro-2-methylbutane}\]

Answer: (b)

Alkenes & Electrophilic Addition Reactions

Alkenes (general formula $C_nH_{2n}$) contain a carbon-carbon double bond ($C=C$), consisting of one strong sigma ($\sigma$) bond and one weaker pi ($\pi$) bond. The $\pi$ electrons make alkenes nucleophilic, highly susceptible to electrophilic addition reactions.

2.1 Preparation: Saytzeff & Hofmann Rules

⭐ Crucial Concept: Elimination Reactions

1. Saytzeff Rule (General Rule): In dehydrohalogenation ($E2$) of alkyl halides or dehydration of alcohols, the more substituted (more stable) alkene is the major product.

\[CH_3-CH_2-CH(Br)-CH_3 \xrightarrow{alc.\ KOH} CH_3-CH=CH-CH_3 \ (\text{Major, But-2-ene})\]

2. Hofmann Rule: When a bulky base like $t-BuOK$ (potassium tert-butoxide) is used, the less substituted alkene is the major product due to steric hindrance.

2.2 Markovnikov vs Anti-Markovnikov Addition

Markovnikov's Rule

Addition of $HX$ to unsymmetrical alkene: H adds to the carbon with more H atoms, X adds to the carbon with fewer H atoms.

\[CH_3-CH=CH_2 + HBr \rightarrow CH_3-CH(Br)-CH_3\]

Mechanism: Carbocation intermediate. Stability dictates product: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$.

Anti-Markovnikov (Peroxide Effect / Kharasch Effect)

In presence of peroxide ($R-O-O-R$), $HBr$ adds opposite to Markovnikov. Br adds to carbon with more H atoms.

\[CH_3-CH=CH_2 + HBr \xrightarrow{Peroxide} CH_3-CH_2-CH_2Br\]

Mechanism: Free radical. Only works for HBr, not HCl or HI.

⚠️ JEE Trap: Why not HCl and HI in Peroxide Effect?

  • HCl: $H-Cl$ bond is too strong ($431\ kJ/mol$). Homolysis is difficult.
  • HI: $H-I$ bond is weak, so iodine radicals form, but $I^\bullet$ is less reactive and prefers to recombine to $I_2$ rather than attack the alkene. The reaction is also endothermic.
  • HBr: Perfect bond strength and exothermic propagation steps.

2.3 Ozonolysis: Structure Determination

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ: Deduce Alkene from Ozonolysis

Ozone ($O_3$) cleaves the $C=C$ double bond to form carbonyl compounds (aldehydes, ketones, or carboxylic acids depending on conditions).

  • Reductive ($Zn/H_2O$ or $Me_2S$): Alkene → Aldehydes / Ketones
  • Oxidative ($H_2O_2$): Alkene → Carboxylic Acids / Ketones

Trick: Join the carbonyl carbons by removing the oxygens and forming a $C=C$ bond!

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023

Question: An unsaturated hydrocarbon 'A' on reductive ozonolysis gives two moles of ethanal and one mole of propanedial. The structure of 'A' is:

(a) $CH_3-CH=CH-CH_3$

(b) $CH_2=CH-CH_2-CH=CH_2$

(c) $CH_3-CH=CH-CH_2-CH=CH-CH_3$

(d) $CH_2=CH-CH=CH-CH_2-CH_3$

Solution:

Products given:

  • 2 moles of Ethanal ($CH_3CHO$)
  • 1 mole of Propanedial ($OHC-CH_2-CHO$)

This means 3 fragments were produced, indicating TWO double bonds in the original molecule (since each $C=C$ breaks into 2 fragments, 2 double bonds give 3 fragments if the molecule is continuous).

Mapping fragments to the alkene structure:

1. $CH_3CHO$ comes from a $=CH-CH_3$ terminal group.

2. $OHC-CH_2-CHO$ comes from an internal $=CH-CH_2-CH=$ group.

3. Another $CH_3CHO$ comes from another $=CH-CH_3$ terminal group.

Joining these fragments by their double bonds (replacing $=O$ with $=C$):

\[CH_3-CH=CH-CH_2-CH=CH-CH_3\]

2,4-Heptadiene

Answer: (c)

3

Alkynes & Acidity of Terminal Alkynes

Alkynes (general formula $C_nH_{2n-2}$) contain a carbon-carbon triple bond ($C \equiv C$), consisting of one $\sigma$ bond and two $\pi$ bonds. The carbon atoms are sp hybridized, making the bond short and strong.

3.1 Preparation of Alkynes

Common Preparation Methods

1. From Calcium Carbide (Industrial):

\[CaC_2 + 2H_2O \rightarrow Ca(OH)_2 + C_2H_2\]

2. Dehydrohalogenation of Vicinal Dihalides:

\[CH_2Br-CH_2Br + 2NaNH_2 \rightarrow CH \equiv CH + 2NaBr + 2NH_3\]

3.2 Acidic Nature of Terminal Alkynes

⭐ Most Important: Why is Terminal Alkyne Acidic?

In terminal alkynes ($RC \equiv CH$), the carbon is sp hybridized, having 50% s-character. High s-character means electrons are held closer to the nucleus, making the carbon highly electronegative. This facilitates the release of $H^+$ ions.

\[ \% \ s\text{-character}: sp(50\%) > sp^2(33\%) > sp^3(25\%)\] \[ \text{Acidity: } HC \equiv CH > H_2C=CH_2 > CH_3-CH_3\]

💡 Chemical Tests for Terminal Alkynes

Terminal alkynes react with certain metal salts to form precipitates, distinguishing them from alkenes and internal alkynes:

  • Tollens' Reagent: $[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+ \rightarrow$ White precipitate of Silver Acetylide ($AgC \equiv CA$)
  • Ammoniacal $Cu_2Cl_2$: Red precipitate of Copper Acetylide ($CuC \equiv CCu$)

3.3 Hydration of Alkynes (Kucherov's Reaction)

In the presence of dilute $H_2SO_4$ and $HgSO_4$ catalyst, alkynes add one molecule of water to form an enol, which rapidly tautomerizes to a ketone.

Keto-Enol Tautomerism

\[CH \equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{HgSO_4/H_2SO_4} [CH_2=CH-OH] \rightarrow CH_3CHO\]

Acetylene gives Acetaldehyde (only exception giving an aldehyde).

\[CH_3-C \equiv CH + H_2O \rightarrow [CH_3-C(OH)=CH_2] \rightarrow CH_3-CO-CH_3\]

Propyne gives Acetone (Markovnikov hydration).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024

Question: Which of the following compounds will give a white precipitate with ammoniacal silver nitrate solution?

(a) But-1-yne

(b) But-2-yne

(c) But-1-ene

(d) Butane

Solution:

Ammoniacal silver nitrate (Tollens' reagent) reacts only with terminal alkynes (compounds containing an acidic $-C \equiv C-H$ bond).

(a) But-1-yne: $CH \equiv C-CH_2-CH_3$ — contains a terminal $C \equiv C-H$ bond. Reacts → White ppt of $AgC \equiv C-CH_2-CH_3$.

(b) But-2-yne: $CH_3-C \equiv C-CH_3$ — internal alkyne, no acidic H on triple bond carbon.

(c) But-1-ene & (d) Butane: No terminal alkyne H.

\[\text{But-1-yne gives the white precipitate}\]

Answer: (a)

4

Aromatic Hydrocarbons (Benzene)

Aromatic hydrocarbons (Arenes) contain carbon and hydrogen and are characterized by a special cyclic structure, most famously benzene ($C_6H_6$). Benzene is a planar, regular hexagon where all six carbons are sp² hybridized, forming a continuous $\pi$ electron cloud above and below the ring.

4.1 Structure & Resonance Energy

⭐ Important Concept: Resonance Energy

Benzene is often represented as a hybrid of two Kekulé structures. Due to this delocalization of $\pi$ electrons, benzene is more stable than a hypothetical 1,3,5-cyclohexatriene.

The difference between the theoretical and experimental enthalpy of hydrogenation is the Resonance Energy (~$150.4\ kJ/mol$).

4.2 Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS)

Because the $\pi$ electron cloud is electron-rich, benzene acts as a nucleophile and undergoes Electrophilic Substitution rather than addition, thereby preserving its stable aromatic ring.

Major EAS Reactions of Benzene

  • Nitration: Conc. $HNO_3$ + Conc. $H_2SO_4$ (50°C) → Nitrobenzene ($C_6H_5NO_2$). Electrophile: $NO_2^+$
  • Halogenation: $X_2$ + $FeX_3$ (Lewis acid) → Halobenzene. Electrophile: $X^+$
  • Sulphonation: Conc. $H_2SO_4$ + $SO_3$ → Benzene sulfonic acid. Electrophile: $SO_3$
  • Friedel-Crafts Alkylation: $R-Cl$ + Anhyd. $AlCl_3$ → Alkylbenzene. Electrophile: $R^+$
  • Friedel-Crafts Acylation: $RCOCl$ + Anhyd. $AlCl_3$ → Acylbenzene. Electrophile: $RCO^+$

⚠️ JEE Trap: Limitations of Friedel-Crafts Reactions

  • Fails when the benzene ring contains a strongly deactivating group (e.g., $-NO_2, -CN, -CHO$).
  • Fails when the ring has a basic group (e.g., $-NH_2, -NHR, -NR_2$), because the Lewis acid $AlCl_3$ complexes with the lone pair of Nitrogen, deactivating the ring heavily and making it positively charged.
  • Alkylation often leads to polyalkylation because the alkyl group activates the ring. Acylation does not (because $-COR$ is deactivating).
  • Rearrangement of carbocations can occur in alkylation (e.g., $n$-propyl chloride gives isopropyl benzene).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024

Question: Friedel-Crafts acylation of anisole with acetyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous $AlCl_3$ gives:

(a) 2-Methoxyacetophenone

(b) 4-Methoxyacetophenone

(c) 3-Methoxyacetophenone

(d) No reaction (catalyst poisoning)

Solution:

Anisole is $C_6H_5OCH_3$. The $-OCH_3$ group is an activating, ortho/para-directing group due to its lone pairs participating in resonance (+R effect).

While $-OCH_3$ is an activating group (unlike $-NH_2$ which poisons the catalyst), the reaction proceeds smoothly.

Because the $-OCH_3$ group is bulky, the para product is the major product due to steric hindrance at the ortho positions.

\[\text{Major Product: 4-Methoxyacetophenone}\]

Answer: (b)

Directive Influence of Substituents

When a substituted benzene undergoes Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS), the existing substituent determines the position (ortho, meta, or para) and the rate of the incoming electrophile. This is called Directive Influence.

5.1 Classification of Directing Groups

TypeDirects toEffect on RateExamples
Strongly Activatingo, pHighly increases$-NH_2, -NHR, -NR_2, -OH$
Moderately Activatingo, pIncreases$-OCH_3, -NHCOCH_3$
Weakly Activatingo, pSlightly increases$-CH_3, -C_2H_5$ (alkyl)
Weakly Deactivatingo, pSlightly decreasesHalogens ($-F, -Cl, -Br, -I$)
Moderately DeactivatingmDecreases$-CN, -SO_3H, -COOH, -CHO$
Strongly DeactivatingmHighly decreases$-NO_2, -NR_3^+$

🔥 JEE Favorite: The Halogen Exception

Halogens are deactivating because they are highly electronegative and pull electron density away from the ring (-I effect dominates). However, they are o, p-directing because they have lone pairs that participate in resonance (+R effect) to stabilize the intermediate arenium ion at o and p positions.

5.2 Mechanism of Directing Effects

Ortho/Para Directors: Stabilize the carbocation intermediate via resonance (+R effect) or hyperconjugation (+I / Hyperconjugation). The positive charge in the arenium ion falls on the carbon directly attached to the substituent, which is stabilized by the lone pair donation.

Meta Directors: Contain a highly electronegative atom attached to the ring carbon via a multiple bond (e.g., $C=O, N=O$). They withdraw electrons (-R / -I effect). At ortho and para positions, the positive charge in the intermediate falls on the carbon attached to the electron-withdrawing group, making it highly unstable. At the meta position, this destabilizing resonance is avoided.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023

Question: The major product of nitration of benzoic acid is:

(a) 2-Nitrobenzoic acid

(b) 3-Nitrobenzoic acid

(c) 4-Nitrobenzoic acid

(d) Equal mixture of 2- and 4-nitrobenzoic acid

Solution:

Benzoic acid ($C_6H_5COOH$) has a $-COOH$ group attached to the benzene ring.

The $-COOH$ group is electron-withdrawing due to the $-R$ (resonance) effect of the $C=O$ bond.

It is a meta-directing group.

Therefore, the incoming electrophile ($NO_2^+$) will preferentially attack the meta position.

\[\text{Major Product: 3-Nitrobenzoic acid}\]

Answer: (b)

⚠️ Common Mistakes in Aromaticity

  • Forgetting that halogens are deactivating but o/p-directing.
  • Confusing Friedel-Crafts failure with aniline: $-NH_2$ coordinates with $AlCl_3$, so no reaction happens.
  • Saying $-N(CH_3)_2$ is meta-directing (it is strongly o/p-directing due to +R effect of N).

Key Mechanisms & Name Reactions

Organic chemistry in JEE relies heavily on understanding the "why" behind reactions. Here is a summary of the most tested mechanisms and name reactions in Hydrocarbons.

1. Kolbe's Electrolysis (Preparation of Alkanes)

Electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium salt of carboxylic acid gives alkane at the anode.

\[2RCOONa + 2H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{electrolysis}} R-R + 2CO_2 + 2NaOH + H_2\]

Note: Produces symmetrical alkanes with an even number of carbons.

2. Hydroboration-Oxidation (Anti-Markovnikov Hydration)

Alkene reacts with $BH_3$ (or $B_2H_6$), then $H_2O_2/OH^-$.

\[CH_3-CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{1. BH_3} \xrightarrow{2. H_2O_2/OH^-} CH_3-CH_2-CH_2OH\]

Key Features: Syn addition (H and OH add from same side), gives Anti-Markovnikov alcohol. No carbocation rearrangement.

3. Oxymercuration-Demercuration (Markovnikov Hydration)

Alkene + $Hg(OAc)_2/H_2O$, then $NaBH_4/NaOH$.

\[CH_3-CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{1. Hg(OAc)_2/H_2O} \xrightarrow{2. NaBH_4/NaOH} CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3\]

Key Features: Markovnikov addition of $H_2O$ without rearrangement because no free carbocation is formed (cyclic mercurinium ion intermediate).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2022

Question: Which of the following reagents will convert propene to 1-propanol without carbocation rearrangement?

(a) Dil. $H_2SO_4$ then $H_2O$

(b) $BH_3$ then $H_2O_2/OH^-$

(c) $Hg(OAc)_2/H_2O$ then $NaBH_4$

(d) $HBr$ then $aq.\ KOH$

Solution:

Target: Propene to 1-propanol ($CH_3CH_2CH_2OH$).

1-propanol is the Anti-Markovnikov addition product of water.

(a) Acid-catalyzed hydration gives Markovnikov product (2-propanol) and can rearrange.

(c) Oxymercuration gives Markovnikov product (2-propanol) without rearrangement, but we need 1-propanol.

(d) Markovnikov addition of HBr, then substitution gives 2-propanol.

(b) Hydroboration-oxidation gives Anti-Markovnikov addition (1-propanol) with syn addition and no rearrangement.

\[\text{Reagent: } BH_3 \text{ then } H_2O_2/OH^-\]

Answer: (b)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Markovnikov's rule?

Markovnikov's rule states that when an unsymmetrical reagent (like HX) adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, the negative part of the reagent attaches to the carbon atom with fewer hydrogen atoms (the more substituted carbon).

What is the Peroxide Effect (Kharasch Effect)?

The peroxide effect or anti-Markovnikov addition occurs when HBr adds to an unsymmetrical alkene in the presence of peroxide. The negative part (Br) attaches to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms. This happens via a free radical mechanism and only works with HBr, not HCl or HI.

Why are terminal alkynes acidic in nature?

Terminal alkynes have an sp-hybridized carbon with 50% s-character. High s-character means electrons are closer to the nucleus, making the carbon highly electronegative. This facilitates the release of H+ ions, making them acidic (pKa ~25).

What is Saytzeff's rule?

Saytzeff's rule states that in dehydrohalogenation or dehydration reactions, the preferred product is the alkene that is more highly substituted (more stable), as it is formed faster due to hyperconjugation.

What are ortho/para directing groups?

Groups that donate electrons to the benzene ring (activating groups) like -OH, -NH2, -CH3 direct incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions. Halogens are an exception: they are deactivating but o/p directing due to lone pair resonance.

📐 Must-Remember Concepts & Values

Addition Rules

Markovnikov: Negative part to less H-rich C

Anti-Mark: Negative part to more H-rich C (Peroxide, only HBr)

Saytzeff: More substituted alkene (E2)

Hofmann: Less substituted alkene (Bulky base)

Acidity & Hybridization

sp (50%) > sp2 (33%) > sp3 (25%)

Ethyne > Ethene > Ethane

Terminal alkyne H is acidic (pKa ~25)

Aromatic Directives

o,p-directing: -OH, -NH2, -X, -R

m-directing: -NO2, -CN, -CHO, -COOH

Halogens: Deactivating but o,p-directing

⚡ 5-Step Exam Strategy

  1. 1
    For Addition reactions: Always check if the reagent contains peroxide. If yes, apply Anti-Markovnikov (only for HBr).
  2. 2
    For Elimination: Check the base. Bulkier base (t-BuOK) = Hofmann product. Normal base (alc. KOH) = Saytzeff product.
  3. 3
    For Aromatic Substitution: Map the existing group to o/p or m. Draw all positions and check for steric hindrance (para preferred if bulky).
  4. 4
    For Ozonolysis: Count the fragments. If an aldehyde is formed, the original carbon had one H. If a ketone, it had none. If CO2, it was a terminal =CH2.
  5. 5
    For Conversions: Remember that alkynes can be selectively reduced to cis (Lindlar) or trans (Na/liq NH3) alkenes.

Hydrocarbons — Complete JEE Study Guide

Hydrocarbons is a critical chapter in JEE Chemistry that covers the structure, preparation, properties, and reactions of organic compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen. It is divided into Alkanes, Alkenes, Alkynes, and Aromatic Hydrocarbons. This chapter forms the bedrock of organic chemistry mechanisms tested in JEE Main and Advanced.

The most important topics for JEE from this chapter are Markovnikov and Anti-Markovnikov additions (specifically the peroxide effect limited to HBr), Saytzeff and Hofmann elimination rules, ozonolysis mapping (deducing the original alkene structure from carbonyl products), acidity of terminal alkynes based on sp hybridization, and the directive influence of substituents in Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS).

Students should focus on understanding the "why" behind the rules. For instance, bromine is more selective than chlorine in free radical halogenation because the H-abstraction step by Br• is endothermic (highly sensitive to bond dissociation energy and radical stability), whereas Cl• abstraction is exothermic (less selective, driven by statistical probability). Similarly, halogens are deactivating yet ortho/para-directing because their strong -I effect withdraws electrons, but their +R effect donates lone pairs to stabilize the arenium ion intermediate.

For JEE Advanced, focus heavily on multi-step synthesis, identifying compounds based on chemical tests (like terminal alkynes with Tollens' reagent), and predicting carbocation rearrangements during hydration and Friedel-Crafts reactions. Mastering the reaction mechanisms—rather than rote memorizing products—is the key to solving complex organic chemistry problems in JEE.
