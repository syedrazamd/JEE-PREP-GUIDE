Classification & Nomenclature

Alcohols, phenols and ethers are oxygen-containing organic compounds. Alcohols (R–OH) and phenols (Ar–OH) contain –OH group, while ethers (R–O–R') contain –O– between two carbon groups. The classification and nomenclature form the foundation for understanding the entire chapter.

1.1 Classification of Alcohols

⭐ Foundation for Lucas Test & Oxidation Reactions

Based on the carbon bearing the –OH group:

Primary (1°)

R–CH₂–OH

–OH on a carbon attached to 1 carbon (and 2 H)

e.g., CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol)

Secondary (2°)

R₂CH–OH

–OH on a carbon attached to 2 carbons (and 1 H)

e.g., (CH₃)₂CHOH (isopropanol)

Tertiary (3°)

R₃C–OH

–OH on a carbon attached to 3 carbons (and 0 H)

e.g., (CH₃)₃COH (tert-butanol)

Special Classifications

Allylic Alcohol:

–OH on carbon adjacent to C=C double bond

e.g., CH₂=CH–CH₂–OH (prop-2-en-1-ol, allyl alcohol)

Benzylic Alcohol:

–OH on carbon attached to benzene ring

e.g., C₆H₅–CH₂–OH (phenylmethanol, benzyl alcohol)

Vicinal, Geminal Diols:

Vicinal (1,2-diol): HO–CH₂–CH₂–OH · Geminal (gem-diol): CH₂(OH)₂

1.2 IUPAC Nomenclature Rules

⭐ Important — Directly Tested in JEE

  1. Select the longest carbon chain containing the –OH group
  2. Number the chain such that the –OH carbon gets the lowest possible number
  3. Suffix: "–ol" (replace final "e" of alkane with –ol)
  4. For multiple –OH groups: –diol, –triol, etc. (keep the "e")
  5. For phenols: –OH gets position 1 by default on the benzene ring

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024

Question: The IUPAC name of the following compound is:
(CH₃)₂CH–CH₂–CH(OH)–CH₃

(a) 4-Methylpentan-2-ol    (b) 2-Methylpentan-4-ol

(c) 4-Methylpentan-3-ol    (d) 2-Methylpentan-2-ol

Solution:

Step 1: Find the longest chain containing –OH

Structure: CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH(OH)–CH₃

Step 2: Longest chain has 5 carbons (pentane)

Step 3: Number from the end giving –OH lowest locant

From right: –OH gets position 2 (lower); methyl branch gets position 4

Step 4: Name = 4-methylpentan-2-ol

\[\text{Answer: 4-Methylpentan-2-ol}\]

Answer: (a) 4-Methylpentan-2-ol

Structure & Physical Properties

Understanding the structure (hybridization, bond angles) of alcohols, phenols and ethers explains their physical properties like boiling points and solubility. The hydrogen bonding capability of –OH is the key factor.

2.1 Structure of Alcohols

⭐ Important Bond Angle Concept

In alcohols, the oxygen atom is sp³ hybridized with two bond pairs (C–O, O–H) and two lone pairs.

  • Ideal tetrahedral angle = 109.5°
  • Actual C–O–H angle in methanol = 108.9° (less than 109.5° due to lone pair repulsion)
  • C–O bond length ≈ 1.43 Å; O–H bond length ≈ 0.96 Å

2.2 Structure of Phenol

🔥 Most Repeated Concept — Resonance in Phenol

In phenol, oxygen is sp²-like due to resonance — the lone pair on oxygen delocalizes into the benzene ring:

  • C–O bond has partial double-bond character (shorter, stronger ~1.36 Å)
  • C–O–H angle ≈ 109° (still sp³ on oxygen, but with conjugation)
  • This resonance makes C–O bond in phenol stronger than in alcohol
  • Consequence: Phenol does NOT undergo C–O cleavage reactions easily (no HX, no HCl/ZnCl₂ reaction)
  • Also explains acidic nature — phenoxide ion is resonance stabilized

2.3 Boiling Points — Hydrogen Bonding

⭐ High PYQ Frequency — BP Comparison

Alcohols have much higher BP than corresponding alkanes, alkenes, ethers and alkyl halides due to intermolecular H-bonding.

\[\text{BP order: Alcohol > Amine > Ether > Alkane (similar MW)}\]

Examples (similar MW ≈ 46):

  • CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol, MW=46) — BP = 351 K
  • CH₃OCH₃ (dimethyl ether, MW=46) — BP = 248 K
  • CH₃CH₃ (ethane, MW=30) — BP = 184 K

BP order of isomeric alcohols: 1° > 2° > 3°

(More branching → less surface area → weaker van der Waals → lower BP)

2.4 Solubility in Water

Solubility Rules

Alcohols are soluble in water due to H-bonding with water. As the alkyl group grows larger, solubility decreases (hydrophobic effect dominates).

  • Lower alcohols (methanol, ethanol, propanol) — miscible with water
  • Butanol — slightly soluble (7.9 g/100 mL)
  • Pentanol onwards — insoluble in water
  • Among isomeric alcohols: 3° > 2° > 1° (more branching → smaller surface area → more soluble)
  • Solubility order: CH₃OH > C₂H₅OH > n-C₃H₇OH > n-C₄H₉OH

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2022

Question: The correct order of boiling points of isomeric pentanols (C₅H₁₁OH) is:

(a) 1° > 2° > 3°    (b) 3° > 2° > 1°

(c) 2° > 1° > 3°    (d) 1° > 3° > 2°

Solution:

Boiling point depends on the strength of intermolecular forces.

All isomeric alcohols can form H-bonds (all have –OH).

Difference comes from van der Waals forces which depend on surface area.

1° alcohol (longest, least branched) → largest surface area → highest BP

3° alcohol (most branched) → smallest surface area → lowest BP

\[\text{BP: 1° > 2° > 3°}\]

Pentan-1-ol (138°C) > Pentan-2-ol (119°C) > 2-methylbutan-2-ol (102°C)

Answer: (a) 1° > 2° > 3°

3

Preparation of Alcohols

Alcohols can be prepared from alkenes, carbonyl compounds, carboxylic acids, esters, alkyl halides, and Grignard reagents. Each method has unique stereochemistry and regioselectivity — making this a high-weightage area for JEE.

3.1 From Alkenes — Acid-Catalyzed Hydration

Markovnikov Addition

\[\text{CH}_3\text{CH=CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3\text{CH(OH)CH}_3\]
  • Markovnikov: –OH goes to more substituted carbon
  • Acid used: Dilute H₂SO₄ or H₃PO₄
  • Proceeds via carbocation intermediate → rearrangement possible
  • Anti-Markovnikov not possible directly

3.2 Hydroboration-Oxidation (Anti-Markovnikov)

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ Topic — Hydroboration

\[\text{CH}_3\text{CH=CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{(i) B}_2\text{H}_6, \text{(ii) H}_2\text{O}_2/\text{OH}^-} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}\]
  • Anti-Markovnikov addition: –OH goes to less substituted carbon
  • Syn addition (H and OH add from same side)
  • Reagent: BH₃·THF (or B₂H₆), then H₂O₂/OH⁻
  • No carbocation rearrangement (concerted mechanism)
  • Oxidation step: B is replaced by OH with retention of configuration

💡 Why Hydroboration gives Anti-Markovnikov

Two reasons:

  • Steric: B (large) attaches to less hindered (less substituted) carbon
  • Electronic: B is electrophilic; it bonds to carbon with more H (more electron-rich via hyperconjugation)

3.3 Oxymercuration-Demercuration

\[\text{RCH=CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{(i) Hg(OAc)}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O/THF}} \xrightarrow{\text{(ii) NaBH}_4/\text{OH}^-} \text{RCH(OH)CH}_3\]

Markovnikov addition, NO rearrangement (no free carbocation, cyclic mercurinium ion intermediate), anti addition of H and OH.

3.4 From Carbonyl Compounds (Reduction)

⭐ Important Reducing Agents

To get 1° alcohol

Aldehyde + [H] → 1° alcohol
RCOOH + LiAlH₄ → RCH₂OH
RCOOR' + LiAlH₄ → RCH₂OH + R'OH
RCOCl + LiAlH₄ → RCH₂OH

To get 2° alcohol

Ketone + [H] → 2° alcohol
RCOR' + NaBH₄ → RCH(OH)R'
RCOR' + LiAlH₄ → RCH(OH)R'

⚠️ Critical Difference — NaBH₄ vs LiAlH₄

  • NaBH₄: Reduces only aldehydes and ketones (NOT acids, esters, amides)
  • LiAlH₄: Reduces aldehydes, ketones, acids, esters, amides, acid chlorides
  • NaBH₄ works in methanol/water; LiAlH₄ requires dry ether (reacts violently with water)
  • Both give alcohols after aqueous workup

3.5 Grignard Reagent Method

⭐ Grignard Synthesis — Most Versatile Method

With Formaldehyde (HCHO) → 1° alcohol

\[R\text{MgX} + \text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{ether}} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} R\text{CH}_2\text{OH}\]

With Aldehyde (R'CHO, R'≠H) → 2° alcohol

\[R\text{MgX} + R'\text{CHO} \rightarrow R\text{CH(OH)}R'\]

With Ketone (R'COR'') → 3° alcohol

\[R\text{MgX} + R'\text{COR}'' \rightarrow R\text{C(OH)}R'R''\]

With Ester → 2 equivalents of same alcohol (if R in ester matches)

\[2 R\text{MgX} + R'\text{COOR}'' \rightarrow R'\text{C(OH)}R_2 + R''\text{OH}\]

⚠️ Grignard Reagent Cautions

  • Cannot use substrates with –OH, –NH, –SH, –COOH (acidic H destroys Grignard)
  • Must use dry ether solvent (water protonates RMgX → RH)
  • Cannot have nitro, nitrile groups in same molecule (they react with Grignard)

3.6 From Alkyl Halides

\[R\text{-X} + \text{KOH (aq)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} R\text{-OH} + \text{KX}\]

Nucleophilic substitution (SN2 for 1°, SN1 for 3°). Note: Alkyl halides prefer elimination with alcoholic KOH (giving alkenes).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023

Question: The major product of the following reaction is:
CH₃CH=CH₂ →(i) B₂H₆ →(ii) H₂O₂/OH⁻ → ?

(a) Propan-2-ol    (b) Propan-1-ol

(c) Propanal    (d) Propanoic acid

Solution:

This is hydroboration-oxidation.

B₂H₆ adds to the alkene in anti-Markovnikov fashion (B goes to less substituted carbon).

Oxidation with H₂O₂/OH⁻ replaces B with –OH (with retention).

So, –OH ends up on the terminal carbon (C1) of propene.

\[\text{CH}_3\text{CH=CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{B}_2\text{H}_6, \text{H}_2\text{O}_2/\text{OH}^-} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}\]

Product = propan-1-ol (n-propanol)

Answer: (b) Propan-1-ol

Preparation of Phenols

Phenols can be prepared from benzene derivatives via several methods — cumene process (industrial), Dow process, diazonium hydrolysis (laboratory), and from benzene sulfonic acid. Each method has its own importance and is frequently tested in JEE.

4.1 Cumene Process (Industrial)

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Cumene Process

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{CH=CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{AlCl}_3} \text{Cumene}\] \[\text{Cumene} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{Cumene hydroperoxide}\] \[\text{Cumene hydroperoxide} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{Phenol} + \text{Acetone}\]
  • Benzene + propene → cumene (isopropylbenzene) via Friedel-Crafts alkylation
  • Cumene + O₂ (air) → cumene hydroperoxide (free radical mechanism)
  • Acid cleavage → phenol + acetone (commercially important co-product)
  • This is the industrial method — gives two valuable products

4.2 Dow Process

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{623\text{K}, 300\text{ atm}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}\]

Chlorobenzene + NaOH at high temperature and pressure → sodium phenoxide → phenol. Mechanism involves benzyne intermediate (elimination-addition).

4.3 From Diazonium Salts (Laboratory Method)

⭐ Important — Most Common Lab Method

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{NaNO}_2/\text{HCl}, 273\text{K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-\] \[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}, \Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{N}_2 + \text{HCl}\]

Steps: Aniline → Diazonium chloride (at 0-5°C) → Phenol (on warming with water)

N₂ gas is liberated (white fumes with HCl)

4.4 From Benzene Sulfonic Acid

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH (fusion)}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}\]

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2020

Question: In the cumene process for the manufacture of phenol, the by-product formed is:

(a) Acetaldehyde    (b) Acetic acid

(c) Acetone    (d) Formaldehyde

Solution:

Cumene (isopropylbenzene) is oxidized to cumene hydroperoxide.

On acid-catalyzed rearrangement and cleavage:

\[\text{Cumene hydroperoxide} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{Phenol} + \text{Acetone (CH}_3\text{COCH}_3\text{)}\]

The co-product is acetone (propanone).

\[\text{By-product = Acetone}\]

Answer: (c) Acetone

Preparation of Ethers

Ethers (R–O–R') are mainly prepared by Williamson ether synthesis (most important for JEE) and by dehydration of alcohols. The choice of method depends on whether the ether is symmetric or unsymmetric.

5.1 Williamson Ether Synthesis

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Williamson Synthesis

\[R\text{-X} + R'\text{O}^-\text{Na}^+ \rightarrow R\text{-O-}R' + \text{NaX}\]
  • SN2 mechanism — alkoxide ion attacks alkyl halide
  • Best with primary alkyl halides (1° R-X gives good yield)
  • 2° and 3° alkyl halides give elimination (E2) instead of substitution — major product is alkene
  • Aryl halides do NOT react (C–X bond has partial double bond character)
  • Alkoxide is prepared by: R'OH + Na → R'O⁻Na⁺ + ½H₂

💡 Choice of Alkyl Halide in Williamson Synthesis

To prepare methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE), should we use:

Option A: CH₃X + (CH₃)₃CO⁻Na⁺ (methyl halide + tert-butoxide) → ✓ Good yield (SN2 on primary CH₃)

Option B: (CH₃)₃CX + CH₃O⁻Na⁺ (tert-butyl halide + methoxide) → ✗ Elimination gives isobutylene

Rule: Use the more hindered group as alkoxide, less hindered as halide.

5.2 Dehydration of Alcohols

\[2 \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4, 413\text{K}} \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{-O-C}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]
  • Continuous, excess alcohol (low temperature 413 K)
  • Limited to primary alcohols (1° gives ether)
  • 2° and 3° alcohols give alkenes by elimination at this temperature
  • At higher temperature (443 K), even 1° gives alkene

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023

Question: The best method to prepare tert-butyl methyl ether (MTBE) is:

(a) (CH₃)₃C-Br + CH₃ONa

(b) CH₃-Br + (CH₃)₃CONa

(c) CH₃OH + (CH₃)₃COH / H₂SO₄

(d) Both (a) and (c)

Solution:

Williamson synthesis uses SN2 mechanism.

For SN2, the alkyl halide must be primary (1°).

Option (a): (CH₃)₃C-Br is 3° halide → reacts with strong base CH₃ONa by E2 elimination → isobutylene (NOT MTBE) ✗

Option (b): CH₃-Br is 1° halide → SN2 works → MTBE formed ✓

Option (c): Acid-catalyzed dehydration of mixed alcohols gives mixture, not selective for MTBE ✗

\[\text{CH}_3\text{-Br} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{CO}^-\text{Na}^+ \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-O-C(CH}_3)_3\]

Answer: (b) CH₃-Br + (CH₃)₃CONa

6

Chemical Reactions of Alcohols

Reactions of alcohols can be divided into: (a) reactions involving cleavage of O–H bond (with metals, acids, esterification), (b) reactions involving cleavage of C–O bond (with HX, PX₃, SOCl₂, dehydration), and (c) oxidation. This is the most important section for JEE.

6.1 Reactions Involving O–H Cleavage

With Active Metals (Na, K, Mg, Al)

\[R\text{-OH} + \text{Na} \rightarrow R\text{O}^-\text{Na}^+ + \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\uparrow\]

Reactivity order of alcohols with Na: 1° > 2° > 3°

(More acidic = more reactive; 1° is most acidic)

Acidity of alcohols: CH₃OH > 1° > 2° > 3°

(More alkyl groups → +I effect → less acidic)

6.2 Esterification

\[R\text{-OH} + R'\text{COOH} \xrightleftharpoons[\text{H}_2\text{O}]{\text{H}^+} R'\text{COOR} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]

Fischer esterification. Reaction is reversible. Mechanism: nucleophilic acyl substitution (addition-elimination).

Also with acid chloride or anhydride (faster, irreversible):

\[R\text{-OH} + R'\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{pyridine}} R'\text{COOR} + \text{HCl}\]

6.3 Reactions Involving C–O Cleavage

⭐ With Hydrogen Halides (HX)

\[R\text{-OH} + \text{HX} \rightarrow R\text{-X} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]

Reactivity of HX: HI > HBr > HCl

Reactivity of alcohols: 3° > 2° > 1° (carbocation stability)

With ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl = Lucas Reagent (see Chapter 9 for details)

With PCl₃, PCl₅, SOCl₂

  • 3 R-OH + PCl₃ → 3 R-Cl + H₃PO₃
  • R-OH + PCl₅ → R-Cl + POCl₃ + HCl
  • R-OH + SOCl₂ → R-Cl + SO₂ + HCl (Darzens reagent; SO₂, HCl escape as gases)

Best method: SOCl₂ with pyridine — pure alkyl chloride, gaseous byproducts escape

6.4 Dehydration of Alcohols

🔥 High PYQ Frequency — Saytzeff Rule

\[R\text{-OH} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4, \Delta} \text{Alkene} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]
  • Order of ease of dehydration: 3° > 2° > 1° (carbocation stability)
  • Saytzeff Rule: Major product is the more substituted (more stable) alkene
  • Temperature: ~443 K for 1°, ~363 K for 2°, ~358 K for 3°
  • Carbocation intermediate → rearrangement possible (1,2-hydride or methyl shift)

6.5 Oxidation of Alcohols

🔥 Most Repeated Topic — Oxidation with PCC vs K₂Cr₂O₇

1° Alcohol Oxidation

RCH₂OH → [O] → RCHO → [O] → RCOOH

Mild oxidant (PCC, PDC, Collins): Stops at aldehyde

Strong oxidant (K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺, KMnO₄/H⁺, Jones reagent): Goes to carboxylic acid

2° Alcohol Oxidation

R₂CHOH → [O] → R₂C=O (ketone)

Both mild and strong oxidants give ketone (ketones resist further oxidation under normal conditions)

3° Alcohol Oxidation

R₃COH → No reaction

3° alcohols do NOT have α-H, so they resist oxidation under normal conditions

Strong oxidizing conditions cleave C-C bonds → mixture of acids/ketones

⚠️ Must-Memorize Oxidizing Agents

  • PCC (Pyridinium chlorochromate): C₅H₅NH⁺[CrO₃Cl]⁻ — stops 1° at aldehyde
  • PDC (Pyridinium dichromate): (C₅H₅NH)₂Cr₂O₇ — stops 1° at aldehyde
  • Collins reagent: CrO₃·2C₅H₅N — stops 1° at aldehyde (in CH₂Cl₂)
  • Jones reagent: CrO₃/H₂SO₄/acetone — 1°→acid, 2°→ketone
  • KMnO₄/H⁺ or K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺: strong, 1°→acid
  • Oppenauer oxidation: Al(i-PrO)₃ — selective, 1°→aldehyde, 2°→ketone

6.6 Pinacol-Pinacolone Rearrangement

🔥 Important Named Reaction — Pinacol Rearrangement

\[\text{(CH}_3\text{)}_2\text{C(OH)-C(OH)(CH}_3\text{)}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{dil. H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{(CH}_3\text{)}_3\text{C-CO-CH}_3\]

Pinacol (2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol) → Pinacolone (3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one)

Mechanism:

  1. Protonation of one –OH → loss of H₂O → carbocation
  2. 1,2-methyl shift (most migratory aptitude: H > aryl > 3° alkyl > 2° > 1° > methyl)
  3. Stabilized oxonium ion
  4. Deprotonation → ketone (pinacolone)

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024

Question: The oxidation of a primary alcohol to an aldehyde can be achieved by:

(a) Acidic KMnO₄    (b) Acidic K₂Cr₂O₇

(c) PCC in CH₂Cl₂    (d) All of these

Solution:

KMnO₄/H⁺ and K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ are strong oxidizing agents — they oxidize 1° alcohols all the way to carboxylic acids (not stopping at aldehyde).

PCC (Pyridinium chlorochromate) is a mild oxidizing agent in anhydrous conditions (CH₂Cl₂) — stops the oxidation of 1° alcohol at the aldehyde stage.

\[R\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{PCC/CH}_2\text{Cl}_2} R\text{CHO}\]

Answer: (c) PCC in CH₂Cl₂

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2021

Question: In the pinacol-pinacolone rearrangement, the migrating group is:

(a) H    (b) OH    (c) Methyl group    (d) Water

Solution:

In pinacol rearrangement, after protonation of one –OH and loss of water to form carbocation, the adjacent methyl group migrates (1,2-shift) to stabilize the carbocation.

The migratory aptitude order is: H > aryl > 3° alkyl > 2° alkyl > 1° alkyl > CH₃

In pinacol (only methyl and –OH groups present), a methyl group migrates.

\[\text{Migrating group: Methyl (-CH}_3\text{)}\]

Answer: (c) Methyl group

7

Acidity & Reactions of Phenols

Phenols undergo reactions involving the –OH group (acidity) and electrophilic aromatic substitution on the ring. The –OH group is strongly activating and ortho/para directing, making phenol highly reactive. Named reactions like Reimer-Tiemann and Kolbe are extremely important for JEE.

7.1 Acidic Nature of Phenol

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Acidity of Phenol

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^- + \text{H}^+\]

Why is phenol acidic?

  1. Resonance stabilization of phenoxide ion: Negative charge delocalized over oxygen and ortho/para carbons of the ring (5 resonance structures)
  2. sp² hybridization of carbon: More electronegative than sp³ carbon of alcohols, pulls electrons from O, weakening O–H bond
  3. Phenol is more acidic than alcohol but less acidic than carboxylic acid and carbonic acid

🔥 Critical Acidity Order — Must Memorize

\[\text{Picric acid} > \text{p-Nitrophenol} > \text{o-Nitrophenol} > \text{Phenol} > \text{p-Cresol} > \text{Water} > \text{Ethanol}\]

Effect of substituents on phenol acidity:

  • Electron Withdrawing Groups (EWG) (–NO₂, –CN, –Cl, –CHO, –COOH) increase acidity (stabilize phenoxide)
  • Electron Donating Groups (EDG) (–CH₃, –OCH₃, –NH₂) decrease acidity (destabilize phenoxide)
  • Ortho effect: o-nitrophenol is slightly less acidic than p-nitrophenol due to intramolecular H-bonding (chelation) which destabilizes phenoxide slightly
  • Picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol) — most acidic phenol (pKa ≈ 0.38) due to three –NO₂ groups

💡 Why Phenol does NOT react with NaHCO₃

Acidity order:

\[\text{Carboxylic acid} > \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3 > \text{Phenol} > \text{H}_2\text{O} > \text{Alcohol}\]

Phenol is weaker acid than carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), so it cannot liberate CO₂ from NaHCO₃. This is used to distinguish carboxylic acids from phenols.

But phenol does react with NaOH (stronger base than NaHCO₃):

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]

7.2 Kolbe's Reaction

🔥 Must-Memorize Named Reaction

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa}\] \[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{CO}_2 \xrightarrow{400\text{K}, 4-7\text{ atm}} \text{Sodium salicylate} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{Salicylic acid}\]

Product: 2-Hydroxybenzoic acid (Salicylic acid) — ortho substitution

Mechanism: Phenoxide is more reactive than phenol; CO₂ is the electrophile; ortho product favored due to chelation

7.3 Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

🔥 Most Repeated Named Reaction

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CHCl}_3 + \text{NaOH (aq)} \xrightarrow{\text{(ii) H}^+} \text{Salicylaldehyde}\]

Product: 2-Hydroxybenzaldehyde (Salicylaldehyde) — ortho major

  • Electrophile: Dichlorocarbene (:CCl₂) generated from CHCl₃ + NaOH
  • Ortho product predominates due to chelation (intramolecular H-bonding)
  • Para product (4-hydroxybenzaldehyde) is minor
  • With CCl₄ instead of CHCl₃: gives salicylic acid (after hydrolysis)

7.4 Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

🔥 Important — Halogenation of Phenol

With Br₂ in CS₂ (low temp)

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{CS}_2, 273\text{K}} \text{p-Bromophenol}\]

Mono-substitution (mainly para)

With Br₂ water (excess)

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 3\text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{2,4,6-Tribromophenol (white ppt)}\]

Tri-substitution; white precipitate — test for phenol

Other EAS Reactions of Phenol

  • Nitration (dil. HNO₃): Mixture of o- and p-nitrophenol
  • Nitration (conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄): 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid)
  • Sulfonation (conc. H₂SO₄): o- and p-hydroxybenzenesulfonic acid
  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation: Difficult because AlCl₃ complexes with –OH
  • Friedel-Crafts acylation: With BF₃ as catalyst instead of AlCl₃

7.5 Other Reactions of Phenol

  • With Zn dust: C₆H₅OH + Zn → C₆H₆ + ZnO (reduction, removal of –OH)
  • Hydrogenation: C₆H₅OH + 3H₂ (Ni/Δ) → Cyclohexanol
  • Oxidation: With Na₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ → p-benzoquinone (yellow)
  • With FeCl₃: Gives violet color (test for phenol; phenol + FeCl₃ → [Fe(OC₆H₅)₆]³⁻)
  • With phthalic anhydride: Phenolphthalein (acid-base indicator)

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024

Question: The correct order of acidic strength of the following is:
I. Phenol   II. p-Cresol   III. p-Nitrophenol   IV. p-Chlorophenol

(a) III > IV > I > II    (b) III > I > IV > II

(c) IV > III > I > II    (d) I > III > IV > II

Solution:

Effect of substituents on phenol acidity:

–NO₂ (strong –M, –I): Strongly acid-strengthening → most acidic

–Cl (weak –M, but –I): Moderately acid-strengthening

–H (unsubstituted phenol): Reference

–CH₃ (+I, hyperconjugation): Acid-weakening → least acidic

\[\text{p-Nitrophenol (III)} > \text{p-Chlorophenol (IV)} > \text{Phenol (I)} > \text{p-Cresol (II)}\]

Answer: (a) III > IV > I > II

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2021

Question: Phenol on treatment with CHCl₃ and NaOH followed by acid hydrolysis gives:

(a) Salicylic acid    (b) Salicylaldehyde

(c) p-Hydroxybenzaldehyde    (d) Anisole

Solution:

This is the Reimer-Tiemann reaction.

Phenol + CHCl₃ + NaOH (aq) → intermediate dichlorocarbene (:CCl₂) attacks ortho position of phenoxide.

After acid hydrolysis → salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde).

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{CHCl}_3 + \text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{Salicylaldehyde}\]

Answer: (b) Salicylaldehyde

8

Reactions of Ethers

Ethers (R–O–R') are relatively inert compared to alcohols and phenols. Their main reactions are cleavage by strong acids (HI, HBr) and peroxide formation on exposure to air. The cleavage mechanism depends on the nature of R groups.

8.1 Cleavage of Ethers by HI

🔥 Most Repeated Ether Reaction — HI Cleavage

Case 1: With 1° or 2° alkyl groups (SN2 mechanism)

\[R\text{-O-}R' + \text{HI} \rightarrow R\text{OH} + R'\text{I}\]

I⁻ attacks the smaller (less hindered) alkyl group via SN2 → that group becomes R'I

The larger group stays as alcohol

Case 2: With 3° alkyl or allylic/benzylic group (SN1 mechanism)

3° carbocation forms, then I⁻ attacks → 3°I + R-OH

💡 Excess HI gives two alkyl iodides

\[R\text{-O-}R' + 2\text{HI (excess)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} R\text{I} + R'\text{I} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]

First HI cleaves ether → alcohol + alkyl iodide

Second equivalent of HI reacts with the alcohol → another alkyl iodide

8.2 Zerevitinoff's Active H Test

\[R\text{-OH} + \text{CH}_3\text{MgBr} \rightarrow R\text{-OMgBr} + \text{CH}_4\uparrow\]

Measures "active H" (acidic H on –OH, –NH, –SH). Volume of CH₄ released can determine the number of –OH groups.

8.3 Peroxide Formation

⚠️ Ether Peroxides — Safety Hazard

Ethers (especially diethyl ether) on exposure to air and light form peroxides via free radical mechanism:

\[(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_2\text{O-O-OH (peroxide)}\]
  • Peroxides are explosive and dangerous
  • Storage: Dark bottles, away from air, with small amount of Cu or FeSO₄ as antioxidant
  • Test for peroxides: Adds KI/starch paper → blue-black color (liberates I₂)
  • Removal: Wash with FeSO₄ solution (reduces peroxides)

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2017

Question: The products of the reaction of CH₃OC₂H₅ + HI (excess) are:

(a) CH₃OH + C₂H₅I    (b) CH₃I + C₂H₅OH

(c) CH₃I + C₂H₅I    (d) CH₃OH + C₂H₅OH

Solution:

First equivalent of HI:

I⁻ attacks the smaller alkyl group (CH₃⁻) by SN2 (less hindered)

\[\text{CH}_3\text{O-C}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{HI} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{I} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}\]

Second equivalent of HI reacts with the ethanol formed:

\[\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{HI} \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{I} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]

Overall (with excess HI):

\[\text{CH}_3\text{OC}_2\text{H}_5 + 2\text{HI} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{I} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{I} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]

Answer: (c) CH₃I + C₂H₅I

9

Identification Tests for Alcohols

Identification tests distinguish between 1°, 2°, and 3° alcohols. The two most important tests are Lucas test (using ZnCl₂/HCl) and Victor Meyer's test (using nitric acid). These are extremely high-frequency PYQ topics.

9.1 Lucas Test

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Lucas Test

Lucas Reagent: Conc. HCl + ZnCl₂ (anhydrous)

\[R\text{-OH} + \text{HCl} \xrightarrow{\text{ZnCl}_2} R\text{-Cl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\]

Observation: Formation of alkyl chloride (cloudy/turbid layer)

Alcohol TypeReaction TimeObservation
3° (tertiary)Immediate (within minutes)Turbidity appears immediately
2° (secondary)5-10 minutesTurbidity in 5-10 min
1° (primary)No reaction at room temperatureNo turbidity (requires heating)

Reason: Based on stability of carbocation intermediate. 3° carbocation forms fastest (most stable), 1° doesn't form at room temperature.

Limitation: Only works for alcohols soluble in Lucas reagent (i.e., those with ≤ 6 carbons)

9.2 Victor Meyer's Test

⭐ Important — Victor Meyer Test Color Changes

Steps:

  1. Alcohol + HI (red P) → Alkyl iodide
  2. Alkyl iodide + AgNO₂ (silver nitrite) → Nitroalkane
  3. Nitroalkane + HNO₂ (NaNO₂/HCl) + NaOH → Sodium salt of nitrolic acid (colored)
Alcohol TypeFinal Color
1° (primary)Red (Blood red)
2° (secondary)Blue
3° (tertiary)Colorless (no reaction)

9.3 Other Tests for Phenol

  • FeCl₃ test: Phenol + neutral FeCl₃ → violet color (forms [Fe(OC₆H₅)₆]³⁻ complex)
  • Bromine water test: Phenol + Br₂ water → white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol
  • Liebermann test: Phenol + NaNO₂/H₂SO₄ → NaOH → red/blue/green color
  • Phthalein test: Phenol + phthalic anhydride + H₂SO₄ + NaOH → pink color (phenolphthalein)

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2019

Question: In the Lucas test, turbidity appears immediately with:

(a) 1° alcohol    (b) 2° alcohol

(c) 3° alcohol    (d) Phenol

Solution:

Lucas test uses conc. HCl + ZnCl₂ (Lucas reagent).

The reaction proceeds through carbocation formation.

3° carbocation is most stable (3 alkyl groups stabilize via +I effect and hyperconjugation) → forms fastest → turbidity appears immediately.

2° alcohol: turbidity in 5-10 min

1° alcohol: no reaction at room temperature

\[\text{3° alcohol} \xrightarrow{\text{Lucas reagent}} \text{immediate turbidity (3° R-Cl)}\]

Answer: (c) 3° alcohol

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2018

Question: Phenol gives a characteristic color with FeCl₃ solution. The color is:

(a) Blue    (b) Green    (c) Violet    (d) Red

Solution:

Phenol reacts with neutral FeCl₃ to form a violet-colored complex.

\[6\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{FeCl}_3 \rightarrow [\text{Fe(OC}_6\text{H}_5)_6]^{3-} + 3\text{HCl} + 3\text{H}^+\]

The complex hexaphenolatoiron(III) ion gives violet color.

Answer: (c) Violet

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is phenol more acidic than alcohol?

Phenol is more acidic than alcohol because the phenoxide ion formed after loss of H+ is stabilized by resonance (negative charge delocalized into the benzene ring), while alkoxide ion (RO-) has no such stabilization. Also, the sp2 carbon of phenol is more electronegative than the sp3 carbon of alcohol, making the O-H bond more polar in phenol.

What is the Lucas test used for?

The Lucas test distinguishes 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols using Lucas reagent (conc. HCl + ZnCl2). 3° alcohols react immediately (turbidity within minutes), 2° alcohols react within 5-10 minutes, and 1° alcohols do not react at room temperature. The test is based on the stability of carbocation intermediates.

What is Williamson ether synthesis?

Williamson synthesis is the reaction of an alkyl halide with an alkoxide ion (or sodium alkoxide) to form an ether: R-X + R'O-Na+ → R-O-R' + NaX. It follows SN2 mechanism. For good yield, primary alkyl halides should be used because secondary and tertiary halides undergo elimination (E2) instead of substitution.

What is the Reimer-Tiemann reaction?

The Reimer-Tiemann reaction is the reaction of phenol with chloroform (CHCl3) in the presence of aqueous NaOH, followed by acid hydrolysis, to form salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde). The electrophile is dichlorocarbene (:CCl2), generated from CHCl3 and NaOH.

What is the product of phenol with bromine water?

Phenol reacts with excess bromine water to give 2,4,6-tribromophenol as a white precipitate. This is because the -OH group strongly activates the benzene ring towards electrophilic substitution. With bromine in non-polar solvent like CS2 at low temperature, only mono-substitution (p-bromophenol) occurs.

What is the cumene process for phenol preparation?

Cumene (isopropylbenzene) is oxidized by air to cumene hydroperoxide, which on acid-catalyzed cleavage gives phenol and acetone. This is the commercial method for phenol preparation and co-produces acetone, making it economically important.

What is the pinacol-pinacolone rearrangement?

Pinacol (2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol) on treatment with dilute H2SO4 undergoes rearrangement to form pinacolone (3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one). The reaction involves protonation of one OH, loss of water to form a carbocation, 1,2-methyl shift, and deprotonation.

Why does hydroboration-oxidation give anti-Markovnikov alcohol?

In hydroboration-oxidation, BH3 adds to the less substituted carbon (anti-Markovnikov) due to steric factors and the boron attaching to less hindered carbon. The addition is also syn. Subsequent oxidation with H2O2/OH- replaces B with OH with retention of configuration, giving the anti-Markovnikov alcohol.

Why is C-O bond in phenol stronger than in alcohol?

In phenol, the oxygen is attached to sp2 hybridized carbon which is more electronegative than sp3 carbon of alcohols. Also, the lone pair on oxygen is delocalized into the benzene ring (resonance), giving partial double-bond character to the C-O bond. This makes the C-O bond in phenol stronger and prevents cleavage reactions like those in alcohols.

How many questions come from Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers in JEE Mains?

On average 1-2 questions (4-8 marks) appear from this chapter in every JEE Main session. Most repeated topics are acidity of phenol, Reimer-Tiemann reaction, Williamson synthesis, Lucas test, dehydration of alcohols, and oxidation by PCC/K2Cr2O7.

Alcohols, Phenols & Ethers — Complete JEE Study Guide

Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers is a vital chapter in JEE Organic Chemistry (Class 12, Chapter 7 of NCERT). It covers the structure, preparation, properties, and reactions of three important classes of oxygen-containing organic compounds. The chapter connects previous concepts like GOC, hydrocarbons, and haloalkanes with the upcoming chapter on aldehydes, ketones, and carboxylic acids.

The most important topics for JEE from this chapter are acidity of phenol and substituent effects (especially the order: picric acid > p-nitrophenol > phenol > p-cresol), Reimer-Tiemann reaction (phenol + CHCl₃/NaOH → salicylaldehyde), Kolbe's reaction (phenol + NaOH + CO₂ → salicylic acid), Williamson ether synthesis (SN2 mechanism with primary alkyl halide), Lucas test for distinguishing 1°/2°/3° alcohols, hydroboration-oxidation (anti-Markovnikov syn addition), oxidation of alcohols (PCC stops at aldehyde, K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ goes to carboxylic acid), pinacol-pinacolone rearrangement (1,2-methyl shift), and cumene process for industrial phenol preparation (co-produces acetone).

Students should focus on memorizing all named reactions with their products and reagents, understanding the resonance stabilization of phenoxide ion (which explains phenol's acidity), and mastering the SN2 vs E2 mechanism in Williamson synthesis (primary halides give substitution, tertiary give elimination). The distinction between PCC (mild oxidant, stops at aldehyde) and K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ (strong oxidant, gives carboxylic acid) is among the most tested concepts.

For JEE Advanced, additional focus should be on the mechanism of pinacol-pinacolone rearrangement (migratory aptitude: H > aryl > 3° alkyl > 2° > 1° > methyl), stereochemistry of hydroboration (syn addition with retention in oxidation step), and predicting products of ether cleavage (SN2 for primary group with I⁻ attacking smaller alkyl; SN1 for tertiary group via carbocation). Identification tests like Lucas (timing of turbidity), Victor Meyer (color: red for 1°, blue for 2°, colorless for 3°), FeCl₃ (violet with phenol), and bromine water (white ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenol) are routinely asked.

Common mistakes to avoid: confusing Markovnikov (acid hydration) with anti-Markovnikov (hydroboration) addition; using tertiary alkyl halides in Williamson synthesis (gives elimination not ether); forgetting that phenol doesn't react with NaHCO₃ (weaker acid than carbonic acid); and mixing up the Lucas test order (3° fastest, not 1°). Mastery of these concepts will guarantee 4-8 marks in JEE Main and help with related questions in JEE Advanced.
