Structure of Carbonyl & Carboxyl Group

Aldehydes, ketones, and carboxylic acids are among the most important classes of organic compounds containing the carbonyl group (C=O). This chapter is one of the highest-weightage chapters in JEE Organic Chemistry, with 2-3 questions appearing in almost every session. Understanding the structure and reactivity of the carbonyl group is the foundation for mastering all subsequent reactions.

1.1 Structure of the Carbonyl Group (C=O)

Key Structural Features of C=O

The carbonyl group consists of a carbon atom double-bonded to an oxygen atom (C=O).

Hybridization: Both C and O are sp² hybridized

Bond angle: Approximately 120° (trigonal planar geometry)

Bond length: C=O bond length = 1.23 Å (shorter than C–O single bond = 1.43 Å)

Electronegativity difference: Oxygen (3.5) is more electronegative than carbon (2.5), creating a polar bond

\[\stackrel{\delta+}{C} = \stackrel{\delta-}{O}\]

The carbonyl carbon is electrophilic (partially positive) and the oxygen is nucleophilic (partially negative). This polarity is the driving force for nucleophilic addition reactions.

⭐ Important for JEE — Resonance in Carbonyl Group

The carbonyl group shows two resonance structures:

\[R-\overset{+}{C}(=O)-R' \longleftrightarrow R-\overset{-}{C}(-\overset{+}{O})-R'\]

The second resonance structure (with charges) is a minor contributor but explains the electrophilic character of carbonyl carbon and nucleophilic character of oxygen.

1.3 Structure of the Carboxyl Group (–COOH)

Carboxyl Group Structure

The carboxyl group (–COOH) is a combination of:

  • A carbonyl group (C=O)
  • A hydroxyl group (–OH)
\[R-C\underset{\displaystyle OH}{\overset{\displaystyle O}{=}}\]

Hybridization: Carbon is sp² hybridized. The –OH oxygen is also sp² hybridized.

Resonance: The lone pair on –OH oxygen participates in resonance with C=O, creating partial double bond character for C–OH bond:

\[R-C(=O)-OH \longleftrightarrow R-C(-O^{-})=\overset{+}{O}H\]

Due to this resonance, the C–OH bond in carboxylic acids has partial double bond character, making it stronger and less reactive than a typical C–OH single bond. This is why –OH is not easily replaced in carboxylic acids (unlike alcohols).

💡 Key Insight — Why Carboxylic Acids Are Acidic

The acidic nature of carboxylic acids is due to:

  • Resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion (RCOO⁻)
  • The negative charge is delocalized over two equivalent oxygen atoms
  • Both C–O bonds in RCOO⁻ have equal bond length (1.27 Å) — between single (1.43 Å) and double (1.23 Å)
\[R-COO^- \longleftrightarrow R-C(=O)-O^- \longleftrightarrow R-C(-O^-)=O\]

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024

Question: The C–O bond length in the carboxylate ion (RCOO⁻) is:

(a) Same as C=O bond length (1.23 Å)

(b) Same as C–O single bond length (1.43 Å)

(c) Intermediate between C=O and C–O bond lengths (~1.27 Å)

(d) Longer than C–O single bond

Solution:

In the carboxylate ion (RCOO⁻), the negative charge is delocalized over both oxygen atoms through resonance.

Due to resonance, both C–O bonds have partial double bond character.

This makes both bonds equivalent with a bond length that is:

- Greater than C=O (1.23 Å) — because it's not a full double bond

- Less than C–O (1.43 Å) — because it has partial double bond character

The measured bond length is 1.27 Å, which is intermediate between the two.

\[\text{C–O bond in RCOO}^- \approx 1.27 \text{ Å (intermediate)}\]

Answer: (c) Intermediate between C=O and C–O bond lengths (~1.27 Å)

Preparation of Aldehydes & Ketones

There are numerous methods for preparing aldehydes and ketones, ranging from oxidation of alcohols to specialized name reactions. JEE frequently tests these preparation methods, especially the name reactions like Rosenmund reduction, Stephen reaction, Etard reaction, and Gattermann-Koch reaction.

2.1 Preparation of Aldehydes

⭐ Important — Most Repeated PYQ Topic

Rosenmund Reduction: Reduction of acid chlorides to aldehydes

\[R-COCl + H_2 \xrightarrow{Pd/BaSO_4} R-CHO + HCl\]

Key Points:

  • Catalyst: Pd on BaSO₄ (BaSO₄ acts as poison to prevent over-reduction to alcohol)
  • Also called Rosenmund-von Braun reaction
  • Formaldehyde (HCHO) cannot be prepared by this method (no HCOCl exists)
  • Can be used for both aliphatic and aromatic aldehydes

Stephen Reaction (Reduction of Nitriles)

\[R-C\equiv N + SnCl_2 + HCl \rightarrow R-CH=NH \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} R-CHO\]

Key Points:

  • Reduces nitriles (cyanides) to aldehydes
  • Intermediate: imine (R-CH=NH), which is hydrolyzed to aldehyde
  • Uses SnCl₂/HCl (mild reducing agent)
  • Useful for preparing both aliphatic and aromatic aldehydes

Etard Reaction (Aromatic Aldehydes Only)

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO} \text{ (via chromium complex)}\]

Key Points:

  • Toluene is oxidized to benzaldehyde
  • Reagent: Chromyl chloride (CrO₂Cl₂) in CS₂
  • Intermediate: chromium complex, hydrolyzed to give aldehyde
  • Only methyl group attached to benzene ring is oxidized
  • Side-chain oxidation stops at aldehyde stage (not over-oxidized to acid)

🔥 High PYQ Frequency — Gattermann-Koch Reaction

Gattermann-Koch Reaction: Formylation of benzene

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + CO + HCl \xrightarrow{AlCl_3/CuCl} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5CHO\]

Key Points:

  • Benzene is treated with CO and HCl in presence of anhydrous AlCl₃ and CuCl
  • Produces benzaldehyde
  • Equivalent to introducing a –CHO group on aromatic ring
  • CuCl acts as co-catalyst
  • Note: This is NOT Gattermann reaction (which uses HCN/HCl and is for phenols/ethers)

DIBAL-H Reduction (From Esters/Nitriles)

\[R-C\equiv N \xrightarrow{DIBAL-H} R-CHO\] \[R-COOR' \xrightarrow{DIBAL-H} R-CHO\]

DIBAL-H (Diisobutylaluminium hydride) reduces nitriles and esters to aldehydes at low temperature (–78°C).

2.2 Preparation of Ketones

From Acid Chlorides + Dialkylcadmium

\[2R-COCl + R_2''Cd \rightarrow 2R-CO-R'' + CdCl_2\]

Key Points:

  • Acid chlorides react with dialkylcadmium (R₂Cd) to give ketones
  • Grignard reagent cannot be used (it over-reacts to give 3° alcohol)
  • Dialkylcadmium is less reactive than Grignard — stops at ketone stage
  • Useful for preparing unsymmetrical ketones

From Nitriles (with Grignard Reagent)

\[R-C\equiv N + R'MgX \rightarrow R-C(=NMgX)-R' \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} R-CO-R'\]

Grignard reagent adds to nitrile; hydrolysis gives ketone.

Friedel-Crafts Acylation (Aromatic Ketones)

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + CH_3COCl \xrightarrow{AlCl_3} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5COCH_3 + HCl\]

Key Points:

  • Aromatic ketones prepared by Friedel-Crafts acylation
  • Acyl chloride (RCOCl) + benzene + AlCl₃ → aryl ketone
  • No poly-substitution (–COR is deactivating)
  • Product is always a phenyl ketone (Ar–CO–R)

From Alcohols (Oxidation)

\[R-CH_2OH \xrightarrow{PCC} R-CHO \text{ (aldehyde)}\] \[R_2CHOH \xrightarrow{K_2Cr_2O_7/H^+} R-CO-R \text{ (ketone)}\]

Key Points:

  • PCC (Pyridinium chlorochromate) oxidizes 1° alcohol to aldehyde (stops at aldehyde)
  • Collins reagent (CrO₃·2C₅H₅N) also stops at aldehyde
  • K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ or KMnO₄/H⁺ over-oxidize 1° alcohol to carboxylic acid
  • 2° alcohols are oxidized to ketones (no over-oxidation)
  • Oppenauer oxidation: Uses Al[OCH(CH₃)₂]₃ — selectively oxidizes 1° alcohol to aldehyde

2.3 From Hydrocarbons

Ozonolysis of Alkenes

\[R-CH=CH-R' + O_3 \rightarrow \text{ozonide} \xrightarrow{Zn/H_2O} R-CHO + R'-CHO\] \[R_2C=CR_2 + O_3 \rightarrow R_2CO + R_2CO \text{ (ketones)}\]

Ozonolysis followed by reductive workup (Zn/H₂O) gives aldehydes and/or ketones depending on the substitution pattern of the alkene. Use of H₂O₂ (oxidative workup) would over-oxidize aldehydes to acids.

Hydration of Alkynes

\[HC\equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{HgSO_4/H_2SO_4} CH_3CHO \text{ (acetaldehyde)}\] \[RC\equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{HgSO_4/H_2SO_4} R-CO-CH_3 \text{ (ketone)}\]

Key Points:

  • Acetylene gives acetaldehyde (only case giving aldehyde)
  • All other terminal alkynes give methyl ketones (Markovnikov addition)
  • Internal alkynes give mixtures of ketones
  • Follows Markovnikov's rule: H goes to carbon with more H's

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Jan 30 Shift 2)

Question: In Rosenmund reduction, the role of BaSO₄ in Pd/BaSO₄ catalyst is to:

(a) Increase the reactivity of the catalyst

(b) Act as a poison to prevent over-reduction to alcohol

(c) Provide acidic medium

(d) Reduce the acid chloride to aldehyde

Solution:

Rosenmund Reduction: RCOCl + H₂ → RCHO + HCl

Catalyst: Pd supported on BaSO₄

Without BaSO₄, Pd would catalyze over-reduction:

RCOCl + 2H₂ → RCH₂OH + HCl (unwanted alcohol formation)

BaSO₄ acts as a poison/catalyst modifier that:

- Reduces the activity of Pd catalyst

- Prevents further reduction of aldehyde to alcohol

- Stops the reaction at the aldehyde stage

Similarly, thioquinanthrene or quinoline/S (Lindlar-type poison) can also be used.

\[\text{BaSO}_4 \text{ acts as poison to prevent over-reduction}\]

Answer: (b) Act as a poison to prevent over-reduction to alcohol

⚠️ Common Confusion — Gattermann vs Gattermann-Koch

  • Gattermann-Koch: Benzene + CO + HCl + AlCl₃/CuCl → Benzaldehyde (only for aromatic hydrocarbons)
  • Gattermann: Phenol/ether + HCN + HCl + AlCl₃ → forms imine → hydrolysis → aldehyde (for activated aromatic rings)
  • Don't confuse the two — Gattermann-Koch is specifically for benzene, Gattermann is for phenols/ethers

Nucleophilic Addition Reactions

Nucleophilic addition is the most characteristic reaction of aldehydes and ketones. The electrophilic carbonyl carbon (δ+) is attacked by nucleophiles, and the resulting alkoxide is protonated. Understanding the mechanism and reactivity order is crucial for JEE.

3.1 General Mechanism of Nucleophilic Addition

⭐ Most Important — Mechanism (High PYQ Frequency)

\[\text{Step 1: } Nu^- \text{ attacks } C^{\delta+} \rightarrow \text{tetrahedral alkoxide}\] \[\text{Step 2: } \text{Protonation of } O^- \rightarrow \text{addition product}\]

Detailed Mechanism:

  1. The nucleophile (:Nu⁻) attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon (Cδ+)
  2. The π bond breaks; electrons move to oxygen, forming an alkoxide ion
  3. The carbon changes from sp² (trigonal planar) to sp³ (tetrahedral)
  4. The alkoxide is protonated (by water/solvent) to give the addition product
\[R_2C=O + :Nu^- \rightarrow R_2C(Nu)-O^- \xrightarrow{H^+} R_2C(Nu)-OH\]

3.2 Reactivity Order — Aldehydes vs Ketones

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Reactivity Order

\[\text{Reactivity: } HCHO > CH_3CHO > CH_3CH_2CHO > CH_3COCH_3 > CH_3COR > R_2CO\]

Two factors govern reactivity:

1. Electronic (Inductive) Effect

Alkyl groups are +I groups (electron-donating). They increase electron density on carbonyl carbon, making it less electrophilic → less reactive.

Aldehydes (1 alkyl group) > Ketones (2 alkyl groups)

2. Steric Effect

Bulkier groups around C=O create steric hindrance, making it harder for nucleophile to attack.

Ketones have 2 groups → more hindered → less reactive

💡 JEE Shortcut — Aromatic vs Aliphatic

Aromatic aldehydes (like C₆H₅CHO) are less reactive than aliphatic aldehydes because:

  • The phenyl group donates electrons by +R (resonance) effect to the carbonyl carbon
  • This reduces the electrophilic character of C=O
  • Also, the phenyl group is bulky → steric hindrance

Extended order: HCHO > CH₃CHO > C₆H₅CHO > CH₃COCH₃ > C₆H₅COCH₃ > C₆H₅COC₆H₅

3.3 Addition of HCN (Cyanohydrin Formation)

\[R-CHO + HCN \rightarrow R-CH(OH)-CN \text{ (cyanohydrin)}\] \[R-CO-R' + HCN \rightarrow R-C(OH)(CN)-R' \text{ (cyanohydrin)}\]

Key Points:

  • Product is a cyanohydrin (α-hydroxy nitrile)
  • HCN is a weak nucleophile; reaction is base-catalyzed
  • The CN⁻ ion is the actual nucleophile (generated by base)
  • Reactivity order: Aldehydes > Ketones (same as general order)
  • Ketones are less reactive due to steric and electronic reasons
  • Benzaldehyde does NOT form cyanohydrin easily (aromatic ring deactivates C=O)
  • Product has one more C atom — useful for chain extension

3.4 Addition of NaHSO₃ (Bisulfite Addition)

\[R-CHO + NaHSO_3 \rightarrow R-CH(OH)-SO_3Na \text{ (bisulfite adduct)}\]

Key Points:

  • NaHSO₃ is a strong nucleophile (HSO₃⁻ ion)
  • Product is a crystalline solid (used for purification/separation)
  • Adduct decomposes with acid or base to regenerate aldehyde/ketone
  • Reactivity: Aldehydes > Methyl ketones > Other ketones
  • Cannot add to: acetophenone (C₆H₅COCH₃), benzophenone (too bulky)

The addition of ammonia derivatives is pH-dependent:

  • Optimal pH ≈ 5 (weakly acidic)
  • Too acidic: The amine (H₂N–Z) gets protonated → loses nucleophilicity
  • Too basic: The carbonyl is not activated → no reaction
  • At pH 5: carbonyl O is protonated (increasing electrophilicity of C), but amine N is still free to act as nucleophile

3.6 Addition of Grignard Reagent

\[R-CHO + R'MgX \rightarrow R-CH(OMgX)-R' \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} R-CH(OH)-R'\] \[R-CO-R' + R''MgX \rightarrow R-C(OMgX)(R')-R'' \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} R-C(OH)(R')-R''\]

Key Points:

  • Aldehyde + Grignard → 1° alcohol (from HCHO) or 2° alcohol (from RCHO)
  • Ketone + Grignard → 3° alcohol
  • HCHO + RMgX → RCH₂OH (1° alcohol with one extra C)
  • Reaction is irreversible (Grignard is a very strong nucleophile)

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023

Question: The correct order of reactivity towards nucleophilic addition reaction is:

(a) HCHO < CH₃CHO < CH₃COCH₃ < C₆H₅COCH₃

(b) HCHO > CH₃CHO > CH₃COCH₃ > C₆H₅COCH₃

(c) C₆H₅COCH₃ > CH₃COCH₃ > CH₃CHO > HCHO

(d) CH₃CHO > HCHO > CH₃COCH₃ > C₆H₅COCH₃

Solution:

Factor 1: Electronic Effect

- HCHO: No alkyl group → most electrophilic C=O → most reactive

- CH₃CHO: One +I group (CH₃) → less electrophilic → less reactive

- CH₃COCH₃: Two +I groups → even less electrophilic → less reactive

- C₆H₅COCH₃: Phenyl group (+R effect) + CH₃ → least reactive

Factor 2: Steric Effect

- HCHO (smallest) → least hindered → most reactive

- C₆H₅COCH₃ (bulkiest) → most hindered → least reactive

Both factors give the same order:

\[HCHO > CH_3CHO > CH_3COCH_3 > C_6H_5COCH_3\]

Answer: (b) HCHO > CH₃CHO > CH₃COCH₃ > C₆H₅COCH₃

Reduction & Oxidation of Aldehydes and Ketones

Aldehydes and ketones undergo reduction to alcohols and (in the case of aldehydes) oxidation to carboxylic acids. The choice of reducing agent determines the product — and JEE tests this knowledge extensively.

4.1 Reduction to Alcohols

Catalytic Reduction (H₂/Ni, Pt, or Pd)

\[R-CHO + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni} R-CH_2OH \text{ (1° alcohol)}\] \[R-CO-R' + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni} R-CH(OH)-R' \text{ (2° alcohol)}\]

NaBH₄ (Sodium Borohydride) — Mild Reducing Agent

\[R-CHO + NaBH_4 \rightarrow R-CH_2OH \text{ (1° alcohol)}\] \[R-CO-R' + NaBH_4 \rightarrow R-CH(OH)-R' \text{ (2° alcohol)}\]

Key Points:

  • Reduces only aldehydes and ketones (selective)
  • Does NOT reduce esters, carboxylic acids, or amides
  • Milder than LiAlH₄
  • Source of H⁻ (hydride ion)

LiAlH₄ (Lithium Aluminium Hydride) — Strong Reducing Agent

\[R-CHO \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} R-CH_2OH\] \[R-CO-R' \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} R-CH(OH)-R'\] \[R-COOH \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} R-CH_2OH\] \[R-COOR' \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} R-CH_2OH + R'OH\]

Key Points:

  • Reduces aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amides, nitriles
  • Does NOT reduce isolated C=C (double bond)
  • More powerful than NaBH₄
  • Reaction must be in dry ether (LiAlH₄ reacts violently with water)

4.2 Reduction to Hydrocarbons (C=O → CH₂)

🔥 High PYQ Frequency — Clemmensen vs Wolff-Kishner

Clemmensen Reduction

\[R-CO-R' \xrightarrow{Zn-Hg/HCl} R-CH_2-R'\]
  • Medium: Acidic (conc. HCl)
  • Reagent: Zn-Hg amalgam
  • NOT suitable for: Acid-sensitive compounds

Wolff-Kishner Reduction

\[R-CO-R' \xrightarrow{NH_2NH_2/KOH} R-CH_2-R'\]
  • Medium: Basic (KOH, ethylene glycol)
  • Reagent: Hydrazine (NH₂NH₂) + KOH
  • NOT suitable for: Base-sensitive compounds

Both reduce C=O to CH₂ — choice depends on compatibility with other functional groups in the molecule.

4.3 Oxidation of Aldehydes

⭐ Important — Aldehydes vs Ketones Oxidation

Aldehydes are easily oxidized to carboxylic acids by mild oxidizing agents:

\[R-CHO + [O] \rightarrow R-COOH\]

Common oxidizing agents:

  • Tollens' reagent [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ — gives silver mirror (Ag↓)
  • Fehling's solution — gives red-brown precipitate (Cu₂O)
  • Benedict's solution — similar to Fehling's
  • K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺, KMnO₄/H⁺ — strong oxidants

⚠️ Critical — Aromatic Aldehydes Exception

Benzaldehyde (C₆H₅CHO) does NOT give Fehling's test!

  • Fehling's test is positive for aliphatic aldehydes only
  • Aromatic aldehydes give Tollens' test but NOT Fehling's test
  • Reason: Fehling's oxidation is slower; the C₆H₅ group stabilizes C=O by resonance
  • Ketones are NOT oxidized by mild oxidants (need strong oxidants + heat → cleavage occurs)

4.4 Oxidation of Ketones (Baeyer-Villiger Oxidation)

Baeyer-Villiger Oxidation

\[R-CO-R' + mCPBA \rightarrow R-CO-O-R' \text{ (ester)}\]

Key Points:

  • Ketone is converted to an ester
  • Oxidant: Peracid (mCPBA, CF₃CO₃H, or peracetic acid)
  • Oxygen is inserted between C=O and one of the alkyl groups
  • The migrating group is the one with higher migratory aptitude: H > 3° alkyl > 2° > 1° > methyl

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2022

Question: Which of the following reagents can be used to convert acetaldehyde (CH₃CHO) to ethyl alcohol (CH₃CH₂OH)?

(a) Zn-Hg/HCl

(b) NH₂NH₂/KOH

(c) NaBH₄

(d) AlCl₃

Solution:

Analysis of each option:

(a) Zn-Hg/HCl is Clemmensen reduction — reduces C=O to CH₂ (gives ethane, not ethanol)

\[CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{Zn-Hg/HCl} CH_3CH_3 \text{ (ethane)}\]

(b) NH₂NH₂/KOH is Wolff-Kishner reduction — also reduces C=O to CH₂ (gives ethane)

\[CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{NH_2NH_2/KOH} CH_3CH_3\]

(c) NaBH₄ is a mild reducing agent — reduces C=O to CH-OH (gives ethanol) ✓

\[CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{NaBH_4} CH_3CH_2OH \text{ (ethanol)}\]

(d) AlCl₃ is a Lewis acid — used in Friedel-Crafts, not for reduction

\[\text{NaBH}_4 \text{ reduces aldehyde to 1° alcohol}\]

Answer: (c) NaBH₄

Aldol Condensation & Cannizzaro Reaction

These two reactions are the most frequently tested name reactions from this chapter in JEE. The key distinction: Aldol requires α-hydrogen, while Cannizzaro requires NO α-hydrogen. Mastering this distinction alone can fetch you marks in multiple questions.

5.1 Aldol Condensation

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Aldol Condensation

Definition: Two molecules of an aldehyde or ketone containing at least one α-hydrogen undergo condensation in the presence of a dilute base (like dil. NaOH, Na₂CO₃) or dilute acid to form a β-hydroxy aldehyde (aldol) or β-hydroxy ketone (ketol).

\[2CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{dil.\ NaOH} CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-CHO \text{ (aldol)}\]

On heating, the aldol undergoes dehydration (loss of H₂O) to give an α,β-unsaturated aldehyde:

\[CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-CHO \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3-CH=CH-CHO + H_2O\]

The complete process (aldol + dehydration) is called Aldol Condensation.

Mechanism of Aldol Condensation

  1. Step 1 (Enolate formation): OH⁻ removes α-H from aldehyde, forming a resonance-stabilized enolate ion
    2. \[CH_3CHO + OH^- \rightarrow ^-CH_2CHO + H_2O\]
  2. Step 2 (Nucleophilic addition): Enolate ion (nucleophile) attacks the carbonyl carbon of another aldehyde molecule
  3. Step 3 (Protonation): The alkoxide ion is protonated by water to give β-hydroxy aldehyde (aldol)
  4. Step 4 (Dehydration): On heating, aldol loses water to form α,β-unsaturated compound

💡 JEE Shortcut — Which Compounds Give Aldol?

Gives Aldol: Compounds with at least one α-H atom

  • ✓ CH₃CHO (acetaldehyde) — 3 α-H
  • ✓ CH₃COCH₃ (acetone) — 6 α-H
  • ✓ CH₃CH₂CHO (propanal) — 2 α-H
  • ✓ C₆H₅CH₂CHO (phenylacetaldehyde) — 2 α-H
  • ✓ C₆H₅COCH₃ (acetophenone) — 3 α-H

Does NOT give Aldol: Compounds without α-H

  • ✗ HCHO (formaldehyde)
  • ✗ C₆H₅CHO (benzaldehyde)
  • ✗ (CH₃)₃C-CHO (pivaldehyde/trimethylacetaldehyde)
  • ✗ C₆H₅COC₆H₅ (benzophenone)

5.2 Crossed Aldol Condensation

When two different aldehydes/ketones undergo aldol condensation, it is called crossed aldol. The product is a mixture of 4 products (unless one has no α-H).

Best case: One reactant has no α-H (like HCHO or C₆H₅CHO) — it can only act as electrophile (not nucleophile), giving cleaner product.

\[C_6H_5CHO + CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{dil.\ NaOH} C_6H_5CH=CH-CHO \text{ (cinnamaldehyde)}\]

This is a crossed aldol between benzaldehyde (no α-H) and acetaldehyde (has α-H).

5.3 Cannizzaro Reaction

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Cannizzaro Reaction

Definition: Aldehydes that do NOT have α-hydrogen atoms undergo self-oxidation-reduction (disproportionation) when treated with concentrated alkali (NaOH/KOH). One molecule is reduced to alcohol and another is oxidized to carboxylic acid salt.

\[2HCHO + NaOH \rightarrow CH_3OH + HCOONa\] \[2C_6H_5CHO + NaOH \rightarrow C_6H_5CH_2OH + C_6H_5COONa\]

Compounds that undergo Cannizzaro Reaction

  • HCHO (formaldehyde) — no α-carbon, so no α-H
  • C₆H₅CHO (benzaldehyde) — α-carbon is aromatic C, has no H attached
  • (CH₃)₃C–CHO (pivaldehyde) — α-carbon is quaternary C, has no H
  • 2,4,6-trinitrobenzaldehyde — electron-withdrawing groups prevent enolization
  • Furfural (furan-2-carbaldehyde)

🎯 JEE Shortcut — Aldol vs Cannizzaro (Decision Tree)

  • Step 1: Check if the compound has α-H
  • Step 2: If YES (α-H present) + dilute base → Aldol Condensation
  • Step 3: If NO (α-H absent) + concentrated base → Cannizzaro Reaction

Crossed Cannizzaro: When formaldehyde (HCHO) is mixed with another aldehyde without α-H, HCHO is preferentially oxidized (since it's more reactive) and the other is reduced.

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Jan 29 Shift 1)

Question: Which of the following compounds will undergo Cannizzaro reaction?

(a) CH₃CHO

(b) CH₃COCH₃

(c) C₆H₅CHO

(d) CH₃CH₂CHO

Solution:

Condition for Cannizzaro: The aldehyde must NOT have α-hydrogen

Check each option:

(a) CH₃CHO → α-carbon is CH₃ → has 3 α-H → gives ALDOL, not Cannizzaro

(b) CH₃COCH₃ → α-carbon is CH₃ → has 6 α-H → gives ALDOL (with acetone)

(c) C₆H₅CHO → α-carbon is the aromatic C of phenyl → NO α-H → gives CANNIZZARO ✓

(d) CH₃CH₂CHO → α-carbon is CH₂ → has 2 α-H → gives ALDOL

\[2C_6H_5CHO + NaOH \rightarrow C_6H_5CH_2OH + C_6H_5COONa\]

Answer: (c) C₆H₅CHO

⚠️ Common Mistake — Aldol vs Cannizzaro

  • Mistake: Assuming aromatic aldehydes (like C₆H₅CHO) always give Cannizzaro
  • Fact: C₆H₅CH₂CHO (phenylacetaldehyde) HAS α-H (on CH₂) → gives ALDOL
  • Mistake: Confusing α-carbon with the carbon attached to functional group
  • Fact: α-carbon is the carbon directly adjacent to C=O. Check for H on THIS carbon.
  • Key distinction: C₆H₅CHO → Cannizzaro (no α-H), but C₆H₅CH₂CHO → Aldol (has α-H on CH₂)

Haloform Reaction & Other Important Reactions

The haloform reaction is an identification test as well as a synthetic method. Along with other reactions like Kolbe-Schmitt, Reimer-Tiemann, and electrophilic substitutions, these constitute the bulk of JEE name reaction questions.

6.1 Haloform Reaction

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Iodoform Test

Definition: Aldehydes/ketones with CH₃CO– group (methyl ketones) and alcohols that can be oxidized to CH₃CO– group react with NaOH + X₂ (or NaOX) to give a haloform (CHX₃) and a carboxylate salt.

\[CH_3COR + 3NaOI \rightarrow CI_3COR \xrightarrow{NaOH} CHI_3\downarrow + RCOONa\] \[\text{(yellow ppt of iodoform)}\]

Compounds giving Haloform/Iodoform test:

  • Methyl ketones (CH₃COR): CH₃COCH₃, CH₃COC₂H₅, C₆H₅COCH₃ (acetophenone)
  • Acetaldehyde (CH₃CHO) — the only aldehyde that gives iodoform test
  • Secondary alcohols with CH₃CH(OH)– group: CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ (isopropanol), CH₃CH(OH)C₂H₅
  • Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) — gets oxidized to CH₃CHO which gives iodoform

⚠️ JEE Favorite Trap — Which does NOT give Iodoform?

Common wrong answers in JEE:

  • ✗ HCHO — no CH₃ group → no iodoform
  • ✗ C₆H₅CHO (benzaldehyde) — no CH₃CO group → no iodoform
  • ✗ Diethyl ketone (CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃) — no CH₃CO group → no iodoform
  • ✗ 1-Propanol (CH₃CH₂CH₂OH) — 1° alcohol without CH₃CH(OH) → no iodoform
  • ✗ 3-Pentanol (CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃) — no CH₃CH(OH) → no iodoform

6.2 Electrophilic Substitution Reactions of Aromatic Aldehydes

Kolbe-Schmitt Reaction

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5OH + CO_2 \xrightarrow{NaOH, 125°C, 4-7 atm} \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(OH)COONa \xrightarrow{H^+} \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(OH)COOH\]

This reaction produces salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) from sodium phenoxide and CO₂. The carboxyl group enters ortho position.

Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5OH + CHCl_3 + NaOH \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_4(OH)CHO \text{ (salicylaldehyde)}\]

This reaction introduces a –CHO group at the ortho position of phenol, producing salicylaldehyde. The reagent is chloroform (CHCl₃) and NaOH (dichlorocarbene intermediate).

6.3 Polymerization Reactions

Polymerization of Formaldehyde and Acetaldehyde

  • Paraformaldehyde: nHCHO → (CH₂O)ₙ on evaporation of formaldehyde solution
  • Trioxane: 3HCHO → cyclic trimer (1,3,5-trioxane) with H₂SO₄
  • Paraformaldehyde: (HCHO)ₙ where n = 6 to 100, formed on concentrating HCHO solution
  • Paraldehyde: 4CH₃CHO → cyclic tetramer with dil. H₂SO₄ at room temperature
  • Metaldehyde: (CH₃CHO)ₙ with HCl at low temperature (used as fuel tablet)

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2023

Question: Which of the following compounds will give a yellow precipitate with iodine and NaOH?

(a) CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol)

(b) C₆H₅CHO (benzaldehyde)

(c) CH₃COCH₂CH₃ (butanone)

(d) CH₃CH₂CH₂OH (1-propanol)

Solution:

Condition for iodoform test: Compound must have CH₃CO– group OR must be oxidizable to CH₃CO– group.

Analysis:

(a) CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol) → oxidized to CH₃CHO → has CH₃CO– group → gives iodoform ✓

\[CH_3CH_2OH \xrightarrow{[O]} CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{NaOH/I_2} CHI_3\downarrow + HCOONa\]

(b) C₆H₅CHO (benzaldehyde) → no CH₃CO– group → no iodoform

(c) CH₃COCH₂CH₃ (butanone) → has CH₃CO– group → gives iodoform ✓

\[CH_3COCH_2CH_3 \xrightarrow{NaOH/I_2} CHI_3\downarrow + CH_3CH_2COONa\]

(d) CH₃CH₂CH₂OH (1-propanol) → oxidized to CH₃CH₂CHO → no CH₃CO– → no iodoform

Both (a) and (c) give iodoform test.

\[\text{Both (a) and (c) give yellow precipitate of CHI}_3\]

Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids can be prepared by oxidation of primary alcohols, aldehydes, and alkyl benzenes, as well as by hydrolysis of nitriles, amides, esters, and Grignard reagents with CO₂.

8.1 From Primary Alcohols, Aldehydes, and Alkenes

\[R-CH_2OH \xrightarrow{KMnO_4/H^+ \text{ or } K_2Cr_2O_7/H^+} R-COOH\] \[R-CHO \xrightarrow{[O]} R-COOH\]

Oxidizing agents: KMnO₄/H⁺, K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺, CrO₃/H⁺ (Jones reagent)

8.2 From Alkyl Benzenes (Side-chain Oxidation)

⭐ Important — Side-chain Oxidation (JEE Favorite)

\[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5CH_3 \xrightarrow{KMnO_4/KOH, \Delta} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5COOK \xrightarrow{H^+} \text{C}_6H_5COOH\]

Key Points:

  • Any alkyl side chain on benzene ring (–CH₃, –C₂H₅, –C₃H₇, etc.) is oxidized to –COOH
  • The entire side chain is oxidized to –COOH regardless of length
  • Requires hot KMnO₄/KOH (strongly oxidizing, basic medium)
  • Toluene → benzoic acid; Ethylbenzene → benzoic acid; Isopropylbenzene → benzoic acid
  • Exception: If the carbon attached to ring has NO H (like in tert-butylbenzene C₆H₅C(CH₃)₃), oxidation does NOT occur

8.3 From Nitriles and Amides

\[R-CN + 2H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+ \text{ or } OH^-} R-COOH + NH_3\] \[R-CONH_2 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+ \text{ or } OH^-} R-COOH + NH_3\]

Nitriles and amides are hydrolyzed to carboxylic acids under acidic or basic conditions.

8.4 From Grignard Reagents

\[R-MgX + CO_2 \rightarrow R-COOMgX \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} R-COOH\]

Key Points:

  • Grignard reagent attacks CO₂ (electrophile)
  • Adds one carbon atom (chain extension)
  • Produces a carboxylic acid with one more C than the original Grignard
  • Must be done in dry ether (Grignard reacts with water)

8.5 From Acyl Halides, Anhydrides, and Esters

\[R-COCl + H_2O \rightarrow R-COOH + HCl\] \[(RCO)_2O + H_2O \rightarrow 2R-COOH\] \[R-COOR' + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+ \text{ or } OH^-} R-COOH + R'OH\]

Acyl halides and anhydrides are easily hydrolyzed. Esters require acid or base catalyst (base-catalyzed is saponification, irreversible).

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2022

Question: Oxidation of toluene to benzoic acid using KMnO₄/KOH requires:

(a) Cold KMnO₄/acidic medium

(b) Hot KMnO₄/alkaline medium

(c) Cold KMnO₄/neutral medium

(d) Hot KMnO₄/acidic medium

Solution:

The side-chain oxidation of alkyl benzenes requires strong conditions:

- Hot KMnO₄ (heating required for complete oxidation)

- Alkaline (KOH) medium

The reaction proceeds as:

\[\text{C}_6H_5CH_3 \xrightarrow{KMnO_4/KOH, \Delta} \text{C}_6H_5COOK \xrightarrow{H^+} \text{C}_6H_5COOH\]

First, potassium benzoate (salt) is formed, which on acidification gives benzoic acid.

Cold/neutral KMnO₄ would give cis-dihydroxylation of the aromatic ring (not side-chain oxidation).

\[\text{Hot KMnO}_4/\text{alkaline medium (KOH)}\]

Answer: (b) Hot KMnO₄/alkaline medium

Chemical Reactions of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids undergo reactions involving: (a) the acidic O–H bond, (b) the C–OH bond, and (c) the α-hydrogen (HVZ reaction). Understanding these three categories is essential for JEE.

9.1 Reactions Involving O–H Bond Cleavage (Acidic Character)

Reactions with Metals, Bases, and Carbonates

With active metals (Na, K):

\[2R-COOH + 2Na \rightarrow 2R-COONa + H_2\uparrow\]

With NaOH/Na₂CO₃/NaHCO₃:

\[R-COOH + NaOH \rightarrow R-COONa + H_2O\]
\[R-COOH + NaHCO_3 \rightarrow R-COONa + H_2O + CO_2\uparrow\]

Key Point: The reaction with NaHCO₃ (evolution of CO₂ gas) is a test for carboxylic acids — phenols do NOT react with NaHCO₃ (weaker acids), so this distinguishes carboxylic acids from phenols.

9.2 Reactions Involving C–OH Bond Cleavage

⭐ Most Repeated PYQ — Esterification & Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

Esterification (Fischer Esterification):

\[R-COOH + R'OH \xrightarrow{H^+} R-COOR' + H_2O\]

Key Points:

  • Acid-catalyzed (usually conc. H₂SO₄)
  • Reversible reaction (ester hydrolysis is the reverse)
  • Mechanism: Nucleophilic acyl substitution (addition-elimination)
  • The –OH of acid is replaced by –OR' of alcohol (NOT the –OH of alcohol)

Conversion to Acid Chlorides, Anhydrides, Amides

With PCl₅, PCl₃, or SOCl₂:

\[R-COOH + PCl_5 \rightarrow R-COCl + POCl_3 + HCl\]
\[R-COOH + SOCl_2 \rightarrow R-COCl + SO_2 + HCl\]

SOCl₂ is preferred — gaseous by-products (SO₂, HCl) escape, giving pure acid chloride.

With NH₃ (forms amide):

\[R-COOH + NH_3 \rightarrow R-COONH_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-CONH_2 + H_2O\]

9.3 Reduction of Carboxylic Acids

\[R-COOH \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} R-CH_2OH \text{ (1° alcohol)}\] \[R-COOH \xrightarrow{B_2H_6/THF} R-CH_2OH\]

Key Points:

  • LiAlH₄ reduces carboxylic acids to 1° alcohols
  • NaBH₄ does NOT reduce carboxylic acids (too mild)
  • B₂H₆ (diborane) in THF also reduces COOH to CH₂OH — selective for COOH in presence of other groups
  • Catalytic reduction (H₂/Ni) does NOT reduce COOH easily

9.4 HVZ Reaction (α-Halogenation)

🔥 High PYQ Frequency — HVZ Reaction

Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction: Carboxylic acids with α-hydrogen atoms react with Cl₂ or Br₂ in the presence of red phosphorus to give α-halo carboxylic acids.

\[R-CH_2-COOH + Br_2 \xrightarrow{P \text{ (red)}} R-CHBr-COOH + HBr\]

Key Points:

  • Specifically halogenates the α-carbon (carbon adjacent to –COOH)
  • Requires red phosphorus as catalyst
  • Works with Cl₂ or Br₂ (not I₂ — too slow)
  • Only ONE α-H is replaced per HVZ step (but with excess X₂, all α-H can be replaced)
  • Acetic acid → mono-, di-, tri-chloroacetic acid
  • The reaction proceeds via acyl halide intermediate (RCOX), which enolizes more readily
  • Test for α-H: If a carboxylic acid gives HVZ reaction, it has α-H

9.5 Decarboxylation

\[R-COOH \xrightarrow{NaOH, CaO, \Delta} R-H + CO_2\]

Key Points:

  • Sodium salt of carboxylic acid + soda lime (NaOH + CaO) → alkane
  • CaO prevents NaOH from attacking glass
  • Kolbe's electrolysis: Electrolysis of sodium/potassium salt of carboxylic acid → alkane (at anode)
    • \[2R-COONa + 2H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{electrolysis}} R-R + 2CO_2 + 2NaOH + H_2\]

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2021

Question: The reaction of acetic acid with Cl₂ in presence of red phosphorus gives:

(a) Chloroacetic acid (ClCH₂COOH)

(b) Dichloroacetic acid (Cl₂CHCOOH)

(c) Trichloroacetic acid (Cl₃CCOOH)

(d) Acetyl chloride (CH₃COCl)

Solution:

This is the HVZ reaction:

\[CH_3COOH + Cl_2 \xrightarrow{P \text{ (red)}} ClCH_2COOH + HCl\]

The reaction specifically replaces ONE α-hydrogen with halogen (per HVZ step).

Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) has 3 α-H on the CH₃ group.

With limited Cl₂ (1 equivalent), only ONE H is replaced → monochloroacetic acid (ClCH₂COOH)

With excess Cl₂, all three α-H can be replaced → trichloroacetic acid (Cl₃CCOOH)

But the standard HVZ product (with stoichiometric Cl₂) is mono-chloroacetic acid.

\[\text{Product: ClCH}_2\text{COOH (monochloroacetic acid)}\]

Answer: (a) Chloroacetic acid (ClCH₂COOH)

Acidic Strength of Carboxylic Acids

Comparing the acidic strength of carboxylic acids is one of the most frequently tested topics in JEE. The key is understanding how inductive effect, resonance, and hybridization of substituents affect the stability of the carboxylate anion.

10.1 Factors Affecting Acidic Strength

🔥 Most Repeated PYQ — Acidic Strength Comparison

Rule: Any factor that stabilizes the carboxylate anion (RCOO⁻) increases acid strength, and any factor that destabilizes it decreases acid strength.

Acid-Strengthening Factors

  • Electron-withdrawing groups (–I effect)
  • Electronegative substituents
  • sp > sp² > sp³ (hybridization)
  • Closer to –COOH group
  • Greater number of EWG

Acid-Weakening Factors

  • Electron-donating groups (+I effect)
  • Alkyl groups
  • sp³ < sp² < sp
  • Farther from –COOH group
  • Resonance donation (+R)

10.2 Inductive Effect Order

Common Substituent Effects

–I groups (acid-strengthening):

\[NO_2 > CN > F > Cl > Br > I > CF_3 > OH > OCH_3 > C\equiv CH > Ph\]

+I groups (acid-weakeninging):

\[(CH_3)_3C- > (CH_3)_2CH- > CH_3CH_2- > CH_3-\]

10.4 Effect of Number and Distance of Substituents

Number of EWG: More EWG → stronger acid

\[Cl_3CCOOH > Cl_2CHCOOH > ClCH_2COOH > CH_3COOH\] \[(pKa: 0.65) \quad (1.29) \quad (2.86) \quad (4.76)\]

Distance from –COOH: Closer EWG → stronger acid (inductive effect decreases with distance)

\[ClCH_2COOH > ClCH_2CH_2COOH > ClCH_2CH_2CH_2COOH\] \[(\alpha) \quad\quad\quad\quad (\beta) \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad (\gamma)\]

10.5 Substituted Benzoic Acids

Effect of Substituents on Benzoic Acid

Acid-Strengthening (at any position)

  • –NO₂ (–I and –R)
  • –CN (–I and –R)
  • –X (halogens: –I > +R)
  • –CF₃ (strong –I)
  • –COOH, –CHO, –COR (–R)

Ortho effect: o-substituted benzoic acids are generally stronger than p- and m- isomers (due to steric inhibition of resonance)

Acid-Weakening (at ortho/para)

  • –OH, –OR (+R > –I)
  • –NH₂, –NHR, –NR₂ (+R > –I)
  • –CH₃, –C₂H₅ (+I and +H)
  • –O⁻ (strong +R)

At meta position, only inductive effect matters (resonance doesn't reach meta)

💡 JEE Shortcut — Halogens on Benzoic Acid

For halogen-substituted benzoic acids:

  • Halogens are –I (acid-strengthening) AND +R (acid-weakening)
  • At meta: Only –I operates → strongest effect
  • At para: –I + +R → net weaker than meta
  • At ortho: Ortho effect (steric) → strongest among the three
  • Order: o-chloro > m-chloro > p-chloro > benzoic acid

📝 PYQ — JEE Main 2024 (Apr 6 Shift 1)

Question: The correct decreasing order of acidic strength is:

(a) FCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH > BrCH₂COOH > CH₃COOH

(b) CH₃COOH > BrCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH > FCH₂COOH

(c) ClCH₂COOH > FCH₂COOH > BrCH₂COOH > CH₃COOH

(d) FCH₂COOH > BrCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH > CH₃COOH

Solution:

Key principle: Electron-withdrawing groups (–I) stabilize the carboxylate anion → increase acid strength.

Electronegativity order of halogens: F > Cl > Br > I

→ –I effect order: F > Cl > Br > I

→ Acid-strengthening order: FCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH > BrCH₂COOH > ICH₂COOH

CH₃COOH has an electron-donating group (+I) → weakest acid

\[FCH_2COOH > ClCH_2COOH > BrCH_2COOH > CH_3COOH\]

pKa values: FCH₂COOH (2.66) < ClCH₂COOH (2.86) < BrCH₂COOH (2.90) < CH₃COOH (4.76)

Answer: (a) FCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH > BrCH₂COOH > CH₃COOH

Frequently Asked Questions

What is nucleophilic addition reaction in aldehydes and ketones?

Nucleophilic addition reaction is the characteristic reaction of aldehydes and ketones where a nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon. The carbonyl carbon is partially positive due to the electronegativity of oxygen. The nucleophile adds to the carbonyl carbon, and a proton adds to the oxygen. Examples include addition of HCN, NaHSO3, Grignard reagents, and ammonia derivatives.

What is aldol condensation?

Aldol condensation is a reaction in which two molecules of an aldehyde or ketone containing at least one alpha-hydrogen undergo condensation in the presence of a dilute base (like NaOH) or dilute acid to form a beta-hydroxy aldehyde (aldol) or beta-hydroxy ketone (ketol). On heating, the aldol undergoes dehydration to give an alpha,beta-unsaturated compound. Example: 2CH3CHO → CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO (aldol) → CH3CH=CHCHO + H2O.

What is Cannizzaro reaction?

Cannizzaro reaction is a disproportionation reaction of aldehydes that do NOT have alpha-hydrogen atoms (like HCHO, C6H5CHO, (CH3)3C-CHO). When treated with concentrated alkali (NaOH/KOH), one molecule is reduced to alcohol and another is oxidized to carboxylic acid salt. Example: 2HCHO + NaOH → CH3OH + HCOONa.

Which compounds give iodoform test?

Compounds that give iodoform test are: (1) Aldehydes/ketones with CH3CO- group (like CH3CHO, CH3COCH3, CH3COR), (2) Alcohols that can be oxidized to CH3CO- group (like CH3CH2OH, CH3CH(OH)R), (3) Methyl ketones. The test involves treatment with NaOH and I2 (or NaOI), producing a yellow precipitate of CHI3 (iodoform).

What is the order of reactivity of aldehydes and ketones towards nucleophilic addition?

The order of reactivity is: HCHO > CH3CHO > CH3CH2CHO > CH3COCH3 > CH3COR > R2CO. This is because: (1) Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones (less steric hindrance, more electrophilic carbon), (2) Electron-donating alkyl groups decrease electrophilicity of carbonyl carbon, (3) Steric hindrance increases from aldehydes to ketones.

What is HVZ reaction?

Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction is the reaction of a carboxylic acid with chlorine or bromine in the presence of red phosphorus to give alpha-halo carboxylic acids. The reaction selectively halogenates the alpha-carbon. Example: CH3COOH + Br2/P → CH2BrCOOH (alpha-bromoacetic acid). The reaction proceeds via the acyl halide intermediate.

What is the difference between Clemmensen and Wolff-Kishner reduction?

Both reduce carbonyl group (C=O) to CH2. Clemmensen reduction uses Zn-Hg/HCl (acidic medium), while Wolff-Kishner reduction uses NH2NH2/KOH/ethylene glycol (basic medium). Clemmensen is not suitable for acid-sensitive compounds, while Wolff-Kishner is not suitable for base-sensitive compounds. Both convert RCHO → RCH3 and RCOR' → RCH2R'.

Why is benzoic acid stronger acid than acetic acid?

Benzoic acid (C6H5COOH, pKa = 4.2) is stronger than acetic acid (CH3COOH, pKa = 4.76) because the phenyl group (C6H5-) is sp2 hybridized and exerts a -I (electron-withdrawing inductive) effect, which stabilizes the carboxylate anion more than the sp3-hybridized methyl group. However, phenyl also shows +R (resonance) effect which slightly reduces its acid-strengthening ability, making benzoic acid weaker than formic acid.

How many questions come from aldehydes ketones carboxylic acids in JEE Mains?

On average 2-3 questions (8-12 marks) appear from this chapter in every JEE Main session. It is one of the highest weightage chapters in organic chemistry. Most questions are from aldol condensation, Cannizzaro reaction, iodoform test, acidic strength comparison, and identification of compounds using chemical tests.

What is Rosenmund reduction?

Rosenmund reduction is the reduction of an acyl chloride (acid chloride) to an aldehyde using hydrogen gas in the presence of palladium catalyst supported on barium sulfate (Pd/BaSO4). The BaSO4 acts as a poison to prevent over-reduction to alcohol. Example: CH3COCl + H2/Pd-BaSO4 → CH3CHO + HCl.

Aldehydes, Ketones & Carboxylic Acids — Complete JEE Study Guide

Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids is one of the most important chapters in JEE Organic Chemistry, covering the structure, preparation, properties, and reactions of carbonyl compounds and carboxylic acids. The chapter includes 15+ named reactions, making it the highest name-reaction density chapter in the syllabus.

The most important topics for JEE from this chapter are aldol condensation (requires α-hydrogen, uses dilute base), Cannizzaro reaction (requires NO α-hydrogen, uses concentrated base), iodoform/haloform test (compounds with CH₃CO– or CH₃CH(OH)– group), nucleophilic addition reactivity order (HCHO > CH₃CHO > CH₃COCH₃), acidic strength comparison (based on inductive, resonance, and hybridization effects), and identification tests (Tollens' test for all aldehydes, Fehling's test for aliphatic aldehydes only).

Students should focus on mastering the distinction between aldol and Cannizzaro reactions (based on presence/absence of α-H), the Clemmensen vs Wolff-Kishner reduction (acidic vs basic medium), and the Rosenmund reduction (acid chloride to aldehyde using Pd/BaSO₄). The HVZ reaction (α-halogenation of carboxylic acids with X₂/red phosphorus) and the side-chain oxidation of alkyl benzenes to benzoic acids (using hot KMnO₄/KOH) are also frequently tested.

For JEE Advanced, additional focus should be on crossed aldol condensation (when one reactant has no α-H), Baeyer-Villiger oxidation (ketone to ester using peracids), Kolbe-Schmitt reaction (phenol to salicylic acid), Reimer-Tiemann reaction (phenol to salicylaldehyde), and multi-step synthesis problems combining multiple reactions. The acidic strength of substituted benzoic acids requires understanding of ortho effect, inductive effect at meta position, and resonance effects at ortho/para positions.

The key formulas to memorize include: equivalent weight concepts (rarely tested here but useful for titration problems), the order of acidic strength (FCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH > HCOOH > C₆H₅COOH > CH₃COOH), the reactivity order for nucleophilic addition (HCHO > RCHO > RCOR'), and the distinction between various reducing agents (NaBH₄ reduces only aldehydes/ketones, LiAlH₄ reduces all carbonyls including acids and esters, Clemmensen/Wolff-Kishner reduce C=O to CH₂).
