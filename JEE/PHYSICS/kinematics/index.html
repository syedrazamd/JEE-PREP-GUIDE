Master Kinematics for JEE Main & Advanced

Your complete guide to motion in 1D/2D, projectile motion, relative velocity, circular motion & graphs.

1. Introduction to Kinematics

Kinematics is the branch of classical mechanics that describes the motion of points, objects, and groups of objects, without reference to the causes of motion (i.e., forces). It's all about how things move: how fast, how far, and in what direction. Mastering Kinematics is fundamental for JEE as it forms the base for understanding dynamics and other advanced topics in mechanics.

1.1 Basic Definitions

Before delving into equations and problems, it's crucial to understand basic terms:

  • Position: The location of an object at a particular time, usually relative to a reference point (origin). It's a vector quantity.
  • Distance: The total path length covered by an object. It's a scalar quantity and always non-negative.
  • Displacement: The change in position of an object. It's a vector quantity, defined as the shortest distance from the initial to the final position.
    \[ \vec{\Delta r} = \vec{r}_{final} - \vec{r}_{initial} \]
  • Speed: The rate at which an object covers distance. It's a scalar quantity.
    Average Speed \( = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} \)
  • Velocity: The rate of change of an object's position. It's a vector quantity.
    Average Velocity \( \vec{v}_{avg} = \frac{\vec{\Delta r}}{\Delta t} \)
    Instantaneous Velocity \( \vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} \)
  • Acceleration: The rate of change of an object's velocity. It's a vector quantity.
    Average Acceleration \( \vec{a}_{avg} = \frac{\vec{\Delta v}}{\Delta t} \)
    Instantaneous Acceleration \( \vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \frac{d^2\vec{r}}{dt^2} \)

Example 1.1: Distance vs. Displacement

A particle moves from point A(0,0) to B(3,4) along a straight line, then moves from B to C(3,0) along a straight line. Find the total distance covered and the magnitude of its displacement.

Solution:

Distance covered (A to B): \( d_{AB} = \sqrt{(3-0)^2 + (4-0)^2} = \sqrt{9+16} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \) units.
Distance covered (B to C): \( d_{BC} = \sqrt{(3-3)^2 + (0-4)^2} = \sqrt{0+16} = 4 \) units.
Total Distance: \( d_{total} = d_{AB} + d_{BC} = 5 + 4 = 9 \) units.

Initial Position: \( \vec{r}_i = (0\hat{i} + 0\hat{j}) \)
Final Position: \( \vec{r}_f = (3\hat{i} + 0\hat{j}) \)
Displacement: \( \vec{\Delta r} = \vec{r}_f - \vec{r}_i = (3\hat{i} + 0\hat{j}) - (0\hat{i} + 0\hat{j}) = (3\hat{i} + 0\hat{j}) \) units.
Magnitude of Displacement: \( |\vec{\Delta r}| = \sqrt{3^2 + 0^2} = 3 \) units.

Note: Distance is 9 units, while displacement magnitude is 3 units, highlighting their difference.

2. Motion in One Dimension

Motion in one dimension (1D) or rectilinear motion occurs when an object moves along a straight line. This is the simplest type of motion to analyze and forms the basis for more complex 2D and 3D motion.

2.1 Equations of Motion for Constant Acceleration

For an object moving with constant acceleration \(a\), initial velocity \(u\), and final velocity \(v\) after time \(t\), covering displacement \(s\):

Key Equations of Motion

  • \( v = u + at \)
  • \( s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \)
  • \( v^2 = u^2 + 2as \)
  • \( s_n = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1) \) (Displacement in \(n^{th}\) second)

2.2 Motion under Gravity (Free Fall)

When an object falls freely under gravity, its acceleration is approximately constant and equal to \(g\) (acceleration due to gravity, approx. \(9.8 \, m/s^2\)). We typically take the upward direction as positive and downward as negative, or vice-versa, but consistency is key.

Equations for Free Fall

(Taking upward as +ve, origin at ground, acceleration \(a = -g\))

  • \( v = u - gt \)
  • \( h = ut - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \)
  • \( v^2 = u^2 - 2gh \)

Example 2.1: Particle Motion

A particle starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at \(2 \, m/s^2\) for \(5\) seconds. It then moves with constant velocity for \(3\) seconds and finally decelerates uniformly at \(4 \, m/s^2\) until it comes to rest. Find the total distance covered.

Solution:

  1. Phase 1 (Acceleration): \( u=0, a=2 \, m/s^2, t=5 \, s \)
    \( v_1 = u + at = 0 + (2)(5) = 10 \, m/s \)
    \( s_1 = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2}(2)(5^2) = 25 \, m \)
  2. Phase 2 (Constant Velocity): \( v = 10 \, m/s, t=3 \, s \)
    \( s_2 = v \times t = 10 \times 3 = 30 \, m \)
  3. Phase 3 (Deceleration): \( u = 10 \, m/s, v = 0, a = -4 \, m/s^2 \)
    \( v^2 = u^2 + 2as_3 \Rightarrow 0^2 = 10^2 + 2(-4)s_3 \)
    \( 0 = 100 - 8s_3 \Rightarrow 8s_3 = 100 \Rightarrow s_3 = 12.5 \, m \)

Total distance covered: \( S_{total} = s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 25 + 30 + 12.5 = 67.5 \, m \).

3. Motion in Two Dimensions (Projectile Motion)

Motion in two dimensions (2D) involves movement in a plane. The most common example is projectile motion, where an object is thrown into the air and moves under the influence of gravity alone (neglecting air resistance).

3.1 Projectile Motion from Ground Level

Consider a projectile launched with initial velocity \(u\) at an angle \( \theta \) with the horizontal.

Formulas for Projectile Motion

  • Time of Flight (T): Time for the projectile to return to its initial height.
    \( T = \frac{2u \sin\theta}{g} \)
  • Maximum Height (H): Highest vertical position reached.
    \( H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g} \)
  • Horizontal Range (R): Total horizontal distance covered.
    \( R = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g} \)
  • Equation of Trajectory:
    \( y = x \tan\theta - \frac{g x^2}{2u^2 \cos^2\theta} \)

Example 3.1: Projectile Calculation

A projectile is fired with an initial velocity of \(20 \, m/s\) at an angle of \(30^\circ\) above the horizontal. Calculate its time of flight, maximum height, and horizontal range. (Take \(g = 10 \, m/s^2\)).

Solution:

Given: \( u = 20 \, m/s \), \( \theta = 30^\circ \), \( g = 10 \, m/s^2 \).

  1. Time of Flight (T):
    \( T = \frac{2u \sin\theta}{g} = \frac{2 \times 20 \times \sin(30^\circ)}{10} = \frac{40 \times 0.5}{10} = \frac{20}{10} = 2 \, s \)
  2. Maximum Height (H):
    \( H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g} = \frac{(20)^2 \times (\sin(30^\circ))^2}{2 \times 10} = \frac{400 \times (0.25)}{20} = \frac{100}{20} = 5 \, m \)
  3. Horizontal Range (R):
    \( R = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g} = \frac{(20)^2 \times \sin(60^\circ)}{10} = \frac{400 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{10} = \frac{200\sqrt{3}}{10} = 20\sqrt{3} \, m \)

4. Relative Motion

Relative motion deals with how the motion of an object is observed from a different moving reference frame. The core concept is that velocities (and accelerations) are frame-dependent.

4.1 Relative Velocity

If an object A moves with velocity \( \vec{v}_A \) and object B moves with velocity \( \vec{v}_B \), then the velocity of A relative to B, denoted \( \vec{v}_{AB} \), is given by:

Relative Velocity Formula

\( \vec{v}_{AB} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B \)

Similarly, the position of A relative to B is \( \vec{r}_{AB} = \vec{r}_A - \vec{r}_B \) and relative acceleration is \( \vec{a}_{AB} = \vec{a}_A - \vec{a}_B \).

4.2 Applications: Rain-Man Problems & River-Boat Problems

  • Rain-Man Problems: Involve finding the velocity of rain relative to a moving person. Often requires vector addition/subtraction.
  • River-Boat Problems: Involve a boat moving in a river with a current. Key scenarios include crossing the river in minimum time or minimum distance.
    • Minimum time to cross: Boat travels perpendicular to the current.
      \( t_{min} = \frac{\text{width}}{v_{boat, river}} \)
    • Minimum distance to cross (straight across): Boat heads upstream at an angle such that resultant velocity is perpendicular to current.
      \( v_{boat, ground} = \sqrt{v_{boat, river}^2 - v_{river, ground}^2} \)

Example 4.1: Rain-Man Problem

Rain is falling vertically downwards with a speed of \(30 \, m/s\). A man is walking on the road with a speed of \(10 \, m/s\). In which direction should the man hold his umbrella?

Solution:

Let \( \vec{v}_R \) be the velocity of rain and \( \vec{v}_M \) be the velocity of the man.

\( \vec{v}_R = -30\hat{j} \, m/s \) (taking downward as -y direction)
\( \vec{v}_M = 10\hat{i} \, m/s \) (taking forward as +x direction)

We need to find the velocity of rain relative to the man, \( \vec{v}_{RM} \):

\( \vec{v}_{RM} = \vec{v}_R - \vec{v}_M = (-30\hat{j}) - (10\hat{i}) = -10\hat{i} - 30\hat{j} \, m/s \)

The magnitude of this relative velocity is \( |\vec{v}_{RM}| = \sqrt{(-10)^2 + (-30)^2} = \sqrt{100 + 900} = \sqrt{1000} = 10\sqrt{10} \, m/s \).

The angle \( \alpha \) with the vertical (y-axis) that the man needs to hold his umbrella can be found using the components:

\( \tan\alpha = \frac{\text{horizontal component}}{\text{vertical component}} = \frac{|-10|}{|-30|} = \frac{10}{30} = \frac{1}{3} \)
\( \alpha = \arctan\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) \)

The man should hold his umbrella at an angle of \( \arctan(1/3) \) with the vertical, tilted towards his direction of motion (i.e., forward).

5. Circular Motion

Circular motion describes the movement of an object along the circumference of a circle or a circular path. It can be uniform (constant speed) or non-uniform (varying speed).

5.1 Angular Variables

For circular motion, we use angular quantities:

  • Angular Displacement (\(\Delta\theta\)): Angle swept by the radius vector. Unit: radian (rad).
  • Angular Velocity (\(\omega\)): Rate of change of angular displacement.
    \( \omega = \frac{d\theta}{dt} \) (Unit: rad/s)
  • Angular Acceleration (\(\alpha\)): Rate of change of angular velocity.
    \( \alpha = \frac{d\omega}{dt} = \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} \) (Unit: rad/s\(^2\))

5.2 Uniform Circular Motion (UCM)

In UCM, the speed of the object is constant, but its velocity changes continuously due to a change in direction. This change in velocity means there is an acceleration.

Formulas for UCM

  • Relationship between linear and angular speed:
    \( v = r\omega \)
  • Centripetal Acceleration (\(a_c\)): Always directed towards the center of the circle.
    \( a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = r\omega^2 \)
  • Time Period (T): Time for one complete revolution.
    \( T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} \)
  • Frequency (f): Number of revolutions per unit time.
    \( f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} \)

5.3 Non-Uniform Circular Motion

In non-uniform circular motion, both the speed and direction of the object change. The acceleration has two components:

  • Radial (Centripetal) Acceleration (\(a_r = a_c\)): Changes the direction of velocity. \( a_r = \frac{v^2}{r} = r\omega^2 \).
  • Tangential Acceleration (\(a_t\)): Changes the magnitude of velocity (speed). \( a_t = \frac{dv}{dt} = r\alpha \).

The net acceleration \( \vec{a} \) is the vector sum of these two components:

\( \vec{a} = \vec{a}_r + \vec{a}_t \Rightarrow |\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_r^2 + a_t^2} \)

Example 5.1: Circular Motion

A particle is moving in a circle of radius \(5 \, m\) with a constant speed of \(10 \, m/s\). Find its angular speed and centripetal acceleration.

Solution:

Given: \( r = 5 \, m \), \( v = 10 \, m/s \).

  1. Angular Speed (\(\omega\)):
    \( v = r\omega \Rightarrow \omega = \frac{v}{r} = \frac{10}{5} = 2 \, rad/s \)
  2. Centripetal Acceleration (\(a_c\)):
    \( a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{(10)^2}{5} = \frac{100}{5} = 20 \, m/s^2 \)
    Alternatively, using \( \omega \):
    \( a_c = r\omega^2 = 5 \times (2)^2 = 5 \times 4 = 20 \, m/s^2 \)

6. Graphs in Kinematics

Graphs provide a powerful way to visualize and analyze motion. Understanding the relationship between position-time (x-t), velocity-time (v-t), and acceleration-time (a-t) graphs is essential.

6.1 Position-Time (x-t) Graph

  • Slope: Gives instantaneous velocity (\( v = \frac{dx}{dt} \)).
  • Straight line: Constant velocity.
  • Curved line: Varying velocity (acceleration present).
  • Horizontal line: Object at rest.

6.2 Velocity-Time (v-t) Graph

  • Slope: Gives instantaneous acceleration (\( a = \frac{dv}{dt} \)).
  • Area under the curve: Gives displacement (\( \Delta x = \int v \, dt \)).
  • Straight line (non-zero slope): Constant acceleration.
  • Horizontal line: Constant velocity (zero acceleration).

6.3 Acceleration-Time (a-t) Graph

  • Area under the curve: Gives change in velocity (\( \Delta v = \int a \, dt \)).
  • Slope: Gives jerk (\( J = \frac{da}{dt} \)).
  • Horizontal line: Constant acceleration.

Position-Time (x-t) Velocity (\(v\)) N/A
Velocity-Time (v-t) Acceleration (\(a\)) Displacement (\(\Delta x\))
Acceleration-Time (a-t) Jerk (\(J\)) Change in Velocity (\(\Delta v\))

Example 6.1: V-T Graph Analysis

A particle starts from rest and its velocity-time graph is a straight line passing through the origin, reaching a velocity of \(20 \, m/s\) in \(4\) seconds. Calculate the acceleration and displacement of the particle in these \(4\) seconds.

Solution:

From the v-t graph, a straight line passing through the origin means constant acceleration. Initial velocity \(u=0\), final velocity \(v=20 \, m/s\), time \(t=4 \, s\).

  1. Acceleration: The slope of the v-t graph.
    \( a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{20 - 0}{4 - 0} = \frac{20}{4} = 5 \, m/s^2 \)
  2. Displacement: The area under the v-t graph. For a straight line from origin, it's a triangle.
    \( \text{Displacement} = \text{Area of triangle} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \, s \times 20 \, m/s = 40 \, m \)

Previous Year Questions Analysis (JEE Main & Advanced)

Kinematics is a consistently important topic for both JEE Main and Advanced. Questions often integrate concepts from different sub-topics.

Weightage & Trends:

  • JEE Main: Typically 1-2 questions from Kinematics. Often direct application of formulas or graph interpretation.
    • Commonly asked from: Projectile motion, relative velocity (rain-man, river-boat), equations of motion, and basic graphs.
    • Recent trend includes more conceptual questions involving graphs and multi-stage motion.
  • JEE Advanced: Usually 1-2 questions, which can be more complex, multi-concept problems. May involve calculus.
    • Emphasis on relative motion in 2D, non-uniform circular motion, and advanced projectile motion scenarios (e.g., projectiles on inclined planes, collision of projectiles).
    • Integration of kinematics with calculus (e.g., finding velocity/position from acceleration given as a function of time or position).

Important Subtopics & Repeated Question Types:

  • Projectile Motion: Range, height, time of flight, velocity at any point, equation of trajectory, and motion on an inclined plane. Questions often involve finding specific launch angles or velocities under certain conditions.
  • Relative Velocity: Rain-man problems (angle of umbrella), river-boat problems (minimum time/distance to cross), and relative motion between two moving objects. Vector approach is critical.
  • Graphs: Interpretation of x-t, v-t, and a-t graphs. Deriving one graph from another, finding acceleration from v-t slope, displacement from v-t area.
  • Motion with Variable Acceleration: Problems where acceleration is a function of time, position, or velocity, requiring integration for solution.
  • Circular Motion: Centripetal acceleration, tangential acceleration, angular velocity/acceleration, total acceleration in non-uniform circular motion.

Strategy for JEE: Focus on understanding the vector nature of displacement, velocity, and acceleration. Practice a diverse range of problems, especially those involving relative motion and graphs. For Advanced, be comfortable with calculus applications.

Practice Problem Set

Test your understanding with these practice problems. Solutions are provided for self-assessment.

Level 1: Basic Application

Problem 1.1:

A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at \(4 \, m/s^2\). What is its velocity after \(6\) seconds and how much distance does it cover in this time?

Show Solution

Given: \( u=0, a=4 \, m/s^2, t=6 \, s \).

Velocity: \( v = u + at = 0 + (4)(6) = 24 \, m/s \).

Distance: \( s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2}(4)(6^2) = \frac{1}{2}(4)(36) = 72 \, m \).

Problem 1.2:

A ball is thrown vertically upwards with an initial velocity of \(25 \, m/s\). How high does it go? (Take \(g = 10 \, m/s^2\)).

Show Solution

Given: \( u=25 \, m/s, v=0 \) (at max height), \( a=-g=-10 \, m/s^2 \).

Using \( v^2 = u^2 + 2as \): \( 0^2 = 25^2 + 2(-10)h \).

\( 0 = 625 - 20h \Rightarrow 20h = 625 \Rightarrow h = 31.25 \, m \).

Level 2: Intermediate Challenges

Problem 2.1:

A projectile is launched from the ground at an angle of \(45^\circ\) with the horizontal. If its horizontal range is \(100 \, m\), find the initial velocity of the projectile. (Take \(g = 10 \, m/s^2\)).

Show Solution

Given: \( R = 100 \, m, \theta = 45^\circ, g = 10 \, m/s^2 \).

Range formula: \( R = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g} \).

\( 100 = \frac{u^2 \sin(2 \times 45^\circ)}{10} = \frac{u^2 \sin(90^\circ)}{10} = \frac{u^2 \times 1}{10} \).

\( u^2 = 1000 \Rightarrow u = \sqrt{1000} = 10\sqrt{10} \, m/s \).

Problem 2.2:

A boat can travel at \(5 \, m/s\) in still water. It wants to cross a river of width \(60 \, m\) flowing at \(3 \, m/s\). In what direction should it head to reach the point directly opposite on the other bank? How much time will it take?

Show Solution

To cross directly opposite, the boat must head upstream at an angle \( \theta \) such that its component of velocity against the current cancels the river's velocity.

\( v_{boat,river} \sin\theta = v_{river} \).

\( 5 \sin\theta = 3 \Rightarrow \sin\theta = \frac{3}{5} \Rightarrow \theta = 36.87^\circ \) (upstream from perpendicular to the bank).

Velocity component perpendicular to the bank: \( v_y = v_{boat,river} \cos\theta = 5 \times \sqrt{1 - (3/5)^2} = 5 \times \sqrt{1 - 9/25} = 5 \times \sqrt{16/25} = 5 \times \frac{4}{5} = 4 \, m/s \).

Time taken: \( t = \frac{\text{width}}{v_y} = \frac{60}{4} = 15 \, s \).

Level 3: Advanced Concepts

Problem 3.1:

A particle moves along a straight line such that its velocity is given by \( v = (3t^2 - 2t + 1) \, m/s \). If its initial position at \( t=0 \) is \( x=2 \, m \), find its position at \( t=2 \, s \).

Show Solution

Given \( v = \frac{dx}{dt} = 3t^2 - 2t + 1 \).

Integrate velocity to find position: \( x = \int v \, dt = \int (3t^2 - 2t + 1) \, dt = t^3 - t^2 + t + C \).

Using initial condition: At \( t=0, x=2 \).

\( 2 = 0^3 - 0^2 + 0 + C \Rightarrow C = 2 \).

So, \( x(t) = t^3 - t^2 + t + 2 \).

At \( t=2 \, s \): \( x(2) = 2^3 - 2^2 + 2 + 2 = 8 - 4 + 2 + 2 = 8 \, m \).
